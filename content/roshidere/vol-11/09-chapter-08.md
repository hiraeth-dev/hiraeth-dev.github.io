“…Nice.”

Standing in the hallway just before the semester’s closing ceremony, Masachika quietly pumped his fist. The student rankings covered the wall ahead of him, and his eyes had fallen to one specific line: “17th - Masachika Kuze.”

Finishing within the top thirty of students in his year had been a goal he had set back in the first semester. It also happened to be one he had stopped wagering over with his partner Alisa. Even so, getting his name onto such a list had become a standard he felt he needed to achieve.

“Time to go.”

Using his newfound sense of accomplishment that burned deep in his chest as fuel for motivation for the next goal ahead, Masachika stepped onto the battlefield.

⋆⋅☆⋅⋆

“Now then. Moving forward, we would like to invite the student council to give their closing speeches.”

At the emcee’s cue, Touya, the student council president, stepped onto the stage. Remaining on the wings, Masachika and Alisa watched him go.

Just as in the first semester, the order of speeches to be given by the first-year student council members had been decided the day before with a coin toss overseen by Maria. With Alisa having won the right to choose this time around, she had deliberately opted for them to go second.

The reason was simple. With Yuki most likely weaving the Christmas party into her speech, it wasn’t hard to imagine Alisa’s and Masachika’s own speeches being driven out of everyone’s minds.

<em>“Yeah, I kinda felt like they’d talked ’bout somethin’ pretty amazing, but what happened afterward left such a huge impression that sorta faded into the background, huuuh~?”</em>

That was exactly the sort of reaction they could imagine as a result: one they wanted to avoid.

What was more, by going second, Masachika and Alisa hoped to leave the audience in suspense: hooked on the anticipation that they might once again clear the high bar, just as they had during the first semester’s closing ceremony.

<em>In fact, Alya’s probably gonna be stealin’ all the attention regardless with this campaign promise of hers… Looks like I’ll really be just her background support this time, huh?</em>

There was simply no need for Masachika to give the type of eccentric speech that he had given back at the first semester’s closing ceremony. All he needed to do today was reinforce the feasibility of Alisa’s proposal from the position of someone supporting her.

<em>Huh…?</em> Masachika stopped himself as a new thought crossed his mind.<em>Now that I think of it, ain’t this basically the same as when I was Yuki’s partner back in junior high?</em>

The realization propped a faint smile across his lips.

<em>In other words, this is what I’m best at, huh?</em>

And at the same time, it also meant that his current partner, Alisa, had become just as dependable a presence as his former one, Yuki…

“……”

Masachika glanced at that very partner beside him as she watched Chisaki give her speech.

Her profile was seemingly still a touch rigid, carrying a trace of tension... But even so, compared to the first semester, when merely just standing on the stage had made her look as though she was on the verge of collapse, Alisa now stood firmly on her feet with a demeanor beyond any comparison.

“Alrighty, Alya-chan,” Maria called out to her. “I’m off, okayyy~?”

“Yes, yes… Have a good one.”

She now even had enough composure to answer her sister with nothing more than a wry smile.

<em>You’ve really become dependable, y’know…?</em> Masachika conveyed to her in his heart. <em>You really have.</em>

Feeling a concoction of happiness, admiration, and just a smidge of loneliness wash over him, he shot a small wry smile at himself.

<em>What am I, her dad or something?</em>

Perhaps misunderstanding what he was smirking about, Alisa glanced up his way.

Shrugging, she gestured toward Maria with her eyes. “She really takes the edge off, am I right?”

“Haha. She does, huh?” Masachika nodded, deliberately leaving her misunderstanding uncorrected as he lowered his voice. “But it’s also admirable that <em>you’ve</em> got enough room to let your guard down, you know?”

Feeling her eyes widen slightly at his words, Alisa lowered her gaze as though reflecting on herself.

Letting a few seconds pass between the two of them, she smiled faintly and nodded.

“…Maybe you’re right,” Alisa whispered.

Shifting a step closer, she then quietly leaned against him in a soft murmur.

【Only because you were there for me.】

<em>Ngh… Too much composure’s a problem too, y’know?</em>

Her unexpected emotional attack nearly threw Masachika off balance, forcing him to rely on his sheer force of will to just maintain his focus.

“Up next, the student council’s officer for public relations, Yuki Suou-san, will now give her address.”

With the emcee’s resonating announcement signifying the conclusion of Maria’s speech, Yuki stepped up onto the stage. Naturally, the appearance of a candidate for the next student council president sparked a different kind of excitement to ripple throughout the gymnasium.

Waving with a smile in response to all the cheers, Yuki glanced toward her brother, who stood watching from the opposite wing of the stage and… narrowed the gaze in her heart.

<em>Ohhh?</em> she hummed to herself.<em>You sure look awfully relaxed, don’tcha?</em>

Just a mere glance at his face was enough for her to more or less deduce the state of mind he was in. They were siblings who shared the same blood, after all. As she presently saw it, Masachika seemed to have no concerns beyond simply standing on stage; if anything, he gave off the faint impression of someone who had already finished the job.

In other words…

<em>He’s underestimating me.</em>

The need to focus on his supporting role. The belief that Alisa was more than capable of putting up a real fight alone. That was what all Masachika was thinking.

<em>Oh well, I s’pose they’ve prepped somethin’ worthy of warranting that confidence then, hm…?</em>

Conversely, Yuki certainly didn’t underestimate Alisa.

All the prep work for the Christmas party—her hidden trump card that would for sure reinforce her campaign promise and seize today’s spotlight—which she had carried out in secret, even from her brother, had come as a result of her renewing her resolve to seriously defeat Masachika. It was the embodiment of her careful and meticulous preparation of every measure necessary to achieve that goal.

And yet, even with all these hidden measures in place… if there was anyone who could deliver a speech worthy of earning an applause that rivaled theirs, it would be Alisa.

That was simply how highly Yuki regarded her.

But even so… her brother’s carefree optimism—assuming that they could willingly turn things around after giving away the opportunity of going first—irritated her.

<em>In other words… he’s assuming that I—that we—won’t be able to completely take control of the atmosphere here, hmmm?</em>

With a vicious fighting spirit flaring right up in her chest, Yuki finally stepped onto the stage.

The words she had declared in her room before resurfaced in her mind.

<em>“I’ll win… and make them realize… that I’m no longer weak or pitiful.”</em>

With a tide of resolve and determination blooming within her, Yuki repeated that vow once more.

<em>I’ll be the one to put you outta your misery, okay, Onii-chan?</em>

And so, concealing the flames that roared behind her refined ladylike smile, Yuki began her speech with dignified confidence.

⋆⋅☆⋅⋆

“As I was just introduced, I am Yuki Suou, the student council’s officer for public relations. So, everyone, how was this second semester for you? I believe that all of you have each gone through a vast array of unique and different experiences. I am aware that this is sudden, but given that this is the closing ceremony, how about we take a moment to look back on the semester, shall we? Everyone, for a little while, please close your eyes and reflect on your memories of these past few months.”

No sooner had Yuki started her address than she fell silent, unleashing a wave of puzzlement to wash over the student body. However, as she continued to keep her silence with nothing but a small smile on her face, everyone present gradually began to retrace their memories, some closing their eyes while others lowered their gazes.

“Everyone, have you all had enough time to reflect?” Yuki spoke again after quietly observing the seated students for a short while. “So, how was it? I imagine that each and every one of you has made many different memories from this semester, but… would it not be fair to say that what came to mind for most were events that have brought about major changes to your daily lives, perhaps?”

Sweeping her eyes over the crowd, she took in the expressions gathered before her.

“For instance, large events such as the cultural festival… or even the sports festival. Or maybe, something personally significant: getting a boyfriend or a girlfriend, or achieving a major milestone within your club activities. Perhaps these were the types of occurrences you thought of: they’re moments when you or those around you underwent major changes, wouldn’t you say? Surely none of you had pictured an ordinary day of classes where nothing special had happened, right?”

Pausing to take a small breath, Yuki adopted a serious expression, abandoning her slightly playful tone.

“As for myself, I believe that what will always remain in the hearts of everyone is change,” she said, her tone resolute. “Changes in everyday life, changes in those around us, and changes within ourselves. For uneventful and flat routines do not leave impressions within the heart. This… is something that I believe all of you have just realized for yourselves.”

Her words elicited a chorus of nods throughout the audience, clearly in agreement. Confirming that her message had taken root among the students, she continued.

“Change: that’s what’s necessary to etch the days you spend at this school more deeply into your hearts for the rest of your lives. Change—<em>significant</em> change—is what I will bring about to this school if elected student council president, and it will begin with listening to all of your voices. Not sweeping reforms or radical transformations, but changes that will add color and a little enjoyment to all your daily lives. As a first step toward this ideal, I will be holding a brand-new event today at 4:30 p.m. Something that this school has never had before: a Christmas party.”

By this point, the audience was completely captivated by her speech.

“I am sure that some of you are eager to head home to enjoy the winter break to its fullest, while others have already made plans to go out and have fun with friends,” Yuki smiled, continuing to address the students. “But please consider this. Today’s the only day you can attend a Christmas party at this school, you know? The time you’ll spend relaxing at home, the time you’ll spend hanging out with all your friends: both will be enjoyable, sure, but which do you think you will still remember a year from now?”

The answer to such a question had naturally already sprouted within the hearts of those who were listening. Looking out at the wave of students, who were clearly deeply moved, Yuki delivered her final push.

“So, everyone, I am sincerely asking you to join the Christmas party that I am hosting. Because if you do, I am sure that all my beliefs—my ideals—will be conveyed to you all as well.”

Her words conveyed nothing but an appeal of utter sincerity, one finished with a radiant smile as she continued to meet the eyes within the audience.

“On that note and with this announcement, I would now like to bring my address to a close,” Yuki proclaimed in a bright voice. “Thank you all very much for your attention.”

With those final words, her speech came to its end.

She lowered her head into a bow. Silence lingered for a heartbeat, before thunderous applause erupted throughout the crowd. Accompanying whistles and cheers calling in support for her quickly enveloped the gymnasium, rising in a wave indistinguishable from a standing ovation.

⋆⋅☆⋅⋆

<em>Are you for real?</em> Masachika couldn’t help but mutter in his heart. <em>She’s reeeally good at speeches.</em>

No, he had always known that Yuki was a good public speaker. He knew that, but….

<em>What</em> is<em>this…? A ridiculous power up or something? This girl…</em>

A growing sense of unease slowly crept throughout his chest; the excitement and sense of unity that had taken over the gymnasium far exceeded his expectations. It felt so overwhelming that his own intuition was screaming to tell him that even Alisa’s words would never reach the hearts of the audience if she were to give her speech now.

<em>No, thank God Ayano’s still gotta go… Alya’s voice should surely reach them if the mood settles down a li’l there. Probably…</em>

Though Masachika was fully aware that his thoughts had spiraled into wishful thinking—far from calm and calculating—he had yet to descend into a full-blown panic.

…At least, not yet.

⋆⋅☆⋅⋆

“Thank you very much, Suou-san,” the emcee’s voice once again echoed through the gymnasium. “Next, we will hear some remarks from the student council’s secretary, Ayano Kimishima.”

Hearing her cue, Ayano turned to Yuki before stepping onto the stage. “I will be taking my turn.”

“Mm,” Yuki nodded to her quiet voice. “I’ll leave the rest to you.”

Her words were so simple, yet they carried her complete trust. Ayano could only stop in her tracks as she heard them, continuing her steps after a moment.

As for her closing ceremony speech, Yuki had never specifically instructed her to say this or talk about so and so. It had been the same during the first semester as well. Even when Ayano had asked about what she should talk about, all Yuki had done was tell her to think of it herself. It was surely a sign of the trust she had in Ayano, and at the same time… a testament to Yuki’s belief that parroting the words of someone else meant you could never truly move the hearts of those who were listening.

<em>I have come to understand that now,</em> Ayano reflected.

It was something she had not grasped back during the first semester, and was why all she had done was simply list Yuki’s virtues and strengths as she thought, appealing to the audience for their support. It wasn’t something she thought of as a mistake, per se… but maybe it wasn’t what was needed to move the hearts of others.

Ayano had come to terms with this fact most strongly during the student assembly last month when she had heard the pleas of the vice president of the piano club, Aoi Tsukamoto.

Listening to her appeal from the wings of the stage had allowed her to understand firsthand: rather than words meticulously woven together in one’s mind… a voice that draws from the heart in its most natural and unaltered shape could, at times, move others more deeply.

And that’s precisely why…

<em>I will… say things as they are. About why I wish to support Yuki-sama. All of it.</em>

Holding only that conviction in her heart, Ayano stepped onto the stage, letting her thoughts drift back to the distant past as she slowly began to speak.

“I do not think that all of you know this, but… when Yuki-sama was young, she suffered from severe asthma.”

Murmurs rippled throughout the audience at her unexpected revelation, delivered before she had even bothered to introduce herself. Paying no attention to any of it, Ayano pushed on.

“As a child, Yuki-sama’s asthma had rendered her unable to attend elementary school properly and… she spent most of each day confined to her bed. Vigorous exercise was out of the question, of course, but more than that, she could not even laugh or cry as her heart wished. Both it and her body were bound by illness.”

The initial confusion that weighed on the students gradually, yet steadily, gave way as Ayano’s words sank into their heads.

“But even so, Yuki-sama had always conducted herself as a brave girl on the surface. Even when she could not get out of bed… she spent her days cheerfully, always with a bright smile, so as to not worry her family.”

For a moment, silence settled over the room as Ayano fought past the lump in her throat.

“However, precisely because of that...”

By this point, a crack had formed in her expressionless façade with tension gathering between her brows. Her throat continued to tighten and her normally flat tone contorted as notes of pain made their way into her voice.

“Seeing Yuki-sama whenever she suffered an asthma attack was truly agonizing; truly pitiful… It made I, who could do nothing but stand by her side and watch, feel utterly helpless and heartbroken…”

The air felt drawn tight as a wire, making even those who were listening finding themselves holding their breaths. Perhaps because her throat had completely given way at this point, Ayano broke off her words, taking a long, deep breath as if to compose herself.

“And yet…” she slowly began again after resetting her expression, “even in such unbearable circumstances, Yuki-sama never turned bitter… She never turned sour and remained noble to the very end. As the heir to the Suou family, she resolved to fulfill what was expected of her. She held that oath firmly within her heart and never allowed her illness to break her spirit, working harder than anyone else. It was from watching that noble figure up close that I came to desire to stand in service to her. I truly decided that I wanted to help her with everything I had.”

Her voice, albeit still pierced with small, lingering traces of pain, carried a powerful warmth that shook the hearts of those in the audience.

Whether she was aware of its effect or not, Ayano pushed on once again, now looking straight ahead.

“After that, Yuki-sama overcame her illness and… despite bearing the tremendous handicap of having spent so many of her early years bedridden, she grew into a splendid young lady, showing no signs of such a past whatsoever. Brimming with knowledge and sophistication, she became a paragon of what a lady should be like, someone whom everyone at this school respects and admires.”

Still, this was something everyone present already knew.

“However…” Ayano paused, her voice ringing with absolute conviction, ready to share something no one else could have possibly known. “I know about it all. I know just how precious and rare her presence has truly become. That is precisely why I will continue to support Yuki-sama with all my strength. I will, without a doubt, use everything I have to support her, to assist her. That is my resolve, and the very meaning of my existence as the person known as Ayano Kimishima…”

Her words hung in the heavy silence that followed. Then, as if jolted back to the reality of her audience, Ayano quickly lowered her head in gratitude.

“Thank you very much for your kind attention.”

Stepping down from the stage, she was met with only hesitant, scattered claps… before they gradually grew louder, transforming into thunderous applause.

“Oooooohhhhhh!!!”

“We’re rooting for youuuu!!!”

“Do your beeeest!!!”

Passionate cheers roared one after another as Yuki and Ayano stood together at the right wing of the stage, their heads lowered into a bow.

There was no doubt about it whatsoever.

The stars of the moment were Yuki and Ayano.

⋆⋅☆⋅⋆

Watching the scene unfold from the other wing of the stage, Masachika could not help but grimace at how far the audience’s excitement had exceeded his expectations.

“Wait, wait, wait, wait…” he muttered in disbelief, his sheer panic escaping from his lips.

<em>Oh shit, oh shit. This is… seriously bad. They’ve taken complete control of the room.</em>

To top it all off, Yuki had painted her platform not on that of sweeping reforms or major changes, but rather a multitude of small changes: a position that directly conflicted with Alisa’s own agenda. With an audience won over so clearly and early like this, just how many students would even be willing to listen to her at this point…?

<em>Damn it, I totally underestimated them… I never imagined things would become</em> this <em>one-sided.</em>

There was no other way to describe it but a blunder. He had been so focused on Yuki’s supposed hidden trump card—the Christmas party—that he had satisfied himself merely by just preparing something capable of matching just that.

But thinking about it calmly… there was no way that those two, known for preparing everything with intense and meticulous care, would have been satisfied with an ordinary speech in just announcing some party.

<em>Tch, now ain’t that right! Why did I assume they’d only play it safe on the day of the dang closing ceremony!?</em>

Masachika had never thought that he was letting his guard down.

And yet… without even realizing it himself, he had grown completely complacent, probably intoxicated somewhere deep down by all this sense of omnipotence that had come with this supposed “invincible mode” he was in.

Now, not even a fragment of that exhilaration remained.

<em>Gah, I can save the regret for later!</em> Masachika desperately racked his brain.<em>Right now’s for figuring out how to get through this…!</em>

But just them, a voice suddenly reached his ear.

“Are you okay, Alya-chan?” It belonged to none other than Maria.

Startled by her quiet voice, Masachika turned toward her, only to find Alisa there, standing with her usual stern expression, yet an undeniable air of unease. Beside her was Maria, looking at her with concern… The sight made Masachika want to beat himself senseless for carelessly letting his panic show.

<em>Gah, the heck am I doing…!? I’m the one who said I’d support her…!</em>

But while he grounded his teeth in frustration… the moment arrived.

“Up next, the student council’s treasurer, Alisa Kujou-san, will now give her address.”

The emcee’s announcement rang through the gymnasium, signalling their turn. Without saying even a word to Masachika, Alisa stepped forward with a tense expression.

…Only for Masachika to instinctively grab her hand and stop her.

“Hm!? Masachika-kun?”

Alisa turned around in surprise. Meeting her gaze, Masachika made his mind up in an instant.

“Change of plans,” he declared in a resolute tone. “I’m going up.”

“Eh? But…”

“Just wait, ’kay?” he forced on a fearless grin to his partner’s wavering gaze. “I’ll take back the atmosphere for sure.”

With the bold proclamation, Masachika strode toward the stage without waiting for a response. A chorus of confused whispers rose from both the audience and the emcee, but he had no room to care about that now.

<em>Oh shiiit. I’ve got nothing.</em>

The speech he’d originally prepared might as well have been a blank sheet of paper now. It was all winging it from here on.

<em>This ain’t even funny…</em>

And he was right. There was nothing funny about the situation at all. All Masachika could do now was bitterly curse at his own carefreeness for not having prepared a backup plan despite knowing that something like this could happen.

<em>Haaaah</em>, he whined in his heart. <em>I’d already gotten carried away with this whole invincible mode I thought I was in last time… Man, I don’t ever learn, huh?</em>

Was pushing his brain so far into a corner to avoid reality a form of self-defense? Because mocking himself like this felt strangely out of place even to Masachika.

Still continuing to mock himself, Masachika stepped onto the stage. However, the moment he laid eyes on the rows of students beyond the dazzling lights… a single secret plan suddenly hatched in his otherwise empty head.

The sort of plan that made him derisively chuckle on the inside.

<em>No way… I’m actually gonna have to do something like that.</em>

Alea iacta est. Masachika slowly drew in a breath before unleashing his voice toward the microphone…

⋆⋅☆⋅⋆

<em>This… is pretty bad, is it not?</em> Sayaka discretely furrowed her brow as she joined the surrounding students in applauding Yuki and Ayano.

Having promised to cooperate with the new student council should Alisa win the election, Sayaka had already heard her campaign promise in advance. It was something that she certainly felt would leave a huge impact on the student body, but… only in the case of the audience being prepared to listen.

<em>And here I was thinking this would be a close contest up until those two gave their speeches…</em>

Because the reality was that the atmosphere had not been bad at all right up until then. The number of people who viewed Alisa in a positive light had increased noticeably thanks to her accomplishments during the cultural festival and how she had carried herself in the recent student assembly, after all; you could even hear many voices anticipating what kind of speech she would be giving today.

But now…

Those presently gathered were now deeply moved by Yuki and Ayano, their hearts completely swept away in the tide of their speeches. At this point, Alisa’s words would most likely fail to even register in the minds of the audience when they were so caught up in this sort of excitement.

<em>Neither Nono-chan nor I will be lending a hand this time, okay…?</em> Sayaka murmured to herself. <em>Are you really going to be fine?</em>

It was only after she begged that question in her heart did Sayaka realize something: somewhere along the way, she had begun to think from the perspective of someone rooting for Alisa and Masachika as if it were second nature.

<em>Wh-what am I even doing…? Did being in a band together make me soften up to her or something…?</em>

Overcome by what could only be described by panic, Sayaka gave her head a small shake before turning her gaze back toward the stage.

<em>I mean, it’s not like I’m cheering for them or anything… I merely just don’t want to see my friends make a fool of themselves, that’s all…</em>

As she found herself mouthing off excuses to no one in particular, the emcee finally called on Alisa’s name, thinning the buzzing excitement rippling through the gymnasium into only a few scattered whispers.

However…

For some reason, it was Masachika who stepped onto the stage.

“Hm?” Sayaka murmured, involuntarily letting her confusion slip, the students around her seeming equally bewildered.

“Eh?”

“Huh?”

“Why Kuze?”

Yet, amidst all that chatter, she immediately grasped Masachika’s intentions.

<em>Ah, I understand. He’s realized that even Alisa-san speaking in this atmosphere wouldn’t have much of an effect.</em>

In other words, he would step out first to change the mood before handing things over to her.

Easier said than done…

It was simply just as hard for Masachika to get people to listen amidst the current mood as well, after all. Sure, his unexpected appearance had certainly piqued their interests, but it still wasn’t enough to cool the excitement that clouded their heads.

<em>Just how on earth is he planning to turn things around from here…?</em> Sayaka asked herself.

As she watched on with half interest and half concern, Masachika finally opened his mouth atop the stage.

【Дорогие друзья，позвольте представиться．Меня зовут Кудзэ Масатика．Я из общего отдела ученического совета． Разрешите поприветствовать вас в качестве доверенного лица кандидата в председатели совета Кудзё Алисы．】<a href="#TN1" id="Return1"><sup>[1]</sup></a>

Soooo much Russian. The students collectively blanked. Sayaka, too, stood frozen with her mouth slightly agape.

“Sorry,” Masachika paused for a moment with a soft chuckle. “I was so nervous I actually started speaking in Russian.”

Laughter erupted throughout the entire gymnasium.

“No way that just happens!” a voice that sounded <em>extremely</em> familiar came flying out somewhere in the crowd.

Even Sayaka couldn’t help but let out a laugh.

Feeling the atmosphere relax all at once, Masachika raised both hands with an easy going smile. “Now then, once again, I’m Masachika Kuze, the student council’s officer for general affairs. Oh, that Russian just now? I did have some help from this translation website, okay? Trying to memorize it by ear’s actually super difficult though, y’knooow? Pronouncing words in Russian is seeeriously rough.”

Sticking his tongue out briefly and shrugging in shoulders in an exaggerated, comical manner, Masachika drew another round of laughter.

“Now then, everyone,” he continued in a casual tone. “You’re all surely wondering why I stepped out here first, aren’t you? Ah, and just to be clear, our emcee here didn’t get the order wrong: this was entirely our own doing. Don’t go havin’ that misunderstanding, ’kay?”

Pressing his palms together toward the emcee, he lowered his head into a bow. “Sorry for taking liberties like thiiiis.”

Clearing his throat lightly, he faced back toward the audience.

“Now then, to start over, as for why I stepped out here first… Well, there’s only one reason.” The casual air vanished from his voice. “Before my partner here speaks about her campaign promise, I wanted you all to prepare yourselves for what you’re about to hear.”

Having heard a little about the plan beforehand, Sayaka knew that Masachika’s appearance before Alisa had been completely unplanned. Yet, Masachika gave no sign of that whatsoever. His tone and demeanor told the tale that everything was proceeding exactly according to schedule, speaking slowly and carefully as if teaching the audience step by step.

“The campaign promise that Alisa Kujou will be about to present is exceptionally ambitious and—among all past student council presidential candidates—incredibly unconventional and different. I think you all will struggle to understand it at first too. Like, ‘Can you even do something like that?’ Some of you might naturally beg the question. <em>I</em> sure did. Honestly, I couldn’t fully wrap my own head around it initially when I first heard what she was aiming for from her own lips.”

Confusion spread among the students at what was an utterly bizarre speech for a student council member’s address. Yet, through that confusion, Sayaka could sense the fervor that had dominated the gymnasium moments earlier being completely rinsed away.

“But,” Masachika continued in clear signal that he was not done, “after listening to her explain all the details and coming to understand what she’s aiming for, for what kind of student council she’s seeking to create… it became something that felt truly wonderful to me as well. I became convinced that it was something worth pursuing seriously—right from the bottom of my heart—and firmly resolved myself to help achieve it.”

Sweeping his gaze across the crowd as confused glances darted from face to face, Masachika anchored the restless room with a low, deliberate cadence.

“I want to tell you all this beforehand: we are absolutely serious. We are entering this presidential election earnestly with this promise, and we fully intend to make it a reality. So please, without laughing it off as empty grandstanding, please listen to her words with an open mind. That’s all from me. Thank you all very much for listening.”

⋆⋅☆⋅⋆

<em>Tch, I’m pathetic, huh…?</em>

Facing the sparse round of applause ringing out here and there, Masachika trotted back toward the wings of the stage, his heart overcome with bitterness.

In the end, all he had managed to do was reset the atmosphere and bring things back to square one. And that was at best. From here, everything else had to be entrusted entirely to his partner. To him, all he could see in the moment were things he should reflect on.

“Good work,” came Alisa’s voice.

“Yeah…” Masachika murmured, returning with his head lowered in apology. “Sorry; I wasn’t much help.”

“That’s not true,” his partner simply shook her head and smiled. “To improvise like <em>that</em> and go that far… I don’t think I could’ve done it myself; you really helped me out.”

“……”

Though her words eased him a little, the reality remained that he had been forced to improvise in the first place because he had been caught flat-footed. His bitter expression refused to soften, unable to shake the immense sense of responsibility that weighed on him. Seeing him like that, Alisa offered a faint smile.

“Geez, don’t make that face,” she nudged his shoulder gently. “I never expected Yuki-san and Ayano-san’s speeches to be <em>that</em> impressive either. It isn’t your responsibility alone; we’re a pair, after all… As long as the two of us can combine our strengths to achieve the best possible result, it’ll be okay. I’m right, aren’t I?”

Her gentle question allowed Masachika to finally manage a small smile. “Yeah…” he nodded. “You’re right.”

“Yes. Now’s your turn to watch me, okay?”

No sooner had Alisa said that than the emcee’s announcement rang out.

“Once again, the student council’s treasurer, Alisa Kujou-san, will now give her address.”

Seeing Alisa turn toward the stage, Masachika caught her eye and gave her a small nod.

“Good luck,” he said, his brief words carrying all the faith he had in her.

“Yes,” Alisa let out a soft breath. “Well then…”

Cutting her own words off at that point… she turned back with a small smile.

“I’ll go win this real quick.”

⋆⋅☆⋅⋆

As Alisa stepped onto the stage, countless gazes filled with a mix of curiosity and anticipating turned toward her.

How would she clear the impossibly high bar that Masachika’s introduction had raised?

What exactly was this campaign promise he had spoken of?

Everyone eagerly waited for the moment that answer would finally be revealed.

“As I had been introduced, I am Alisa Kujou, the student council’s treasurer. Today, I would like to take this opportunity to broach the policies I intend to implement should I be elected student council president for the coming year.”

Her words pushed the audience’s anticipation to a fever pitch. The atmosphere was immense and trampled over her from all sides. Nevertheless, Alisa continued calmly, not dropping her unwavering conviction.

“I will,” she paused, gripping the edges of the podium, “abolish the system that limits eligibility for admission into the Raikoukai to only the student council president and vice president.”

Silence. That was all that swept across the students. Even after Masachika’s lengthy preface, most were unable to fully process the bold declaration she had just made, remaining sat frozen in place.

“Beyond just the president and vice president…” Alisa leaned forward, her voice cutting through the gymnasium with absolute certainty, “any student within the student council who has achieved accomplishments worthy of earning such a qualification will be allowed to join the Raikoukai.”

It was only with this nail in the coffin that most of the students began to understand what Alisa was putting on the table…

Yet, at the same time, another wave of confusion erupted among them.

“H-huh?”

“Wha? What did she just say?”

“Uh? Wh-what does she mean?”

Their bewilderment was only natural.

Because simply put, at first glance, Alisa’s promise offered no apparent benefit to herself at all. Even if she became student council president and secured her own ticket to being part of the Raikoukai, what was possibly in it for her in petitioning them to extend that same ticket to others? If anything, one would think it offered nothing but risk: the possibility of having her own qualification revoked.

For students at this school, the reason for aspiring to become the student council president was, first and foremost, entry into the Raikoukai. That was the fundamental premise.

Some wanted to join to secure a stable future. Others wanted to join as it was key to making their dreams come true. A number sought admission because it was a duty imposed upon them by their parents.

The details varied from person to person, but the bottom line was that everyone shared the same goal: they wanted to join the Raikoukai.

As a result, those who were elected student council president basically never did anything that could be seen as challenging the Raikoukai themselves. Much less would an ordinary student dare interfere with the very structure of the organization itself.

“Could it be… she wants someone other than herself and Kuze to join the Raikoukai too?” one student suggested.

“Oh…” another wondered. “Is that what this is?”

Though still confused, the audience desperately searched for an explanation that made sense. After hearing the murmurs settle down somewhat, Alisa began with her rationale.

“Since enrolling in this school and working within the student council as one of the candidates for president… I have come to meet many who aspire to join the Raikoukai.”

Pausing briefly, Alisa faced the collective gaze of the audience head on.

“People who have ruthlessly trampled over others in a bid to join the Raikoukai,” she spoke each word as though savoring their weight. “People who have dragged the entire academy into turmoil in the pursuit of admission. People who have lost their qualification to join and, after much anguish, went down the wrong path.”

Sayaka Taniyama, whom she had once faced in a debate and now counted as a friend.

Yuusho Kiryuuin, once an enemy whom she later temporarily joined forces with.

Taiki Kaji, whose circumstances Yuki had once spoken of with a tinge of blue in her voice.

“And people,” Alisa spoke on, “who despite deeply caring for each other… ended up in conflict over who would join the Raikoukai.”

They were, of course… Masachika and Yuki: a brother and sister who loved one another, yet found themselves clashing over who would join the Raikoukai and inherit the Suou family. And Ayano too, caught in between them, equally devoted to both siblings.

And then there was Masachika himself, who still suffered as he wrestled with his own feelings.

Recalling all their faces, Alisa could only harden her eyes as she spoke, driven by a sense of duty that tugged at her heart.

“I… want to create a system in which all of them can join hands and work together. I want to give these candidates—those who had no choice but to compete against and fight each other—the choice to cooperate.”

So that close friends would not have to become rivals. So that, instead of dragging each other down, they could shoot for the Raikoukai together.

“If we can do that, this school will surely become an even better place! Even if there are limits to what a president and vice president can accomplish, to what two people can do alone, imagine having two pairs, three pairs, all possessing the same level of strength: surely we can achieve so much more! This,” Alisa paused, the word hanging in the air as her resolve shone through, “is the type of student council that I want to build!”

Hearing her plea, one that almost sounded like a cry from the heart, the audience finally came to understand the type of student council that Alisa idealized.

The scene of her standing shoulder to shoulder with Sayaka and Nonoa during the first semester’s closing ceremony. and the words that Masachika had offered, suggesting to bring Yuki and Ayano into their circle at the time, were evidence to what Alisa was speaking about now: making a new student council, one that the two of them had envisioned back then, the norm at this school.

It was certainly an ambition that was vast… A dreamlike fantasy.

“I had said this during the first semester’s closing ceremony: if I could not believe myself as worthy of being student council president, then I will withdraw from the election by my own accord.”

It was a resolve that Alisa had once declared, yet one she now sought to overwrite with one even greater.

“I retract these words,” she announced, placing one hand over her chest. “Whether I’m worthy or not is irrelevant. I <em>have</em> to become the student council president, for the student council that I envision—the Seirei Academy that I envision—will surely be more graceful, more enjoyable, than it is now! And because <em>I</em> am the only one who can make that ideal a reality! It is something that only I can do, for I am the only person with a foot in this race running not to join the Raikoukai, but to simply become student council president for the sake of being the student council president!”

Her words were delivered with such an immense force that they seemed to be directed not at the audience, but at herself. It bore testament to her character: someone who sought for her ideals without the approval or sympathy of those who listened.

“I will remain true to this ideal that I have found,” Alisa proclaimed, gazing upward with an intense pride. “I will devote nothing but my utmost effort into making it a reality. As for whether someone like me is worthy of becoming the student council president… I’ll leave that judgement to all of you. Thank you very much for listening.”

Bowing her head, Alisa stepped down from the stage, and yet, even as she began to walk away, all the students remained frozen in place. They had completely lost their abilities to speak, utterly overwhelmed by such a display and could only watch her departure in silence.

It was only after Masachika emerged from the wings to stand beside her in a bow did a few claps finally rise from somewhere within the crowd, gradually growing across the gymnasium into an applause that gently rained down upon them.

Sure, it did not harbor the same sheer volume heard in the ovation that Yuki and Ayano had received, but nobody present cared about that.

Because <em>everyone</em> was applauding.

The difference was merely in the nature of the applause.

The ovation directed at Yuki and Ayano had carried a sense of enthusiasm.

The applause now directed at Alisa and Masachika was one of respect.

An applause full of respect for two people walking forward while carrying the weight of an immense ideal: an applause from those who watched over them… From those who could do nothing <em>but</em> watch.

⋆⋅☆⋅⋆

In the end, neither pair emerged from the closing ceremony with a clear advantage over the other… But even so, in spite of this, everyone came away sharing a single conviction.

A conviction that either Alisa Kujou or Yuki Suou would become the next student council president. And that no matter which of the two would ultimately claim such a position, Seirei Academy would undoubtedly change.

With the students’ anticipation quietly growing stronger… the second semester’s closing ceremony came to a close.
