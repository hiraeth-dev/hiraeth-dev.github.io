“By the way, what kinda campaign promises are you going with, Alya-san?”

A few days had passed since the entire debacle at the student council office. The four of them—that is, Masachika, Alisa, Takeshi, and Hikaru—were currently in the classroom eating their lunches, with Takeshi being the first to strike up a conversation.

“Campaign promises…?”

“Ah, no, I’m just curious, y’know?” he clarified. “Since you know how it’s basically expected for candidates to touch on their campaign promises in their speeches during the second semester’s closing ceremony…? It just sorta got me wondering.”

Watching him wave his hand in front of his face as if to say that there was no deeper meaning behind it, Alisa fell into thought.

Though she had certainly heard about such a closing ceremony tradition from quite a while ago, there was no denying that she still hadn’t decided what exactly she would say.

<em>What should I do…</em>

She was at a complete loss for words.

“About that… Look, consider it somethin’ to look forward to at the actual ceremony,” Masachika chimed in, jumping in from the side to help her out.

“Eeeh<em>~</em>? For reaaal?”

“Oh, the current president’s campaign promise was about changing the summer uniforms, right?” Hikaru noted.

Hearing Takeshi whine, he caught Masachika’s glance, quickly helping to change the subject.

“That <em>was</em> his main promise, huh?” Masachika murmured. “I think he also mentioned making a bunch of smaller changes to things that were pretty outdated too, though…”

“Ohhh, that’s right, huuuh? It’s seriously amazing that he pulled all of that off. Like, honestly, I’d thought that there’d be no way any of that would work when I first heard it…”

Seeing Takeshi immediately taking the bait, Masachika shrugged with a small smile.

“Oh well, making all sorts of false promises can at worst getcha a vote of no confidence, y’know? So I guess we actually follow through on stuff like that here, unlike real politicians.”

“You say that as if real politicians never keep their promises…” Hikaru replied with a wry smile. “No, wait, yeah, nevermind.”

Putting a hand to her chin, Alisa glanced at the ceiling. “A vote of no confidence, hm? Is that when a student assembly declares… the sitting president and vice president unfit for their positions, by any chance? Has that actually ever happened?”

“Hmm, not that much in recent years, apparently…” Masachika answered. “Ah, but, I’ve heard ’bout this story from around five years ago where they supposedly got stripped of their eligibility for the Raikoukai, y’know?”

“Eh, seriously?”

“Yep. They actually weren’t removed from being president themselves though… Wait, actually, now that I think about it, being told you can’t join the Raikoukai but still being made to work ’til the end sounds pretty rough, don’t it?”

“True…”

“Like, right? It’s basically just unpaid labor at that point.”

“Why did that specific president get a vote of no confidence, by the way?”

“Hmm… If I recall it’s ’cause they failed to fulfill a campaign promise. Guess I forgot the details.”

“Speaking of campaign promises…” Hikaru interjected, suddenly speaking up as though a thought just occurred to him. “The light music club was apparently banned ’til just a few years ago, right? At least until the president at the time gave them the okay, or something.”

“Eh, for real?” Takeshi chirped. “Even <em>I</em> didn’t know that.”

“Yeah. Well, I mean… it wasn’t actually <em>just</em> the light music club, you know?” Masachika added. “There was this sorta period where requirements to form a club were absurdly strict, at least compared to now… Or rather, it was basically like an impossible game, so that president you’re thinking of campaigned on easing those restrictions and actually made it happen in the end, basically.”

“Oooooh.”

As Masachika gave a more detailed explanation to Takeshi, Alisa tilted her head in curiosity. “What do you mean by it was impossible to form a club?”

“Hmm. Basically something ’bout how only clubs deemed ‘worthy’ of Seirei Academy were allowed to exist, or something? From what I’ve heard, all it took was a teacher to decide something like, ‘This club is not suitable for this school,’ to get your proposal rejected.”

“Even if they had enough members?”

“Even if they did. It’s why clubs like the wind ensemble or piano club were fine while the light music club was basically a no-go.”

“Why?”

“Who knows? Apparently there was this kinda view that bands are for delinquents back then, or something?” Masachika remarked, glancing toward Hikaru and Takeshi, earning nods from the both of them.

“I’ve heard from my parents that there really was a time like that, actually,” the former said.

“Yeeeeah,” the latter hummed. “Good ol’ rock bands were the sorta groups that rebelled against society and its norms and stuff, ya know? I’ve honestly heard some pretty outrageous stories about them too.”

“Eeeh?” Alisa tilted her head. “Is that so? I didn’t know that at all.”

“Nobody’s got that sorta prejudice against rock bands anymore nowadays though,” Masachika said with a wry smile. “I guess this school’s just behind the times, after all. Like, they did ban them ’til <em>only</em> a few years ago and all.”

He shrugged.

“Oh well, with all those restrictions loosened and stuff, all sorts of clubs popped up one after another, including the light music club… And that meant they ended up running outta classrooms pretty quickly, causing a whole mess like what happened the other day, you know?”

“<em>Ahh</em>…” Alisa breathed, understanding the catch.

Scratching the back of his head sheepishly, Takeshi hunched his shoulders. “But man… I kinda feel really bad that you got dragged into this whole conspiracy just ’cause our club wanted a new room, Alya-san…”

“Eh? Ah, that’s not really something you need to apologize for, Takeshi-kun…”

“No, seriouslyyyy,” he whined, half grumbling and ignoring Alisa’s slightly bewildered response as he recalled what happened back then. “I never thought things would turn out like that. Sure, I did think our clubroom was cramped and inconvenient and all, but I never imagined we’d actually go and negotiate with the piano club… And, like, we got this whole student assembly out of nowhere, with the vice piano club’s president getting seriously cornered and lookin’ all pitiful. Only for these random bunch of people to start showing up outta nowhere too, huuuh?”

“Yeah… Honestly, even <em>I</em> had no idea what was going on…” Hikaru added. “You really handled it calmly though, didn’t you, Alya-san?”

He gave a look of admiration, with Takeshi joining in as he vigorously nodded in agreement.

“Exactly! We were all watching from the audience, and maaan, you were seriously cool! I was like, woooah, she’s amazing!”

“Th-thank you...”

“No like, seriously, it gave me the chills, ya know? I mean, from the way you quietly struck back at all those guys who set up all that fraud, Alya-san! Like, it kinda somehow even made me feel proud as your former bandmate too, or something like that… It also… actually became a huge topic in the light music club as well!”

“Eh, really?” Alisa asked, rapidly blinking her eyes.

“Yeah,” Hikaru confirmed with a gentle smile and nod. “Alya-san, a lot of people were saying you looked really cool back there, you know? No, well, you already had a ton of people from the light music club who liked you from back when you were performing with us at the cultural festival, though… But judging by how things are now, almost everyone there will probably vote for you in next year’s student council election, don’t you think?

“<em>Eeh</em>!? …Really?”

His words bewildered Alisa, unable to bring herself to believe them.

“Well it’s honestly not <em>that</em> strange, is it?” Masachika remarked as though it were only natural. “Like from last week when we were searching for <em>that</em> person with Tsukamoto-senpai too: there were a lotta people who were clearly for you, ain’t that right?”

“Ah, <em>ahh</em>, well…”

In fact, Alisa was only met with constant praise for how she had acted during the student assembly everywhere she went, with many even offering warm words of support.

Still, maybe because it hadn’t truly hit her yet, she continued to tilt her head in puzzlement, as though it were actually happening to somebody else.

It was something that Takeshi and Hikaru could only smile warmly at.

“We’re really gonna have to make you face reality during the second semester’s closing ceremony, huuuh?” the former mused.

“Haha, now ain’t that right?” the latter agreed. “Alya-san, I think you’d be surprised, you know? With just how many people are rooting for you right now.”

“…Really?”

With Alisa’s timid doubt showing once more, Takeshi and Hikaru glanced at each other and laughed.

However, as though he suddenly noticed something, Hikaru turned toward Masachika…

“Oh right. We sorta just let that slide just now, but… you were referring to searching for <em>that</em> mastermind back there, amirite?”

“Hm? Ohhh… I mean, yeah, I guess.”

“Ooooh” he hummed in with interest. “So they’ve apparently got caught or something, huh? Wasn’t it… Kimishima-san who got to them? I heard they were let go due to lack of evidence though…”

Though that was how the official story went, the reality was that the real mastermind had never actually been caught… Even more so, the other true mastermind—who had attempted to frame Alisa during that very student assembly—had actually been Nonoa, something that neither Hikaru nor Takeshi had any way of knowing.

And it was all something that Masachika had no intention of letting them in on.

“Yeah, something like that,” he simply nodded with a straight face. “Ah, just sayin’, but I can’t exactly go talking ’bout who it was, okay?”

“Oh, well that’s expected, I guess?” Takeshi said with a shrug.

<em>“</em>Hmm, gotcha…” Hikaru followed in tow. “But man, it really makes you wanna know, amirite? Like Takeshi was saying earlier, that certainly was one heck of a twist… but now that I think about it, that logically only means the mastermind had been orchestrating it all from the start, hadn’t they?”

Takeshi fell into a stunned silence, blinking repeatedly as though the realization had only just hit him. “…Huh? Ah, so was that what it was?”

“Haha,” Hikaru chuckled wryly in response. “But really, that has to be what happened, right? That sudden summoning of a student assembly too: that probably was just the mastermind pulling strings behind the scenes. Isn’t that not it?”

“Huuuh!? Ahhh, I get iiiit… Ohhh, so that’s how it was, huuuh…? Man, now I’m even more curious ’bout who this mastermind really is!”

With that, Takeshi suddenly leapt forward toward Masachika.

<em>“</em>C’mooon<em>, tell me secretly, okay?”</em> he sneakily whispered, raising a hand right beside his mouth. <em>“For friendship’s sake!”</em>

“Not a chance. I’ve got confidentiality obligations, ya know?”

“Gaaah! For real!? I’m sooo curious though!”

Seeing Takeshi blatantly wear his heart on his sleeve, itching with both curiosity and an immense dissatisfaction, Masachika and Alisa could only exchange glances, letting out slightly bittersweet smiles as Hikaru watched it all with a blank sense of puzzlement…

⋆⋅☆⋅⋆

<em>“Hey.”</em>

“Hm?”

It was now after they had finished eating, with everyone preparing for the afternoon classes. Having heard Alisa quietly call out to him from his side, Masachika confirmed that the teacher had still not arrived before leaning in slightly toward her.

<em>“What’s up?”</em> he asked, whispering back.

<em>“Um, it’s about Nonoa-san…”</em>

<em>“…Ahhh.”</em>

Fully understanding what she meant with those words alone, Masachika briefly glanced toward Hikaru in the seat ahead, quickly shaking his head from side to side.

<em>“There’s no need to tell them that. No one’s ending up happy even if we did, don’tcha think?”</em>

<em>“Yeah… That’s right, huh?”</em>

In truth, the chances of Nonoa doing anything suspicious to Takeshi or Hikaru were pretty low at the moment, with the real issue lying in the risk of her doubling down if they learned the truth. It was a line of reasoning that Alisa probably understood too.

“……”

Even so, Nonoa was still a bandmate to Takeshi and Hikaru, and that very fact alone left Alisa completely restless. Because, at the end of the day, they were two people she probably considered as friends too.

<em>I mean, I can’t say I don’t understand her dilemma and doubt over whether it’s really okay to leave those two in the dark, but still…</em> Masahika mused, fully understanding his partner’s feelings.

He shrugged deliberately, his tone light. <em>“I mean, it’s not like you need to know someone’s true nature to get along with them, y’know? You and Yuki were like that too, no?”</em>

<em>“Ah…! That</em> is <em>true, right?”</em>

Presented with such an easy-to-understand example, Alisa let out a small wry smile.

<em>“So, well, just don’t worry about ’em,” Masachika cheerfully continued, smiling back at her.</em> “<em>Look, just go ahead and think ’bout those campaign promises that Takeshi was talking about instead.”</em>

<em>“Hey… I don’t appreciate being spoken to as if I haven’t been thinking about it at all, okay?”</em>

<em>“Ohhh? That sooo?”</em>

Pulling himself back from his leaning position just as the teacher entered the classroom, Masachika then added one final remark.

<em>“Oh well, you’ve got almost a month ’til the closing ceremony. Feel free to take your thinking ’bout it, okay?”</em>

A suggestion delivered with such casual ease.

And yet, it would only take Masachika a few hours to realize how naïve that way of thinking had been.

⋆⋅☆⋅⋆

“Okay then, I take this to mean the piano club’s continued existence has officially been decided, correct?”

“Yeah. I mean, they did properly get their membership numbers back within a month after falling below the minimum, so it <em>is</em> only natural, you know.”

It was now after school. Gathered in the student council office, its members set about discussing the aftermath of last week’s hectic student assembly.

Sat at his usual seat at the head of the long table was the President, Touya.

“Anyway, regarding that whole hunt for the mastermind that Chisaki and you all were carrying out… Just as Kuze had proposed, we’ve announced that a suspect has been caught but released due to a lack of evidence, stopping any further witch-hunts sprawling about on campus as a result. Or rather, it’s really starting to look like this whole fraud incident is apparently being viewed as just part of the elections, I s’pose. In any case, unlike during the cultural festival, no one was actually directly harmed, so it’s not like we’re having a situation where the student body’s seriously trying to hunt the culprit here… If anything, the only thing that’s really been heating up as of late is everyone speculating over who tried bringing down <em>both</em> li’l sis Kujou and Kimishima.”

“I’ve done a little digging too,” Chisaki chimed in. “The most popular theory is that someone besides Alya-chan and Yuki-chan is supposedly aiming to be the next student council president and attempted to take down both pairs at once, apparently.”

“Haha, well that’s very typical of this school, amirite?” Masachika quipped, giving a dry chuckle in response to how obvious his two upperclassmen were silently judging the students for being too carefree.

“So,” Touya peered at Masachika through his glasses. “How much of the truth have you lot actually figured out in the end?”

“…Yeeeeah.”

Slowly shifting his gaze away at his question, Masachika exchanged a glance with Yuki who sat across from him.

However, before either of them could decide how much to reveal, the president merely shrugged.

“Oh well, it <em>is</em> a rule that the sitting student council president and vice president shouldn’t meddle with the current candidates and the election in the first place… I won’t pry if you don’t want to say anything, I s’pose.”

“Thank you for your consideration...”

“Not at all… Still, make sure to not let your guards down; you <em>can</em> get the rug pulled out from under you and end up eliminated before the actual election, you know?”

Nodding with a small smile, Masachika accepted his senior’s words of warning. “Yes, understood.”

“’Cause the student council really won’t function anymore if we lose any more people than this!”

“Yeeeah. That really does sound like a life-or-death issue, doesn’t iiit?”

Seeing her brother half-smile at Touya’s rather painfully realistic concern, Yuki smoothly raised her hand up.

“Excuse me, Prez,” she spoke. “I would like for us to discuss that project from before. Would that be alright?”

“Hm? Ohhh… <em>That</em>, huh? Sure, go ahead.”

“Thank you very much.”

With a pleasant smile of her own, she then took some documents out from her bag.

<em>That project…?</em>

Not having the foggiest idea what she meant, Masachika exchanged a few glances with Alisa beside him before lowering his eyes to the document that Yuki handed over.

“Eh, a Christmas party...?” Alisa, who had also received a copy, voiced her surprise.

Written on the copies of paper was a proposal for holding a Christmas party on December 24th at school, right after the closing ceremony. It was to be specifically organized by Yuki and Ayano, with volunteer students serving as staff.

For the record, such an event did not exist anywhere on Seirei Academy’s annual schedule.

In other words… this was an entirely new initiative.

“As I had once mentioned at the first semester’s closing ceremony, I intend to listen to the student body’s wishes and hold various events once I become student council president,” Yuki explained. “You could say that this is the very first test of that idea, I suppose.”

Faced with such a casually dropped bombshell and the contents written in the documents before him, Masachika couldn’t help but feel his expression stiffen.

<em>Whoa, whoa, whoa, what’s this…?</em> he begged the question to himself. <em>Oh, I see now. So Prez had already known about this while it’s complete news to us, huh?</em>

It was unmistakably an effort to build a track record with the elections in mind and obvious to anyone that she intended to demonstrate that her declaration during the first semester’s closing ceremony had not been mere empty words.

<em>I’ve got no idea how long she’s been prepping this for, but… Tsk, this is pretty…</em>

Trailing his thoughts off, Masachika continued to sift through the documents, learning that recruiting the staff needed for the actual event had already been taken care of and that she had somehow already received approval from the school’s administration.

At this point, with the project already this concrete, there was nothing he could do.

Had it not been, he could have still likely forced his way into helping organize the whole thing with something like, “It sure sounds tough; lemme help, okay?”. That would’ve made it easy for him to steer things toward a whole “Sure, Yuki <em>did</em> come up with the big idea, but it wasn’t like it was accomplished through the efforts of the Yuki-Ayano pair alone, you know?” sort of dynamic.

<em>Oh well, I guess that’s exactly why she’s only showing this to us now.</em>

And as though she sensed those exact thoughts, Yuki clasped her hands together with an innocent looking smile before speaking in a tone that betrayed no ulterior motive.

“That party itself is to be run by myself and my volunteers, so I will not be requiring any assistance from the student council. I do hope that everyone will feel free to join and enjoy themselves by all means, okay?”

⋆⋅☆⋅⋆

“We’ve been had…” Masachika muttered through clenched teeth. “I never expected that Yuki had been pushing forward with a plan like this.”

It was now after the student council meeting. Masachika and Alisa, having returned to Class 1-B’s room, were now currently in an emergency strategy meeting of their own.

Naturally, the topic centered on the Christmas party project organized by Yuki, which had only just come to light.

“I know Yuki-san said that and all, but… what should <em>we</em> do?” Alisa asked. “Are we joining?”

“We don’t really have a choice…” Masacika replied with a bitter expression. “Prez and even Masha-san said they’d both be joining too. Not choosing to attend out of spite here basically just exposes us to the entire student body as petty and narrow-minded.”

Stopping to pause for a moment, he then let out a long sigh.

“Still, with a huge event like this, the second semester’s closing ceremony is surely getting its spotlight stolen.”

“That’s right, huh…” Alisa responded to Masachika’s grim voice with an equally serious look. “We were just talking about it during lunch too, but… it’s basically expected to talk about our campaign promises during that closing ceremony speech, right?”

“Yeah… The whole flow’s to introduce yourself during the first semester and touch upon your promises in the second, I guess. In Yuki’s case though, she was pretty much already so well-known that introducing herself wasn’t even necessary in the first semester, so she was able to basically skip the whole step and jump right to her campaign promises, y’know?”

Saying that much, Masahika then lightly waved the Christmas party documents in his hand.

“And now in the second semester, she’ll bring up her promises again and use <em>this</em> to give those words some actual persuasive power… Seeing is believing, after all. I guess there’s probably not a more effective way to make people feel the vision she has for this school, huh?”

“Right, yeah… Pulling off a project of this scale with just her connections would also show off her ability to execute things, wouldn’t it?”

“Yeah. Honestly… I guess against something like this, ordinary campaign promises aren’t gonna cut it if we’re talking about even just catching the student body’s interest, huh? That said, going for impact and making some grand promise would just make people doubt whether it’s actually feasible compared to this project. ‘Sure, that sounds impressive, but can ya really pull it off?’ I can already hear people asking that and all their scoffs.”

“I suppose… that’s how it will turn out, right?”

The more the both of them thought about it, the more perfect this calculated move appeared to be. At this point, it was more than fair to say that Yuki had already cemented her spot as the main character of the second semester’s closing ceremony.

But then, right at that moment, Masachika’s expression shifted as a new thought took hold.

“On the other hand,” he dropped his voice an octave, “if this project ends up more underwhelming than expected, or if some kinda scandal were to happen, then Yuki’s reputation’s getting absolutely crushed. Her approval among the student body would probably plummet too, but…”

However, after lowering his eyes right to the written proposal once more, he immediately shook his head.

“No, she’s probably covered all those bases judging from this document.”

“That’s right, huh?” Alisa agreed. “Besides… I don’t like hoping for our opponent’s failure or anything like that.”

“I mean, I guess, yeah. Anyway, to begin with, our biggest problem’s the fact that the closing ceremony’s now before this Christmas party.”

Shrugging with that in mind, Masachika scratched his head roughly as if to say, “The hell do we even do here?”

Going into this without some sort of counterplan would mean the student body would be completely preoccupied with thoughts of Yuki’s Christmas party by the time of the closing ceremony, after all. It was completely possible to imagine Alisa and Masachika’s speeches simply going in one ear and out the other.

<em>Inviting Alya onto a school broadcasting session right before the first semester’s closing ceremony, and now this too…?</em> Masachika mused. <em>Her thoroughness with prepping things in advance is just so expected of her, or rather, impressive… No, man, this ain’t the time to be admiring her.</em>

Finding himself unintentionally impressed by his younger sister’s meticulousness, Masachika shook his head from side to side.

“So then, what do we do? I did just ask you this at lunch, but do you already have some sort of idea for a campaign promise?”

His tone clearly carried the feeling that he was only “just asking for now.”

Still, holding her hand to her chin, Alisa slowly nodded in thought.

“…Yes,” she spoke after a moment. “There’s actually something that I’ve been thinking about.”

“Oh, really?”

Without even attempting to hide his surprise—or maybe because he simply didn’t have any composure left to do so—Masachika widened his eyes and stared intently at Alisa.

Perhaps he had even thought that Alisa had <em>only</em> sought the student council presidency merely for its own sake, having nothing in particular she wanted to specifically accomplish afterward.

<em>Well, he would’ve been correct though…</em> Alisa murmured to herself, giving a faint wry smile.

Because indeed, that had been true during the first semester. All she wanted was to simply stand above everyone, to become someone acknowledged by many others. That had simply been all, and she had never thought about what came after achieving such a feat.

However…

“……”

The student assembly.

The chaos during the cultural festival.

Masachika and Yuki’s determination to join the Raikoukai for the sake of their own goals.

The temporary alliance she’d formed with the latter due to Nonoa’s betrayal.

All these experiences had given Alisa one specific idea.

Slowly but surely, she put that thought into words.

“I want to…”

⋆⋅☆⋅⋆

Having listened to the vision that Alisa spoke of, Masachika widened his eyes more than ever before. In fact, Alisa herself understood that it was a reaction fully warranted from what she had just told him.

“What… do you think?”

Lowering his gaze at her hesitant tone, the boy in front of her drifted into a pensive silence.

But it was only natural. After all, Alisa honestly had absolutely no idea how such a thing could ever make what she thought of a reality, even after saying it all herself. All she only knew was that realizing such a goal was so daunting that “difficult” would be an utter understatement in and of itself.

And beyond that… what she was proposing directly conflicted with Masachika’s own objective of succeeding the Suou family in Yuki’s place.

<em>But… even so, I…</em>

Holding firm onto the unwavering resolve in her heart, Alisa continued to stare intently at her partner seated across from her…

After a few long seconds, Masachika let out a breath and slowly raised his own gaze.

<em>His eyes…</em>

His eyes, filled with such a resolute sense of resolve, held a look so direct and determined that they seemed to pierce straight through Alisa, feeling as though they gripped right at her heart.

“Got it.”

Short. Simple.

And yet, for Alisa, those very words felt more reassuring than anything else she had ever come across before.

Unable to give a response of her own immediately, she swallowed several breaths before finally finding her voice.

“…Are you sure?”

“Yeah. If that’s your wish.”

“But if we actually… make this a reality…” Alisa trailed off, biting at her lip.

Reading the hesitance at her core, Masachika gave her a reassuring smile.

“There’s no need to worry about something like that,” he spoke in a gentle tone. “What matters to me is… that you become student council president in the way that you want.”

“Masachika-kun...”

His devoted words, so free of hesitation and almost with the spirit of self-sacrifice, filled Alisa’s heart with both an immense joy and pain.

On one hand, she felt truly happy that Masachika was willing to devote himself to her this much, and yet, on the other, the sheer guilt of knowing that she was forcing a wish upon him that ran counter to his own goals sent agony down her spine.

As a countless number of emotions clashed deep within her heart, Alisa lowered her gaze, furrowing her brows tightly as though holding back her tears.

“Don’t make that face, Alya,” then came Masachika’s gentle voice.

Turning her gaze toward him, she found him looking at her with eyes that carried a deep calmness, yet a gaze that carried a strong sense of resolve.

“It’s not like I’m bending over backwards just to support you here. My will is to stand by <strong><em>you</em></strong>. That’s why… your goals are my goals too.”

In that instant, the words that Yuki had spoken a few days earlier resurfaced in Alisa’s mind.

<em>“But in spite of all of that… I really love that part of him: the way he can put his full effort into something when it’s for someone else.”</em>

The image of Yuki with her hands clasped right before her chest. The sound of her soft voice, filled so deep with affection. All of it came rushing back to her mind… and made Alisa feel like she was about to cry.

<em>Right… That’s exactly right…</em>

Squeezing her eyes shut and forcing back her tears, Alisa gave a small whisper of her own.

【I feel the same way.】

“Huh?”

“It’s nothing,” she answered, quickly turning away and leaving Masachika blinking in confusion. “<em>Mm</em>, alright!” she stood up with determination. “Then it’s settled: that’ll be the promise we’ll announce at the closing ceremony, okay!”

“Yeah, we’ll go with that, huh…?” Masachika stood as well, scratching his head with a wry smile. “Haha, never thought we’d end up putting forward such a huge promise like this.”

“Oh my? Getting cold feet, are we?”

“As if. I actually get excited when taking on goals worth striving for, y’know?”

His response came instantly, without a trace of hesitation. The smile on his face turned fierce, and a sharp radiance burnt within his dark eyes, as though their spark served to prove that his words held no lies whatsoever…

Taking it all in at once, Alisa shot back a satisfied nod, the fearless gleam in her own eyes contrasting the hesitation she clung onto earlier. “Good then. Let’s blow everyone away with our closing ceremony speech and march proudly into Yuki’s Christmas party.”

“Haha, now that plan sure sounds nice, huh…” Masachika dropped into a theatrical bow. “May this humble servant be entrusted with the honor of being your escort?”

“Yes,” Alisa accepted, meeting his show of extravagance with a poised smile. “Of course. There’s no one else I’d choose.”

“It would be the highest honor.”

Giving yet another polite bow, Masachika placed his hand over his chest.

Blinking lightly in response, Alisa eventually extended her right hand.

“And the kiss?” she asked, her head tilted with a soft smile as he took it.

“Hm?”

“You’ll be supporting me, won’t you? So, wouldn’t it only be fitting for you to seal it with a kiss of oath?”

Egging him on with a somewhat smug expression, Alisa watched as Masachika gave a small, wry smile… before dropping to one knee and lightly pressing his lips against the back of her hand.

“As you say… my princess.”

“Oh my… being called your ‘princess’ doesn’t sound so bad, does it?”

And so, the princess, freed from solitary and no longer untouchable, smiled with a spirited confidence.

The following day, after school.

Masachika, taking a break from having just finished a practice run with the wind ensemble club, suddenly felt an unusual number of eyes on him as he lifted his face from the piano.

Sure enough, a large number of members were staring straight right at him, making him unconsciously recoil back.

“…Uh, what?” he muttered with visible unease, right as the club’s president approached him with an impressed look on her face.

“Maaaan,” she beamed with awe. “Your playing’s sure got more intense again!”

“Elena-senpai… You really think so?”

“Mm-hmm. Did somethin’ happen?”

“Did something happen, huh…?”

Something <em>had</em> happened. Specifically yesterday, from when he had embraced Alisa’s wish, promising her that he would support it.

<em>It feels like… I’m back in invincible mode again, huh?</em> Masachika mused at the thought.

It felt as though he had been silently trekking upward in a deep fog all this while, only for it to suddenly clear, unblocking his path and allowing him a glimpse of the summit he was supposed to reach… That was the sort of feeling that surged through Masachika’s body right now, filling him with a dynamism almost akin to omnipotence.

“…I mean, I guess you could say I got inspired by a hardworking partner.”

“Oh? You mean Alisa-chan?”

“Pretty much.”

“Oho! Now that sounds spicy, doesn’t iiiit~?”

Annoyed at the characteristically suggestive tone in Elena’s voice, Masachika stood right up.

“I’m going to the restroom for a sec.”

Leaving only those words behind, he quickly exited the music room, with quite the number of female club members following his back with heated gazes.

Not that he noticed any of it, though.

“Oh my?” a voice called out to him in the hallway. “Now if it ain’t Kuzecchi.”

“Nonoa...”

Met with Nonoa’s presence and her characteristic half-open squint, Masachika immediately scrunched his face into a slight frown.

“Ehhh~? That reaction actually kinda hurts, y’knooow?”

Whining playfully in response, she casually smacked his arm a few times with a lazy grin.

“…What d’you want?” Masachika bluntly asked, not even attempting to match her energy at all.

Beckoning him over in response and without another word, Nonoa led him toward the deserted end of the hallway.

“Soooo,” she casually began, leaning her back against the wall with crossed arms. “There was that broadcast at lunch today announcin’ that Yukki’s hosting some Christmas party, right?”

“Yeah.”

“I could totally crash it if you’re up for it, y’know, Kuzzechi?”

“You little…”

Sharpening his gaze, Masachika fell into disbelief at how calmly she was able to say something so malicious with the same nonchalance of offering to lend someone a textbook…

However, quickly deciding it was pointless, he let out a long sigh.

“No need,” he replied curtly.

“Oh. That so?”

Leaving it at that in response to the answer she most likely expected from the start, Nonoa gave a shrug.

Still, despite her nonchalance, the reality remained that she would without a doubt have followed through had Masachika actually taken the offer.

And that was precisely why he decided to set the record straight.

“Don’t do anything extra,” he sternly warned. “We’ll be showing you something that’ll blow your expectations out of the water soon anyway.”

“Oooooh…?”

Curling the edge of her lips upward in amusement, Nonoa peered right into his face.

“Have you changed, Kuzecchi?”

“Maybe, huh?”

“Hmmmmm~?”

Letting that hum slip right from her chest, Nonoa pushed herself off the wall and spun around on her heel.

“Alrighty then, I’ll be lookin’ forward to seeing this thing that’ll quote-unquote blow my expectations outta the water, just so you know,” she teased. “Ah, I’m planning to go to the party too, so go ahead and dance with me if you feel like it, ‘kaaay~?”

Seeing her wave lazily over her shoulder as she walked away, Masachika hesitated for a moment before calling out to her.

“Nonoa!”

His voice stopped her right in her tracks, and she tilted her head slightly to its side. Masachika glossed over his next words carefully.

“I… honestly don’t think I can genuinely call you a friend anymore.”

He really simply couldn’t. Not after he knew that her malice could also be directed toward him and Alisa.

“But Alya’s different, you know?” he continued, this time in a slightly heavier voice. “Even now, even after all <em>this</em>, she still genuinely thinks of you as her friend.”

Keeping her gaze forward, Nonoa gave nothing away. Even so, Masachika continued, pleading with the hope that his words might reach her heart, even if just a little.

“Hey… can’t you just respond to Alya’s feelings as nothing more but a friend? Is that really impossible?”

Silence. For a while, that was all he got.

“If I could do that,” Nonoa muttered, eventually turning around, “then I wouldn’t have ended up like this.”<a href="#TN1" id="Return1"><sup>[1]</sup></a>

Leaving only those flat words behind, she walked away.

Watching her back disappear off into the distance, Masachika finally turned on his heel and began to walk in the opposite direction…
