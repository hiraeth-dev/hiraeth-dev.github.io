Hello, SunSunSun here, the original author. As I had also written in the afterword of Volume 8 of the manga adaptation, I, SunSunSun, quit my company job in August to become a full-time author. Wooooh! It reaaally ain’t exactly something I should be celebrating, though!!!

No, but all things considered, I guess I’d been a part-time author for about four and a half years, huh? Even I would call that a preeetty long time, wouldn’t I? So then, why did I keep this as just a side-gig for so long? I actually get asked that quite often, so I’ll answer it here: the reason was because I wanted to be prepared to be able to quit being a professional novelist at any time. That’s really how it was, you know? To put it more bluntly, it’s because I had neither the confidence or resolve to live the rest of my life as a novelist. I guess you’d get away with describing it like that, right? As I had written in the afterwords of Volumes 1 and 2, I was originally part of the whole “just enjoying Shousetsuka ni Narou”<a href="#TN1" id="Return1"><sup>[1]</sup></a> crowd, and never once actually thought that I’d be able to make my professional debut as a novelist.

So, embarrassingly enough, even when my debut had been set in stone, I actually had none of that whole “Alrighty, I’ll be making a livin’ solely on writing novels from here on out!” type of determination, you know? However, with Roshidere growing into a much bigger series that I could have ever imagined after, I unexpectedly found myself becoming an extremely busy author. But even so, that didn’t make me suddenly say things like, “Alright, guess I’ll go all in with being a full-time novelist, huh?” or anything… So that’s why, even though I was super swamped, I just couldn’t bring myself to quit my company job. In the end, what changed my mind was hearing that there were actually quite the number of authors and manga artists who went in full-time for a while, before then going back to being company employees during their forties and whatnot, you know? It meant that you didn;’t actually have to make a living through solely writing for the rest of your life just because you became a full-time author… Realizing that made it seem actually sort of obvious, and I thought, well, I guess maybe I don’t need to force myself to keep this double career way of living, huh? So, yes, in other words, I still don’t have the confidence or resolve to continue being a novelist for the rest of my life even now. Perhaps I’ll be back working as some company employee again in a few years from now, but for the time being, I think I’ll stick to writing Roshidere while consuming more material for creative input and the like—something that was pretty difficult to manage when I still had this whole double career thing. Hopefully I’ll be able to draw inspiration from all of that and even try writing a new series… That’s the sort of thing I’ve got in my mind right now, so I think I’ll try my best to work as a full-time and professional novelist for as long as I can.

Now then, I’ll leave my pointless ramblings about my own life at that. I’ve got an actual promotional announcement that I’ve been unusually asked to make, so let’s do that, shall we? Yep, unlike my usual promos that nobody actually asked for, this one came right from the editorial department. What is it, you may ask…?

In 2026, an official body pillow cover will finally go on sale to commemorate Roshidere’s fifth anniversary!!!

Yeah, that’s basically it. “What d’you mean ‘finally’?” “Don’tcha already have one?” I can already feel those questions flying my way, so let me explain what I mean. So, you know how the ones that already exist are ones with anime illustrations, am I right? This will be different from those: a body pillow cover featuring new illustrations drawn by the original illustrator, Momoco-sensei! Okay, so, why finally, you may ask…? Welp, from what I;’ve heard, body pillow covers are among the more expensive merch officially released by KADOKAWA<a href="#TN2" id="Return2"><sup>[2]</sup></a>, and it’s precisely for that reason that they’re apparently only made for a select few series… Well, but, now that I think about it, I’ve not actually bought a body pillow cover myself, you know? I don’t even have an actual body pillow to put it on in the first place, nor the space to hang it up on some wall for display either, so, yeah… Anyway, more than that, hey, you there! The one thinking that buying a body pillow’s kinda embarrassing! Yes, you! I completely getcha, but I can also assure you you won’t have to worry about that when it comes to this Roshidere body pillow cover this time around. And here’s why! Technically speaking, what you’d be getting this time ain’t no body pillow cover, but a novel that will come with said body pillow cover!!!

…You’re probably wondering what on earth I’m talking about, but I’ll say it one more time since it’s important. What’s being released isn’t a body pillow cover that comes with a bonus novel, but rather a novel that comes with a body pillow cover! What sort of novel, you ask? Well, a newly written story that matches the illustration on said body pillow cover. It’s about 10,000 Japanese characters long<a href="#TN3" id="Return3"><sup>[3]</sup></a>, and, of course, I’ve written it myself.

“No, the novel’s the bonus and not the other way around if it’s only 10,000 characters, ain’t it not?” “The body pillow cover is definitely accounting for the most of what we’re gonna be paying for, don’tcha think?” Yeah, I can already hear all you people objecting like that. But still, at the end of the day, it is first and foremost a novel that comes with a body pillow cover. KADOKAWA is officially taking this position; there can be no mistake about it. They;’d be able to sell it at bookstores nationwide by insisting on that, after all. And what’s more, you consumers can easily pick it up with a look on your faces that just says, “I’m just buyin’ a book; what of it?” It’s groundbreaking and genius, I’ll have you know. That’s really how it is. The latter part’s just how I’m interpreting it, though… So when the time comes for its release, please, everyone, go and put on your shamelessly innocent faces and buy it as if you’re sayin’ “I’m strictly here to just buy a book, okay…? Oh, the bonus is huge? Well, this kinda thing happens sometimes, y’know? Commonly with, like, magazines and stuff.” …No, but, in all seriousness, the price is what it is, so I can’t really casually urge you all with a simple “Please buy it”, right? So please treat this as merely an announcement that this specific product is coming out soon. As for all the details—including its price and illustrations—please wait for further updates from Roshidere’s official Twitter account (I am absolutely <em>not</em> calling it X; as for why, feel free to refer to the afterword of Volume 4 of the manga adaptation). Alright, that’s the promo I was asked to do wrapped up!

Well then, I’ve still got some pages left, so let’s talk ’bout boobs or something, shall we? Yep, I’m referring to the oh-so curious phenomenon of Chisaki and Nonoa somehow tending to get their breasts buffed when I supervise the illustrations for our game and other merchandise, while Ayano tends to get nerfed in that area and the height department. It’s… something I find strange, you know…? Officially speaking, Ayano’s supposed to be the one with the largest breasts among the main first-year characters excluding Alya. As I thought, I guess it’s a matter of character image, hm? Maybe Nonoa tends to get her breasts buffed because she’s supposed to be a model with a good figure, while Ayano tends to get hers nerfed because of the image or idea that she’s Yuki’s servant, and that servants are supposed to be “more restrained” in their presence than their masters. Could that be it, I wonder? That being said, if that’s the case, then I don’t really get why Chisaki tends to get hers made larger… Because, you guys also get this strong image of short-haired super-strong female characters being more slender, right? Oh well, even though I’m saying she tends to get buffed, it’s really only on the level of going from being more slender to average-sized. Huh? Sayaka? Yeaaah, she’s almost always never buffed. She’s always remarkably quite restrained in that depar—oh, whoa. Is that a murderous intent I sense?

Ah, just so I’m clear, this isn’t me making some kind of statement like, “There are so many dang illustrators not drawing my characters according to their original measurements; it’s a real pain, you know? <em>Haaaah</em>, good grief, good grief.” I really don’t mind it at all, and if anything, as the original author, it really does sound like I’m actually getting a pretty good deal out of being able to see all sorts of different variations to them, right? Every time, I’ll find myself muttering something along the lines of “Oh, Nonoa’s breasts do look sort of big this time, huh? I get it, this has its merits too… but how unfortunate. That’s far beyond you” before asking them to make the tweaks. By the way, when it comes to character figures in particular, Alya also tends to have her breasts buffed. She sometimes actually gets buffed so much that I even have to ask for a second round of tweaking with the thought, “I’ve had them fix it, but they’re still too big, huh…?” Hey, you there, if you’re sittin’ there thinkin’, “The hell are ya correcting it for? Give us that size, ya blockhead of a bastard,” I applaud you for being so honest about your desires, but let’s calm down a li’l.

Now then, I’ve got no more pages left, so I’ll move on to the announcements. First again, to Suzuki-sama, who took charge of editing this volume as well. Thank you very much for all your precise advice and thoughtful support. Next, to Momoco-sensei, who once again brought this series to life with an absolutely incredible lineup of illustrations for this volume. Thank you so very much, as always. And continuing, to Tenamachi-sensei, whose manga adaptation has recently finished covering the lore of Volume 2 of the original light novels. Thank you very much, and I appreciate all your hard work. I look forward to your continued support as you start to cover Volume 3 of the original light novels. Lastly, I would like to express a gratitude—too great to even be wrapped by a sarashi<a href="#TN4" id="Return4"><sup>[4]</sup></a>—to Miyagawa-sama, my lead editor whom I am always indebted to; Katou-sama, who oversees merchandising; everyone involved in this series’ publishing; and to all my readers who have been reading Roshidere. Thank you very much! Here’s to meeting again in the next volume’s afterword!
