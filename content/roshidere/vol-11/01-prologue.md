“…Say what now?”

It was the night when the piano club’s proposal in the to-be-convened student assembly had been brought to the student council. Masachika had been relaxing in his room, only to have suddenly received a call from Yuki, her words leaving him utterly perplexed.

<em>“Like I said,”</em> Yuki reaffirmed, <em>“It really sounds like there’s gonna be some sorta scheme aimed at Alya-san regarding that student assembly thing that came up today, the one between the piano and light music clubs. Just thought I’d let you know.”</em>

Of course, Masachika understood what she was saying. But why did Yuki have such information in the first place, and why was she going out of her way to tell Masachika, who was, by all accounts, a rival candidate?

“Why… are you warning me here?”

<em>“Because, to be honest… it looks like it’s gonna be something that might be more than I can handle by myself,”</em> Yuki answered after a brief pause in response to his frank question.

It was an unusually timid admission that only deepened his perplexity.

<em>“However,”</em> she then raised her voice slightly as she continued, <em>“I’ve got one condition. As payment for the info, I want you, Onii-chan, to promise not to interfere with the informant.”</em>

“And what’s that s’posed to mean?”

<em>“Well, to put it in a nutshell… Ayano’s the one who’s brought me this info, so we don’t want ol’ Onii-chan getting in the way of her spy work, do we now?”</em>

“Ayano? You mean she’s…” Masachika trailed off, recalling how strangely Ayano had been acting lately as he heard Yuki’s explanation.

<em>Ah, could this be what… that’s been about…?</em>

His childhood friend had looked to be carrying something heavy on her mind, after all. Faced with what seemed like the reason behind it, the option of not asking for the details had simply vanished for Masachika.

“…Alright,” he conceded. “So what’s with this info?”

A brief pause then hung in the air for a moment.

“Nonoa-san looks to be betraying Alya-san.”

“…Huh?”

⋆⋅☆⋅⋆

“What… do you mean?”

It was now early the next morning, where in the student council room, Alisa, having heard the warning that Masachika had gotten from Yuki, donned a look of complete bewilderment. Her mind struggled to keep up, and Masachika didn’t seem to fare any better.

“No like, me too… Nonoa betraying <em>you</em>? I don’t get it,” he parroted her confusion. “In any case, what I’ve heard from Yuki is that she’s trying to use Ayano to isolate me and her from coming to the student assembly, and that she’s trying to maneuver things so that you and Ayano would be assigned to counting the votes. There’s apparently supposed to be… some sorta scheme that’ll threaten your position then…”

Even saying it out loud himself didn’t help it make much more sense. In fact, it was probably why Yuki had passed along such information in the first place: for his help in making sense of it all.

“Regarding all of this… How certain is it?” Alisa asked after seriously thinking it over for a moment.

“I can’t imagine Ayano lying here…” yet Masachika’s tone showed he clearly couldn’t give a firm answer. “Her going all the way to spy is surprising, but again… I guess it’s something she might do if it’s for Yuki. Or rather, it sounds like she’s decided she couldn’t get any more info out of Nonoa and turned to Yuki for her judgment; that’s entirely plausible too. Still, there ain’t really much proof that Yuki herself’s being completely honest ’bout the whole thing either…”

The tip had come from a rival candidate, after all, a tip where they were now forced to suspect their own ally, no less. Who to trust, what to believe, or how far to accept any of it? None of it was clear.

And yet…

“My gut’s telling me that… Yuki’s not lying here. That’s what I think,” Masachika concluded, prompting deep lines to form between Alisa’s brows.

It was only natural. Trusting Yuki meant doubting Nonoa. And for Alisa, the sheer emotional strain of suspecting one of her only friends had to be immense.

“…What if we just go straight to Nonoa-san and probe her about it directly?”

“Nope, can’t do that.”

“Why’s that?”

“I promised I wouldn’t interfere with Ayano’s spying on Nonoa. Us messing up with the wrong move here could make her suspect that her scheme’s been leaked.”

“…In other words, you’re saying we have to pretend we don’t know anything, right?”

“That’s how it looks, hm~?”

Even so, facing the student assembly while pretending to know nothing without any prep was utter foolishness. What kind of scheme awaited them? Or rather, was there even a scheme to begin with at all? There was just so much the two didn’t know, but assuming a conspiracy was in play here, they <em>had</em> to come up with some sort of countermeasure.

Yet… with only the knowledge that Alisa was the target, what kind of action could they even take…?

<em>Should I just have Alya skip the student assembly too…?</em> Masachika let that thought float throughout his mind. <em>No, taking our eyes off the whole thing’s dangerous in its own way, huh? And to start with, dodging the matter here only means kicking the problem down the road. Smarter to deal with it now than get blindsided later…</em>

Thinking through it this far, one person connected to this debacle suddenly came to mind.

“Come to think of it…” Masachika uttered.

“What?”

“Ah, no,” yet he quickly shook his head the moment Alisa clung to the words he had let slip from his mouth.

“Tell me. We need any scrap of information we can get right now, no matter how trivial it is.”

“Ngh, hmm…” Masachika hesitated in the face of his partner’s earnest gaze, before reluctantly speaking. “Truth is… Kiryuuin had also said that Nonoa was plotting something for the student assembly…”

“Kiryuuin…? Like, baldi—Sumire-senpai’s cousin?”

“Yeah, <em>that</em> Kiryuuin. Yuusho Kiryuuin.”

Picturing the pretentious schoolmate who’d been doing nothing but pestering him lately, Masachika’s mouth twisted in displeasure.

“Right. Then, let’s ask <em>that</em> Kiryuuin-san about it too,” Alisa said with a swift decision.

“Huuuuh…? Seriously?”

“Why not?”

“No, for starters, it’s like the guy naturally has it out for Nonoa; no way we’re getting anything objective from him here…” Masachika grimaced as if the thought itself offended him, all while Alisa tilted her head slightly with a complicated look.

“I don’t really know him well, but… him aiming to be student council president back in junior high means he’s capable at the very least, right?” she asked.

“Ahhh. Well… he’s a li’l overconfident and doesn’t really care ’bout the means to get what he wants.”

“And if I remember, he had a debate against Sayaka-san and Nonoa-san and lost, if I’m not mistaken?”

“Yep, right.”

“That means, in other words… he knows Nonoa-san as an opponent more than anyone else, doesn’t he?” Alisa reasoned, eliciting a gasp from Masachika. “That sort of instinct—call it intuition, or rather his judgement—might actually be worth trusting, don’t you think?”

“……”

Such a thought had never crossed Masachika’s mind, and all he could do was freeze, eyes wide at Alisa’s unexpected remark.

<em>That’s true… It’s precisely ’cause he’s a villain who’s used to tossin’ malice at others that maybe he’s all the more sensitive to malice directed at himself</em>, he rationalized. <em>Could that… actually be it? But, man…</em>

Nevertheless, Masachika still couldn’t help but feel a strong reflexive sense of aversion to relying on Yuusho. He couldn’t deny the feeling of not wanting to expose someone as dignified as Alisa to someone like that.

<em>And in the first place, getting involved with him now’s nothing but a risk if we’re thinking ’bout the election here… His awful reputation’s definitely dragging down our support if he ends up cooperating with our camp. Hmmmmm~</em>

Seeing Masachika stuck in indecision after turning over every possible angle, Alisa could only shrug.

“Alright. <em>I’ll</em> go talk to him first alone.”

“Huh?”

“The two of you seem to have this heavy history, and you being there means he might not be able to stay calm either.”

“No, that’s…” Masachika instinctively tried to refute her words, only to trail off in the end.

But still, letting Alisa and Yuusho meet one-on-one was the last thing Masachika could accept.

Then again, setting aside all that emotion, thinking about it rationally made Alisa objectively correct: they <em>should</em> hear Yuusho out.

In that case, Masachika’s own conclusion was already set…

“Nnghhh<em>~</em>,” he squirmed. “Fine! But I’m coming too.”

“Eh? But like I said, you being there means he’ll—”

“I’ll leave the talking to you. I’ll just listen in from the back.”

“What, are you <em>that</em> worried about leaving it to me?” Alisa sulked with a displeased tone, prompting Masachika to shake his head.

“No, it’s not that I’m not confident with leaving it to you… I’m worried because I don’t know what <em>he</em> might do.”

“He’s not going to do anything, is he…?”

“No, no… No way he won’t,” Masachika immediately denied with a tone as if it were the most obvious thing in the world. “This is the guy who caused that whole mess at the cultural festival, y’know? It’s better to stay on your guard, ain’t it?”

“……”

“I see what you’re trying to say, but…” yet Alisa could only tilt her head, unconvinced.

“What?”

“Considering what happened at the cultural festival… Kiryuuin-san is the type who works from behind the scenes without dirtying his own hands, am I right? If we think about it that way, the chances of him doing something to me directly are pretty low, aren’t they…? There’s no reason for him to.”

“……”

Hearing Alisa’s calm and logical point, Masachika finally realized that the “rational judgment” he thought he was making was, in fact, driven purely by emotion.

<em>Ah, I see… I was…</em>

To put it simply, unable to stand the thought of leaving Alisa alone with Yuusho. The both of them alone in some room: just imagining that caused a thick fog of unease to spread deep within his chest.

<em>No, that’s just… ’cause I’m worried about her. And because I just hate the damn guy. Ugh, but I guess that means I’m not one to talk, huh…?</em>

Realizing that he was guilty of the same kind of thing he had just accused Yuusho of, namely how “the guy naturally has it out for Nonoa so there was no way they’d get an objective opinion,” Masachika reflected on himself with a heavy sigh.

Nevertheless, that didn’t change the fact that things that were hard to accept were simply hard to accept…

“…Oh well, just in case—”

“You’re overprotective, hm?” Alisa cut him off with a sigh mixed with exasperation and resignation at Masachika’s terse insistence on accompanying her, before putting on a faint sulkish expression as she then lightly pursed her lips while toying with a strand of her hair.

Seeing that, all Masachika could do was hesitate for a few seconds before averting his eyes.

“…I <em>am</em> worried, y’know?” he started off with that murmur. “Not about whether you can handle it… but about <em>you</em>.”

In face of the small fragment of his true feelings that Masachika let slip, Alisa’s eyes shot out wide. The fingers that played with her hair paused, and she stared at him as he looked away, his lips twitching awkwardly.

Quickly then casting her gaze downward…

“You <em>are</em> overprotective, aren’t you?” she repeated the same words, but with a different nuance this time as she started to fiddle with her hair again, her eyes darting all over the place.

【Not that I hate it though,】 she then added in a tiny whisper.

<em>Ugh.</em>

It had been a while since Alisa’s blunt murmurs of affection hit him square in the chest like that, and all Masachika could feel was his breath caught in that moment.

Still, pretending not to notice as she glanced at him over and over shyly, he forced a calm front.

“Then, that settles it. I’ll send Kiryuuin a message since thanks to the fact I’ve still got his contact from our time in the junior high student council; I’ll handle calling him over. As for the time and place…”

“…How about the student council room during lunch?”

“<em>Mm</em>… Sounds good. Let’s go with that, shall we?”

⋆⋅☆⋅⋆

“Hey, sorry ’bout that,” came an almost insincere voice. “Had a teacher stop me for a moment.”

““……””

It was now lunch break, and Yuusho had arrived at the student council room about five minutes later than the agreed time. Masachika greeted him with a glare, all the while Alisa shot him a look of 80% bewilderedness and 20% judgement.

Though he did technically offer an apology along with an excuse, his expression showed no remorse at all, completely at odds with his words.

<em>Maybe he showed up late out of spite for calling him here so suddenly?</em>

“Your baldness is spreading again, ain’t it?” Masachika lobbed that question straight at him, as if to substitute his criticism of how tardy he was.

“…It’s all Sumire-neesan’s choice, okay?” Yuusho explained after making a face akin to swallowing something unpleasant. “I have to keep my head shaved for at least the rest of the year.”

“Ahhh, so you’re getting it shaved regularly, huh? No wonder the patchy spots of hair keep shifting about a bit. Ain’t got a clue to how you’re shavin’ it here,” Masachika shrugged, earning a shot of unspoken protest from Yuusho before the latter composed himself.

“So then? What do you want?” Yuusho snapped in a slightly curt tone, throwing the two a suave side-glance that dripped with pretension.

“I’m just the messenger here,” Masachika responded by flicking his gaze toward Alisa with a dismissive gesture. “The one who wants to talk’s my partner here.”

Having said that, Masachika folded his arms as he leaned his back against the wall, making it clear that he intended to simply watch. As Yuusho responded with a slight shrug, Alisa stepped forward.

“Nice to meet you… I suppose that’s appropriate since it’s our first proper meeting, Kiryuuin-san?”

“Ahhh, that’s right,” Yuusho lifted his chin with an insufferably arrogant gesture. “Is calling you Kujou-san alright here?”

“Yes, ” Alisa responded while maintaining her serious expression. “I’d like you to be frank with me: what do you mean when you say that Miyamae-san is plotting something for the upcoming student assembly concerning the piano and light music clubs?”

Faced with her question, Yuusho tilted his head lightly before casting a quick glance toward Masachika.

“You hear that from Kuze?” he then spoke in a needlessly self-important tone. “Oh well, he’s the one who refused to listen to me at all, though~”

A blatant jab, one Masachika couldn’t help but twist his mouth over, with Alisa also frowning in tow.

“Isn’t it only natural that he wouldn’t want to listen if <em>that’s</em> the tone you take?” she pointed out clearly.

Her exceedingly straightforward remark made Yuusho’s eyes widen in surprise, and he instinctively ran his right hand through his hair as if dispirited… only to realize that there wasn’t any to grab at in the first place, letting his shoulders droop a little sadly.

“That’s true, I was clearly a bit rude. I apologize,” he then bowed his head honestly.

This time, it was Masachika who widened his eyes in surprise.

With Yuusho not saying anything more on that point, he passively dropped his theatrical demeanor and looked straight at Alisa.

“So, this is about Miyamae, was it? It’s simple enough. The whole matter clearly screams that someone’s malice is at work.”

“…Specifically?” Alisa probed, prompting Yuusho to snap his index finger upright as he continued.

“First, the fact our vice president’s trying to recklessly convene a student assembly. Given her personality, she’d normally try to persuade the light music club before ever resorting to something like <em>this</em>. Unless, of course, someone talked her into it, hm?”

“I see… and the next?”

Prompted, Yuusho then raised his middle finger alongside his index.

“Negative chatter about the piano club has been heating up again out of nowhere. For gossip that had been finally calming down to flare up again now? I can only guess that someone’s obviously stirring the pot.”

Having said that, he then lightly spread his arms and shrugged.

“Then, you’ve got the whole origin of this matter lying with none other than the light music club: the club Miyamae belongs to… And, well, the rest’s just my intuition.”

“Your intuition…” Alisa softly repeated his words, frowning for a while in thought. “…In other words, you mean you’re convinced this student assembly is all Nonoa-san’s doing, based on your own experience of being up against her back in junior high, right?”

“Well, that’s about it?” Yuusho confirmed. “Judging from how you’re acting, it looks like you’ve happened to get some solid information from someone other than me, huh now?”

“…Something like that.”

Though slipping past Yuusho’s own probing with ease, Alisa still wore a difficult expression as she deliberated.

“If you’re sitting there and wondering, ‘why would Miyamae do such a thing~?’, it’s pointless,” Yuusho shrugged and said. “That woman acts when she decides to act. Trying to analyze her reasoning using ordinary standards or common sense is utterly meaningless.”

His words pushed Alisa’s eyes to waver as she looked toward Masachika. Her look held a desperate desire for denial, and yet… all Masachika did was merely lower his own gaze slightly, offering Yuusho a form of reluctant agreement.

“!”

Precisely reading Masachika’s intent, Alisa clenched her teeth sharply and lowered her face.

“Alya…” Masachika muttered.

He stepped away from the wall to stand beside his partner as he saw her struggling. But just as he hesitantly reached out to place a hand on her shoulder, Alisa suddenly snapped her head up and looked straight at Yuusho.

“So then. Assuming Nonoa-san <em>is</em> plotting something, what exactly do you intend to do about it?”

“Stop her, obviously. The piano club’s survival is at stake here. And besides, the idea of letting that woman have her way puts a bad taste in my mouth.”

“How do you intend to specifically? You don’t actually know what Nonoa-san is planning either, do you?”

“I unfortunately have no obligation to be that frank with you, do I?” Yuusho turned his face away, refusing to answer this time.

“Then let’s make a deal, shall we?” Alisa offered, smoothly shifting from a position of questioning to that of negotiation as if she expected his response. “If you tell us your plan, I’ll share the info <em>we’ve</em> gathered. And if you’re intending to do something during the student assembly, then I’ll offer certain arrangements as a member of the hosting student council.”

Faced with her fluid words, Yuusho could only look over at her in mild surprise, staring directly at her face before letting out a wry smile.

“In other words, me refusing here means you might bar me from attending the student assembly altogether, is that it now?”

“Eh? No, that’s not—”

“<em>Heh</em>… You’d better learn to wield those sort of threats if you intend to become student council president someday~,” he then swapped the nature of his smile as he saw Alisa immediately let her gaze wander in distress, sweeping Masachika—who had quietly dropped his outreached hand—into view. “Sure. Regarding this student assembly, let’s form a united front, shall we?”

⋆⋅☆⋅⋆

“So, if push comes to shove, you’re saying you’ll provoke your former piano club members to bring the entire student assembly to a halt, right, Kiryuuin-san?”

“Sure, but that’s only possible if they’re moved in the first place, riiight~? And that… all depends on our vice prez, doesn’t it?”

“We should probably inform Prez and our vice president quietly then,” Alisa suggested. “Especially Sarashina-senpai. She’ll likely physically block a disruption onstage if we don’t lay the groundwork beforehand.”

“<em>Ahaha</em>, that’s easy to imagine alright…” Masachika uttered.

It was currently the afternoon after school. Following Yuusho’s insistence that they could never be sure which students on campus might be under Nonoa’s influence, the five of them—Masachika, Alisa, Yuki, Ayano, and Yuusho—had gathered at a karaoke parlor two stations away from school, sharing the information each of them had as they held a strategy meeting.

Given there were only a few days left before the student assembly would convene, they’d gathered in a rush. Still, with these five sitting face-to-face, the discussion moved quickly.

“I want to identify whatever tricks may be set in motion as much as possible… So could you wait until the last possible moment to intervene?” Alisa asked.

“Well, I want to expose Miyamae’s shenanigans as much as you do, so that’s what I’ll do,” Yuusho agreed. “But depending on how things go, I may move without waiting for a signal.”

“That’s fine with us.”

“Then I’ll head home on the day of,” Yuki concluded. “Masachika-kun will go to the wind ensemble clubroom, and we’ll all meet again afterward.”

“Yeah… Got it.”

With the meeting itself being wrapped up after just over thirty minutes, Masachika and the rest all let out small sighs as they sipped at their juices, all the while Yuusho suddenly queued up a song.

“…No, what’re you tryna sing for here?”

“Coming all the way here to karaoke and not singing at least one song’s pretty lame, isn’t it~?” he reasoned as he ignored Masachika’s lazy stare, adjusting the volume before standing up and beginning to sing in English. “Wow…..So jeallyyyy.”

<em>A Western song too… Damn, he’s insanely good at it as well.</em>

Letting out a small, exasperated sigh in the face of an utter narcissist enjoying himself alone, Masachika confirmed that Yuusho’s attention was fully fixed on the karaoke screen before turning to Ayano.

“So… you doing okay, Ayano?” he asked, with the girl who had, as always, erased her presence and blended into the air, jumping lightly at his question before tilting her head.

“…I am not experiencing any particular physical discomfort,” she stated, keeping her ever so formal tone.

“No, that’s not what I meant… Like, doing something you’re not used to, like spying? I was just wondering if you were holding up mentally here,” Masachika explained, yet he regretted asking something so foolish the moment he did.

There was no way she was okay. No matter how distant this childhood friend of his was from Nonoa, the very act of tricking someone and getting information out of them like that was something even she would feel hurt over.

And yet…

“There is no problem,” Ayano spoke. “I was merely driven by a sense of duty for a task that only I could accomplish.”

“……”

Her emotionless tone gave no insight to any of her true feelings. Glancing at his sister and seeing her raise her shoulders with a “it can’t be helped” gesture, Masachika chose not to press further.

<em>Oh well, I’ll make sure to properly thank her once this is all over</em>, he instead conveyed to Yuki with his eyes.

<em>Yeah, that’s right</em>, she silently replied with her own before picking up the remote. “Well then, since we’re already here, why don’t we sing a little too? But really… Kiryuuin-san’s actually good at singing…”

“The way he sings with that whole full of himself act really gets under my skin, though.”

“Eh? We’re singing now? Does that mean me too?” Alisa asked.

“Of course, it only really starts when our diva sings here.”

“Who’re you calling a diva… Geez.”

“I’m for some reason getting a li’l hungry here too; should we just get dinner here while we’re at it?”

“Ah~, sounds good. Let’s do that.”

And so, the five of them ended up enjoying a karaoke sesh for about an hour while having dinner before finally parting ways.

⋆⋅☆⋅⋆

“Right then, I’ll take a taxi home.”

“Okay then, see you tomorrow.”

“Thank you all for today. Please be careful on your way home.”

“Mm-hmm.”

“See you tomorrow.”

After watching Yuusho head to the main street and Yuki and Ayano get into the Suou family car, Masachika turned to Alisa.

“Right then, we should head back too,” he suggested.

“Yes.”

But as the two began walking side by side toward the station, Alisa suddenly let a small smile slip, making him blink in surprise.

“Alya? What’s up?”

“Ah, <em>mm</em>…” she shook her head a little sheepishly, probably not even fully conscious that she had smiled, pausing for a moment before speaking. “Um, hearing that Nonoa-san was trying to set up a trap for me had made me really uneasy, but… being able to work with Yuki-san and Ayano-san about it makes me feel relieved, I guess.”

Her thoughts seemed to settle all the more as she talked, and Alisa continued with a gentle smile.

“As for me, I <em>do</em> want to become student council president, and I absolutely don’t want to lose to Yuki-san… But at the same time, I guess it’s also true that I never really want to fight if I could help it.”

“Hmm…”

Though it was only temporary, Alisa appeared to be happy that she was cooperating with someone she considered both a rival and a friend. It was something that Masachika felt like agreeing with as well, almost responding with a calm nod.

“Yeah, true,” he wanted to say, however…

“…Hate to rain on your parade here, but you really shouldn’t be letting your guard down too much, y’know?”

“Hm? Wh-why?” Alisa blinked repeatedly as she turned toward him, caught off guard by his unexpected warning.

Looking away from her gaze, Masachika could only face forward as he spoke slowly.

“It’s true: we’ve decided to work together to deal with whatever Nonoa seems to be plotting up, but… that doesn’t make us allies. No, we’re still rivals at the core even with this whole thing. Yuki understands that too.”

“What… do you mean?”

“I mean…”

And with that, time returns to the day of the student assembly.
