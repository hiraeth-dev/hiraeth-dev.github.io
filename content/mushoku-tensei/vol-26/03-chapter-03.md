<p>WHEN I GOT BACK, Eris and the others were holding up just fine. They&#x27;d lost me and the Superd warriors, and Cliff and Elinalise were still not there, but things had stabilized. Ghislaine dashed about the battlefield, practically on all fours. The sweet spot of the Fighting God&#x27;s fist was high —he was a tall guy—so she kept low to the ground to avoid the range of the blast of wind it generated, slashing at him from the front, side, and back to assist the others. Ghislaine didn&#x27;t have enough offensive power, but from the way the Fighting God was waving his arms around, she was giving him a hard time.</p>
<p>Sylphie&#x27;s presence was similarly massive. The situation called for faster recovery, so her unvoiced healing magic was a perfect fit. When the Fighting God sent Zanoba or Cliff flying, she&#x27;d be at their side and have them back on their feet in an instant. Sylphie had been off active duty for a long time now, so I thought she&#x27;d struggle to keep up with the physical demand, but she was single-handedly doing as much healing as Cliff and I had combined.</p>
<p>I suppose I should give special mention to Isolde. She was out in front of the rest, deflecting all the Fighting God&#x27;s attacks against her and striking back with her own. She moved with grace and precision. Her technique made the Fighting God&#x27;s violent blows—any one of which could&#x27;ve been lethal—look like a child&#x27;s tantrum.</p>
<p>Obviously, she wasn&#x27;t going to defeat him like that. No matter how many flowing strikes she hit back at the Fighting God with or how many times she sliced into his arms or his legs, she didn&#x27;t do any damage. In a one-on-one battle, she might have given him a good fight, but she&#x27;d never have won. At some point, she&#x27;d get tired, and then it&#x27;d be over.</p>
<p>When it came to buying time until I made it back, though, her presence was worth its weight in gold.</p>
<p>&quot;Sorry for taking so long!&quot; I called out to Sylphie.</p>
<p>&quot;Rudy…! All of you, fall back!&quot; At her signal, they all put distance between themselves and the Fighting God.</p>
<p>&quot;Well, well.&quot; Badigadi didn&#x27;t try to follow them, didn&#x27;t even spare them a glance. His eyes were on me.</p>
<p>We were about the same size. The Fighting God Armor was about two and a half meters tall. The Magic Armor was about three meters tall. Those few dozen centimeters meant I was only a little taller, and because I&#x27;d stopped to stand about ten meters away from him, it wasn&#x27;t enough to look down on him.</p>
<p>&quot;That must be the Magic Armor that my dear sister bestowed upon the Dragon God in recognition of his worth!&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Um…&quot; I said hesitantly. &quot;You saw the Version Once back at the port town, right?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Did I now?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Yup, only then you smashed it to pieces in a single blow.&quot;</p>
<p>I thought back on that single blow. I&#x27;d taken the full impact because I&#x27;d overestimated my defenses, but still, it was amazing that Eris and Ruijerd were still alive after taking hits like that. It had to be the difference having a battle aura made to your defensive strength…though in that case, I had to worry about Cliff. He hadn&#x27;t taken a direct punch, but it wasn&#x27;t like he could cloak himself in the protection of a battle aura in the event that he did.</p>
<p>&quot;You said &#x27;Version One&#x27;. I take that to mean that this armor is different?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;That&#x27;s for me to know and you to find out,&quot; I said, looking around me. The others were standing around watching me from afar. Though there was a solid distance between us, they could still get caught in the crossfire.</p>
<p>Oh, right. Sylphie was going around the remaining wounded.</p>
<p>I&#x27;d trust her with Cliff for now.</p>
<p>&quot;Shall we get this show on the road?&quot;</p>
<p>The battle commenced.</p>
<p>My Stone Cannon stood in for a starting bell to kick off the fight. I backed away, shooting Stone Cannons, with Badigadi hot on my heels. I was following the same pattern as my fight with Orsted: retreat and fire off Stone Cannons indiscriminately. Honestly, I&#x27;d thought it&#x27;d be a struggle to pull off that much, but the lumbering bulk of the Version Zero moved like a dream when I fed mana into the King Dragon Blade. This was what gravity manipulation felt like, huh? I felt like I could do anything with the sword in my hand—only, seeing as I hadn&#x27;t trained with it, I&#x27;d settle right now for making myself lighter.</p>
<p>&quot;Fwahahahaha! I&#x27;ve had worse mosquito bites!&quot; The Fighting God came after me, splintering trees and gouging holes in the earth. It was plain to see that my attacks weren&#x27;t doing much. Even at this close range, the Fighting God didn&#x27;t bother to dodge or deflect the Stone Cannons. They plunged straight into him then fell out of his back. He hadn&#x27;t taken any damage, or at least, that was how it looked. Orsted had said Stone Cannon might work, but it was doing nothing.</p>
<p>&quot;Turning tail and running away, are you?&quot; Badigadi called after me.</p>
<p>I had some other things in mind. Once I&#x27;d led him where I wanted him, I aimed the shotgun at his feet, leaving a giant crater in the ground under his next step.</p>
<p>Badigadi stumbled. For a split second, he lost his balance. That&#x27;s when I closed.</p>
<p>&quot;Guh?!&quot;</p>
<p>I emptied the Gatling gun, then struck with the sword mounted on the back of my right hand. It sliced through the armor like a hot knife through butter, exposing black flesh.</p>
<p>&quot;Shotgun Trigger!&quot; I bellowed, then fired. The blast sent Badigadi&#x27;s arm flying clean off.</p>
<p>&quot;Fwahahaha! Payback time!&quot;</p>
<p>I&#x27;d taken four hits myself. The Magic Armor shook all over from each impact, and I slid around ten meters back.</p>
<p>But I was okay. I&#x27;d managed to withstand a direct hit.</p>
<p>&quot;Oof!&quot; Straight away, I wheeled around and went to retrieve Badigadi&#x27;s arm. It pulsated inside the golden gauntlet. I tossed it away.</p>
<p>&quot;Fwahahaha! Futile, all futile!&quot; Badigadi&#x27;s arm regrew, popping right out of his shoulder…like a certain race of green aliens. &quot;Hmph.&quot;</p>
<p>Oh, but it hadn&#x27;t been futile. His new arm was bare—it wasn&#x27;t covered by the armor.</p>
<p>&quot;Oho! That&#x27;s how we&#x27;re doing this, are we? You&#x27;ve thought this through, boy!&quot;</p>
<p>Now, on the ground where I&#x27;d thrown the arm, there was a magic circle ready to go. The arm and the armor lay there and didn&#x27;t start regenerating. Maybe it was my imagination, but Badigadi looked like he&#x27;d shrunk.</p>
<p>I hadn&#x27;t thought this through. I&#x27;d just guessed.</p>
<p>The power of the Fighting God Armor made Badigadi faster and stronger, but he wasn&#x27;t that much faster than the master sword fighters I&#x27;d met. Orsted could outpace him, and maybe Alec, too. He was way faster than me usually, but I was wearing the Magic Armor; he wasn&#x27;t so fast that I couldn&#x27;t handle him. I was making good use of the experience I&#x27;d built up training with Orsted and Eris.</p>
<p>It was his extremely strong defense and durability that were frustrating me. The Fighting God Armor was hard. It might have been tougher than the Magic Armor—it was at least tough enough that Eris and the others might scratch it if they attacked with all their strength, but they had no chance of cutting off his head or limbs. The armor restored itself instantly and went on fighting like nothing had happened. Under normal circumstances, the person inside the armor would go on taking damage… but Immortal Demon King Badigadi couldn&#x27;t die.</p>
<p>The damage from Eris&#x27;s sword and Ruijerd&#x27;s spear should have penetrated the armor, but they did nothing against Badigadi. Whether they slashed, stabbed, or struck him, Badigadi healed instantly. Before long, the attackers would grow tired and then, they&#x27;d be easy pickings for the destructive power of those six arms.</p>
<p>How were we going to beat him? Atofe had given me a hint. The figure of Immortal Demon King Atofe, rising again to stand against her foes no matter how many times she was struck down, stood for all the demon kings of the Demon Continent as a symbol of fear. There were two ways to beat her. The first was to cut all her limbs off and seal them so that they didn&#x27;t regenerate. This was the most conventional method. She&#x27;d been brought down twice this way in the past. It would take some heavy-duty barrier magic if you wanted to keep her sealed away for centuries, but simply fencing her in with advanced-level magic barriers would prevent her from coming back.</p>
<p>The second way was to make her admit defeat. Immortal Demon King Atofe had her own rules that she usually fought by, and when she saw she&#x27;d lost by those rules, she&#x27;d surrender. Unfortunately, I didn&#x27;t see Badigadi giving up so easily. I decided we&#x27;d go with the first method.</p>
<p>I had Cliff set up barrier magic circles throughout the forest in advance. They&#x27;d activate when I threw one of Badigadi&#x27;s limbs into them. I&#x27;d been worried that they wouldn&#x27;t work on the Fighting God Armor, but it hadn&#x27;t been a problem. My plan was to use this defense-negating sword to cut through the armor, tear his arms off, then seal them away. When I&#x27;d taken care of all six arms, I&#x27;d make Badigadi admit defeat. What I really wanted was to seal his whole body…but without Cliff, I couldn&#x27;t use that Magic Circle.</p>
<p>&quot;Gaaaah!&quot; I bellowed, charging. I no longer cared about dealing damage. I didn&#x27;t know how much longer the Version Zero would keep running at full power. Maybe the King Dragon Blade had extended its uptime a little, but it still could power down at any moment. I had to keep this battle short and decisive.</p>
<p>&quot;Come, then, champion!&quot; The Fighting God spread his arms wide as I closed in on him, sending his fist swinging around to meet me. I thrust my sword out in response to the incoming fist, meaning to counter. The agility of those six arms was mind-boggling, but after the last battle, I sort of knew what to expect. Today, I was on form.</p>
<p>I could dodge them.</p>
<p>I cut into his lower left arm, jamming the barrel of the shotgun into the incision as I did so. I fired, and the arm was blasted off. Unfortunately, I couldn&#x27;t help leaving myself open for a split second. As the arm tore away, another fist plowed into me, and I went shooting back.</p>
<p>&quot;Ngh!&quot;</p>
<p>A crack split the front of the Magic Armor. In the end, it couldn&#x27;t withstand the fists of the Fighting God. Still, I could ignore the unarmored arms.</p>
<p>Four more to go. My armor only had to last until I&#x27;d taken them all off.</p>
<p>I started. Something else had caught my eye.</p>
<p>The barrier. In our last exchange of blows, the aftermath of the battle had scraped the magic circle off the ground. It had happened so easily I couldn&#x27;t believe it hadn&#x27;t occurred to me. Some magic circles remained, but I didn&#x27;t know how many or where they were.</p>
<p>&quot;Damnit!&quot; I choked out, then quickly hurled the arm as far as I could throw it. It sailed off down into the Earthwyrm Ravine. It had taken Atofe a while to recover after she&#x27;d been cut into pieces. By putting distance between Badigadi and his severed limbs, he wouldn&#x27;t be able to restore them right away. They&#x27;d come back eventually… But it had to be worth something to make that feat take longer.</p>
<p>Hm? For some reason, the armor wasn&#x27;t regenerating. Did it become nonoperative when separated from the wearer, even if it wasn&#x27;t sealed? Had long years of disuse even caused the Fighting Armor to lose some of its performance? Lousy excuse for regeneration, if so.</p>
<p>Or was this all Badigadi&#x27;s ruse?</p>
<p>Never mind. Now wasn&#x27;t the time for pointless worrying. His failure to regenerate was an opportunity. The only thing to think about was cutting off the rest of those arms.</p>
<p>&quot;Grr…&quot; Badigadi grumbled, but no new arm sprouted from the stump. Instead, the arm he&#x27;d regenerated earlier retracted into the armor like a turtle going into its shell.</p>
<p>&quot;Eh?!&quot;</p>
<p><em>What&#x27;s going on?</em></p>
<p>In a second, two of the remaining four arms disappeared into the body of the armor, gauntlets and all. Then, the last two arms grew thicker. There was a straining metal noise as they swelled.</p>
<p>Okay, the last two arms were bigger now. Too difficult to cut? No, I could manage it. This sword hit more effectively against tougher targets. The Fighting God could strengthen his arms and reinforce his defenses all he liked and it wouldn&#x27;t matter.</p>
<p>Making a split-second decision, I kicked off and charged at the Fighting God. An alarm bell was ringing somewhere in my mind, but I ignored it. Whatever Badigadi did now, I&#x27;d already laid my trump card on the table. My mana was getting closer to zero with every passing moment. Unless I attacked, I couldn&#x27;t win.</p>
<p>&quot;Gaaaah!&quot;</p>
<p>I yelled. Shouting helped generate power. I squashed my fear and uncertainty and let a little courage show on my face. It was the little bit of courage I needed to plunge in further. It enabled me to charge in, like Eris did, toward victory.</p>
<p>I rammed into the Fighting God. He absorbed the impact, but he stumbled. I swung my right fist. It bit into his left arm and out the other side. Then, I stabbed with my left, pushing the shotgun into the gash.</p>
<p>&quot;Shotgun Trigger!&quot; I shouted, and Badigadi&#x27;s Fighting Armorencased arm went flying. Only, I was knocked back too. Badigadi had punched me with his one remaining arm. The front surface of my armor was smashed to pieces, and the impact had penetrated through it. I felt like my body was going to be crushed flat. I fell backward.</p>
<p>&quot;Hurgh…rghh…&quot; Blood bubbled up from my throat. My heart was screaming, <em>Not yet!</em> But it was no good. He&#x27;d outplayed me. I hadn&#x27;t seen it coming. He&#x27;d merged his arms together to make his attacks stronger. Lose an arm, win the war, so to speak. He&#x27;d driven his fist into the crack in the Magic Armor with unerring precision and shattered it. Why hadn&#x27;t that occurred to me when I saw his arms get thicker? Was I a moron?</p>
<p><em>No, you&#x27;re fine. This is okay. You&#x27;d have done the same thing even if you&#x27;d seen it coming.</em></p>
<p>I&#x27;d taken some heavy damage…but it wasn&#x27;t over yet. There was still one arm left.</p>
<p>Then, to my shock, I realized I wasn&#x27;t moving. The Magic Armor felt heavy. And my wounds weren&#x27;t healing. Right next to where my body sat in the Magic Armor, there was a part that was essentially its core. Breaking that would cause the armor to lose mobility. It wouldn&#x27;t stop moving entirely, mind. It wasn&#x27;t so simple a machine. Still, it would be massively limited. Fatal in a battle like this.</p>
<p>Panicking, I sent out mana. Right, I had mana left. I could still move, I wasn&#x27;t fully drained. I could fight. So why wasn&#x27;t I moving?</p>
<p>&quot;A good plan, and such spirit…&quot; While I lay there, unable to move, Badigadi approached. &quot;And you gave me a good fight. Farewell, Rudeus. Not even Laplace dreamed of such an elaborate scheme.&quot;</p>
<p>He raised his cannon-like fist above his head, then brought it swinging—</p>
<p>&quot;Guh!&quot; Badigadi grunted as something red rammed into him from the side. Whatever-it was slashed at his arm, severing it at the shoulder and sending it flying off through the air.</p>
<p>&quot;Grr!&quot;</p>
<p>There was only one red something in this forest: Eris. Could it be? Had she followed me? Had she been at my side this whole time—here with me?</p>
<p>I didn&#x27;t know. No other support arrived. Eris had come charging in alone. A moment later, I noticed something off. It was her sword. Eris&#x27;s sword was broken. The famed Phoenix Dragon Sword had snapped off at the hilt. Of course. Up until now, any damage we&#x27;d done to the outside of the armor hadn&#x27;t been enough to cut Badigadi&#x27;s arms off. She&#x27;d forced her sword to cut straight through. <em>Any</em> sword would break.</p>
<p>&quot;Gyaaaah!&quot; Her sword broken, Eris refused to stop. She faced down the Fighting God as though she hadn&#x27;t even noticed, howling all the way. Looking around, I saw she wasn&#x27;t alone. Following her out of the forest, one after another, came Sylphie, Ruijerd, Ghislaine, and Isolde. But they were too slow.</p>
<p>&quot;Only a fool would dare to stand in my way alone!&quot; Badigadi said, advancing on Eris. There was no one to protect her. Without hesitation, I activated the escape circuits and ejected from the Magic Armor. Strapped on its back was a sword.</p>
<p>The moment I gripped the hilt, a feeling of awesome, unlimited power rushed through my body. The sword contained a staggering volume of mana. It was a sword crafted specifically to make a person into a hero. I poured yet more mana into it, trying to wring out every last drop I had left.</p>
<p>I doubted I could use it myself. But a member of my family was standing in front of me with a broken sword raised before her, growling with her teeth bared, and all to protect me.</p>
<p>I threw the blade to her.</p>
<p>&quot;Eris!&quot; The magic sword flew in a lazy curve through the air to where Eris turned and caught it.</p>
<p>It was the King Dragon Blade Kajukut, renowned for being the most powerful sword in the world, and the greatest of the magic swords forged by the great demon bladesmith Julian Harisco.</p>
<p>Eris raised it high above her head.</p>
<p>&quot;Gyaaaaaah!&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Hrm? Surely not…!&quot;</p>
<p>She brought the blade down. In the split second before it made contact, the Fighting God&#x27;s body floated into the air.</p>
<p>The blade sliced into him with a flash of light that blotted out my vision. The ensuing explosion blew out my eardrums.</p>
<p>We were at the mercy of an overwhelming force.</p>
<p>Destruction blossomed.</p>
<p>There was no blast wave, no shock wave. Only silence. The destruction was all directed inward. All that mana driven into the blade became a sphere that enveloped Badigadi. The sword had released not only Eris&#x27;s strength, but all the mana I had put into it.</p>
<p>I looked into the sphere of mana as it destroyed everything within it while it rose slowly into the air. I watched cracks appear in the Fighting God Armor. It fell to pieces. Badigadi was compressed within the mass of energy and then faded into dust without even a murmur.</p>
<p>I think he struggled, but there was nothing he could do. The Fighting God Armor didn&#x27;t function, and Badigadi was crushed even as he tried to regenerate.</p>

<p>The sphere vanished. The broken remains of the armor fell into the Earthwyrm Ravine. There were some clangs and clatters as they fell away, bouncing off the cliff walls, and the King Dragon Blade, still impaled in the metal, went with them.</p>
<p>All that remained was the armor. All traces of Badigadi&#x27;s black flesh had vanished.</p>
<p>For a good while, I stared. I stared both at the now silent ravine, and after the Fighting God Armor that had since vanished from sight. Badigadi&#x27;s arm lay nearby. It didn&#x27;t move or even twitch. It definitely didn&#x27;t look like it was about to start regenerating. Was he dead? Did we win, or was something else coming? Any moment now, was I going to hear a &quot;Fwahaha!&quot; as Badigadi returned?</p>
<p>I stared down into the ravine, wondering. Nothing happened. I saw no sign of anything coming back up. All that was left was silence.</p>
<p>I heard a thud from behind me and turned. Eris had fallen to her knees, her face pale. I rushed over to her. Was she injured? Had Badigadi got in a counterstrike? I reached out my hand, thinking I had to heal her right away, but then I sank to my knees as well.</p>
<p>&quot;Ohh…&quot; It wasn&#x27;t an injury. I recognized this sensation, as well as the look of Eris&#x27;s face. This was mana drain. Sucking out all my mana hadn&#x27;t been enough to satiate the King Dragon Blade Kajakut. It had used up Eris&#x27;s mana as well. Eris probably hadn&#x27;t experienced mana drain since childhood. She sank back, blinking.</p>
<p>&quot;Eris.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Rudeus…&quot; she said. &quot;Your hair&#x27;s gotten whiter.&quot;</p>
<p>I put a hand to my head, although I couldn&#x27;t tell myself. But returning her regard, I saw that one lock of her hair had turned white as well, like she&#x27;d put a streak in it.</p>
<p>&quot;Yours too, Eris.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Huh… Then I guess we match,&quot; she said, then toppled forward. She hadn&#x27;t fainted. She was only weak after using up all her strength. I wanted to fall on top of her, but I held myself firmly back.</p>
<p>&quot;Rudy!&quot; Sylphie was peering at us, worry in her eyes. She wasn&#x27;t alone, either. Ruijerd, Ghislaine, Isolde…they were all here.</p>
<p>&quot;Sylphie, where&#x27;s Cliff?!&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Um, well, someone else healed his wounds, then Zanoba and Dohga carried him back to the village. All of us here came straight after you, but then I didn&#x27;t want to get in the way, so I hesitated… But Eris ran in alone huh?&quot; Sylphie had laid a hand on Eris&#x27;s prone body, and now she looked puzzled. She&#x27;d probably cast a healing spell on sheer reflex. But Eris wasn&#x27;t wounded, so she didn&#x27;t get up.</p>
<p>&quot;I think it&#x27;s mana drain. That sword consumes the mana of whoever wields it.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Oh. All right, then.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Anyway, Sylphie, those arms over there—get them to an undamaged magic circle. Then get Eris back to the village. I need you to tell Sir Orsted what happened, then bring Cliff here.&quot;</p>
<p>I stood up. The Version Zero was in ruins, and my mana was practically exhausted…but I could still move. I didn&#x27;t know how much time it would take Badigadi to restore himself. After being crushed by that much mana, the way he&#x27;d disappeared looked like he&#x27;d been annihilated. And that was practically an understatement. The arms showed no signs of starting to regenerate, so I wanted to believe we had a while. Maybe that was naive. Wishful thinking. Most importantly, the Version Zero was destroyed, and the Version One was gone, too. I was on the verge of mana drain and Cliff, who could cast barrier magic, wasn&#x27;t here. Badigadi had fallen into the ravine, and we had no way of sealing him. If we went down there in this state and found him waiting for us, our chances of victory were close to nonexistent. There&#x27;d be nothing for it but to ask Orsted to take to the field. I wanted to get through this without him using any mana, but I might not have a choice.</p>
<p>I wasn&#x27;t strong enough.</p>
<p>Still, I&#x27;d driven Badigadi into a tight spot. I&#x27;d done everything I could. Whether Badigadi was up and about down there in the ravine, I didn&#x27;t know, but surely I&#x27;d brought him as low as it was possible for him to go.</p>
<p>I felt disgust at my own weakness.</p>
<p>&quot;Ruijerd, Ghislaine, and you too, Isolde. Come with me, please.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Rudy? Where are you going?&quot;</p>
<p>I thought I&#x27;d done everything I could, but there was still something else I had to finish. Even with my mana close to exhausted, I had to do it.</p>
<p>&quot;I&#x27;m going after Geese.&quot;</p>
<p>***</p>
<p>We found him straight away. Next to no effort. I didn&#x27;t even have to use what little mana I had left, it was that easy. The moment we crossed the ravine and entered the flame-blackened forest, there, in the shadow cast by the charred remains of a great tree, we found Geese lying on the ground. His whole body was charred dark by terrible burns. When I&#x27;d cast Flashover, it had burnt the forest and him with it. When I first saw him, I thought he was dead. He was so still he looked like a black boulder. But luckily Ruijerd had found him first, and he&#x27;d used his third eye to probe further. Geese wasn&#x27;t dead.</p>
<p>&quot;Geese,&quot; I said.</p>
<p>&quot;Hey, boss.&quot;</p>
<p>He wasn&#x27;t dead, but it was clear he would be soon, and I wasn&#x27;t about to heal him. I was here to do the opposite…although I didn&#x27;t want to finish him off straight away, either.</p>
<p>&quot;Heh heh. Water magic, earth magic, the Magic Armor… I thought up ways to counter all of &#x27;em, only for this to be what gets me. Didn&#x27;t know you had a knack for fire magic too, boss. Never once saw you use it.&quot;</p>
<p>Geese had all sorts of items on him. He wore a blue vest with a brown band around his middle and something that looked like chainmail. It was hard to tell now, when they were practically burnt to a crisp, but they were presumably precautions against all kinds of magic. I guessed it hadn&#x27;t been the Fighting God Armor&#x27;s power that allowed him to survive Electric back in the Third City of Heirulil.</p>
<p>&quot;And now you&#x27;re here, boss, which I reckon must mean my final plan fizzled…&quot; Geese&#x27;s scorched cheeks twisted. His final plan? I guess that depended on whether you called sending Badigadi out alone counted as a &quot;plan.&quot;</p>

  <img src="/novels/mushoku-tensei/vol-26/insert-05.webp" alt="Illustration" loading="lazy" />

<p>&quot;If any of &#x27;em, the Sword God or the North God, the Ogre God, the Abyssal King…if there&#x27;d been just one more, things might&#x27;ve been different… None of &#x27;em listened to me, y&#x27;know.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Well, none of them were the best listeners,&quot; I replied. Geese seemed half delirious.</p>
<p>&quot;Hah, you&#x27;re one to talk. Eris, Atofe. That Ghislaine I see over there? You&#x27;re surrounded by folks who don&#x27;t know how to listen, yerself.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Yeah, well… I guess I was lucky.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Nah, that ain&#x27;t it. It&#x27;s &#x27;cause you did things the right way. Told &#x27;em what was goin&#x27; on, won their trust, and then worked hard to make honest allies of &#x27;em all. And that&#x27;s why, when push came to shove, they listened right and followed your orders right.&quot;</p>
<p>He might&#x27;ve had a point. Atofe and the Ogre God, who I&#x27;d only joined forces with because I&#x27;d had to at the time, hardly listened to me at all. Sandor and Dohga were exceptions, but Ariel fit the pattern. If I hadn&#x27;t been able to build trust with everyone, there would have been more who refused to listen to me.</p>
<p>&quot;Turns out just twistin&#x27; things to make a reason to fight, scroungin&#x27; people together, stirring &#x27;em up, then sneakin&#x27; around nudging &#x27;em behind their backs just doesn&#x27;t work…&quot;</p>
<p>Neither the Sword God nor the North God had followed Geese&#x27;s instructions. At the end of the day, they put their own interests first. That was why I was alive.</p>
<p>&quot;I thought I knew what was what, but I was wrong. Still, I thought I&#x27;d pull it off somehow. Only then it turned the one who really didn&#x27;t know… wasn&#x27;t me.&quot; Geese laughed. &quot;The Man-God, y&#x27;know. He was throwin&#x27; a <em>real</em> tantrum a little while ago. &#x27;Why?! Why?! It&#x27;s all <em>your</em> fault! Ya stupid ape!&#x27;&quot; Geese flashed a careless, mocking smile. &quot;I mean, what&#x27;d he expect? Who&#x27;s gonna lend an honest hand to a bastard who tricks and sneers at the folks that work their hardest for him?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Then…does that mean you cut corners too, Geese?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Is that what you think, eh? Was it that easy for you? I gave it my all, I&#x27;ll have you know.&quot; Geese coughed and something black like soot trickled</p>
<p>from his mouth. &quot;See, Badigadi and I, we&#x27;re unusually softhearted. Who else&#x27;d help out a guy who screams at his allies for bein&#x27; useless even now? Softies is who.&quot;</p>
<p>The black soot we saw now was like a representation of Geese&#x27;s soul. I could tell he was growing weaker.</p>
<p>&quot;Thing is though, boss. Even after all that, the Man-God saved me. Yeah, he did some nasty stuff to me too, but when you add it all up, he saved me.&quot;</p>
<p>When I didn&#x27;t say anything, Geese went on. &quot;You wouldn&#x27;t get that, eh, boss? You can do anythin&#x27;, go anywhere in the world, all on your own. You wouldn&#x27;t get what it&#x27;s like when you just can&#x27;t do nothin&#x27;.&quot;</p>
<p>I <em>did</em> get it. Or at least, I thought I did. I understood what it was like not to be able to do what everyone else could. Geese was…me. Me as I&#x27;d been long ago, only there was one difference. Back then, I hadn&#x27;t even tried. When I came up against a wall, I just ran away. Geese, on the other hand, really didn&#x27;t have the ability. In this world dominated by monsters and violence, the most important power was the power to fight, and he didn&#x27;t have it. He&#x27;d learned how to do everything else, but he couldn&#x27;t survive.</p>
<p>&quot;No, Geese, you&#x27;re wrong…&quot; I could say he was wrong, but no more than that. I couldn&#x27;t say that I understood. I didn&#x27;t want to give him an answer. All I could do was deny it.</p>
<p>&quot;Heh. Hey, Rudeus. If you&#x27;re gonna tell me I&#x27;m wrong, take some pride in it. You won, y&#x27;know? Ya beat me. The world says it&#x27;s winners who get to be right and losers are wrong. So stand up straight and tell me, &#x27;That&#x27;s wrong, Geese. That ain&#x27;t how it is.&#x27; Then, see, you go on and give me a lecture, seein&#x27; as I&#x27;m about to die. &#x27;Ya shoulda done it like this, ya shoulda stuck with me and never gone over to the Man-God.&#x27; That sorta thing.&quot; With that, the strength seemed to go out of him, and he said, his face empty, &quot;Me, Badigadi, and the Abyssal King are all gone. The Man-God&#x27;s got no one left who&#x27;ll go outta their way to help him out. He lost. Ain&#x27;t no one left in this world who can mess with Rudeus Greyrat. In fact, he said himself that if this was a dud, there&#x27;d be nothin&#x27; he could do to ya. So I reckon he&#x27;ll quiet down, at least &#x27;til you take him out. Count on it: He&#x27;ll be sneakin&#x27; about behind the scenes, though.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;You&#x27;re joking, right?&quot; I said, butting it without thinking. Geese didn&#x27;t smile.</p>
<p>&quot;If that&#x27;s how ya wanna think, I ain&#x27;t gonna stop you. I&#x27;m only guessing that he&#x27;ll quiet down, no more. Go on wavin&#x27; that &#x27;Down with the Man-God!&#x27; flag if you like. It&#x27;ll be bad for him but not for you, eh?&quot;</p>
<p>I wasn&#x27;t laughing either.</p>
<p>&quot;Hey, now, what&#x27;s the glum look for? You&#x27;re Paul&#x27;s son, aren&#x27;t ya? Paul&#x27;d look a bit more cheerful if he were around. Maybe not right before he died, though. He really got older while I wasn&#x27;t lookin&#x27;… But anyways, take some pride in this already! Rejoice a bit, even if it don&#x27;t last. How d&#x27;ya think I feel when you don&#x27;t? Makes me look like a real moron, after I went all over the world gettin&#x27; the Sword God and the North God and the Ogre God to join me, then getting &#x27;em fired up like, &#x27;Let&#x27;s take him down!&#x27; only for it to all fall to pieces. All &#x27;cause I couldn&#x27;t control &#x27;em. At the end I took a risk and sent Badi. Look where that got me. At least remember me as a strong opponent, yeah? That&#x27;s how I wanna be remembered.&quot; Before I knew it, Geese was crying. Tears were streaming down his soot-stained face. When I saw that, I knew for sure that he hadn&#x27;t held anything back.</p>
<p>&quot;Okay. You were strong, Geese. True, I&#x27;m standing here now, but if just one thing had gone awry, I&#x27;m pretty sure our places would be reversed. This was the hardest, most brutal fight of my life.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Heh… Heh heh. Cheers, Rudeus.&quot;</p>
<p>He had been strong, all right. It had taken me a year to defeat him. For a whole year, I&#x27;d prepared…not to mention everything I&#x27;d brought to the fight that had been built up over a much longer period. No one could call him weak after all that.</p>
<p>&quot;Geese.&quot; Suddenly, Ghislaine stepped forward. She looked down at Geese. Her bangs hid her face so that I couldn&#x27;t read her expression.</p>
<p>&quot;Hey, Ghislaine. Been a while.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;It has.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I&#x27;m headin&#x27; on.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Yes. Say hello to Paul for me.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Gotcha… Maybe, when it&#x27;s your time, we can get a drink. I wanna see Paul get drunk again, then stickin&#x27; his face into ya chest and makin&#x27; Zenith sulk…&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Zenith won&#x27;t be going anywhere for a while yet. My time will probably come first.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Heh, yeah, I know… Anyway…&#x27;til we all…meet…ag…&quot;</p>
<p>Geese fell still. Just like that, something in him was gone, suddenly, even though he hadn&#x27;t finished talking.</p>
<p>Ghislaine&#x27;s ears twitched, then her tail drooped. &quot;He&#x27;s dead,&quot; she said.</p>
<p>Dead. Geese was dead.</p>
<p>***</p>
<p>I beat Geese. I could think that now, but as I&#x27;d expected, it didn&#x27;t make me happy. I was aware I was in shock. There was something about seeing someone I knew die in front of me that I couldn&#x27;t process. He was my enemy, and I&#x27;d known I had to take him down…but it wasn&#x27;t like I&#x27;d loathed Geese with every fiber of my being or anything. If we&#x27;d lost the battle and they&#x27;d killed Eris or someone close to me, I might have ended up loathing him. I might have felt like this was justice. <em>I took down the bastard. I got my revenge.</em> That sort of thing. But…</p>
<p>I couldn&#x27;t deal with it. All I knew was that I was able to numbly ruminate the way I was because I hadn&#x27;t lost a single person in the battle who was important to me. I&#x27;d met my victory conditions. I&#x27;d wiped out the disciples and kept Orsted in reserve. It&#x27;d been a hard fight and there&#x27;d been missteps but despite that, it was a perfect victory—rare, for me. Maybe I was trying to use the way Geese had died to mess it up a bit. Perhaps some part of me thought that, if I&#x27;d been cleverer, Geese might have come back over to my side.</p>
<p>Thinking about it wasn&#x27;t doing any good. At the very least, though, I could take his bones home and make him a grave. Next to Paul&#x27;s. That&#x27;d be good. He&#x27;d said something about being together.</p>
<p>Those were the thoughts running through my head as I watched Geese&#x27;s body burn. Ghislaine stared intently at the cremation proceedings. Maybe it was my imagination, but when it was over and we&#x27;d recovered the bones, her ears and tail seemed limp.</p>
<p>&quot;Let&#x27;s go home.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Yeah.&quot;</p>
<p>We crossed the ravine.</p>
<p>Whatever else aside, it was really over this time. I was worn out. I barely had any mana left; I was physically exhausted. If I lay down I&#x27;d be out like a light. Not that I could afford to sleep until Badigadi was sealed…</p>
<p>I was eager to get back to Sharia, anyway. I wanted to get a good night&#x27;s sleep in my own bed and then wake up to eat. I&#x27;d have rice for breakfast… Yeah—here in the Biheiril Kingdom, they had soy sauce. I could make a perfect tamagokake gohan bowl. When I got back, I&#x27;d eat. I&#x27;d eat until I burst. Then, of course, it&#x27;d be time for some sexy shenanigans. Rudeus the Celibate had died with Geese. Sylphie…or Roxy…or Eris… who to pick? Scratch that, how about all three at once? Eris wouldn&#x27;t like it, but surely it&#x27;d be all right to ask her, just this once. Chances like this didn&#x27;t come along every day, right? Right.</p>
<p>The post-mortem for this battle could wait until later. For now, I&#x27;d forget what Geese had said. Now, it was time to rest. I was exhausted.</p>
<p>&quot;Rudeus.&quot; As I walked, dragging myself wearily along with every step, I heard a voice from behind me. It was Ruijerd. He was walking at the back of our group and had turned to look behind him. Back at the ravine.</p>
<p>&quot;What&#x27;s the matter?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;An enemy.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;What?&quot;</p>
<p>There was a hand clinging to the edge of the ravine. A hand. A hand. Something was climbing up out of the ravine. But what? Never mind, there was no point wasting words on it. The hand shone gold. It wore a golden gauntlet.</p>
<p>&quot;You&#x27;re kidding.&quot;</p>
<p>It was Badigadi.</p>
<p>Surely this was too soon. Wasn&#x27;t it? Thinking back, I&#x27;d thrown a few of his arms into the ravine, then his body after them. His body had appeared to be practically annihilated, but there could have been a few larger chunks here and there…maybe by pulling together the bits that remained, no matter how minute, he&#x27;d been able to fast-track his regeneration. Were immortal demon kings <em>that</em> immortal…?</p>
<p>As we stood frozen, the armor rose out of the ravine. Only, it looked different now. It only had two arms, which was how it had been when I&#x27;d defeated it, but the overall design had changed. The helmet was a different shape, and it was shorter, not even two meters tall. It was also holding a sword. An enormous sword. The world&#x27;s greatest sword, forged from the King Dragon King.</p>
<p><em>No. It isn&#x27;t him. That isn&#x27;t Badigadi.</em></p>
<p>&quot;No matter how desperate he becomes, a hero always rises again and turns the tides. That is what I have achieved!&quot;</p>
<p>That voice. The way he said &quot;hero.&quot; There was no way I could mistake that calling card.</p>
<p>&quot;I am North God Kalman III, Alexander Rybak!&quot;</p>
<p>He was alive. Huh! I&#x27;d really thought he was dead. His body hadn&#x27;t so much as twitched back when I sent him down here, and yet here he was. He&#x27;d survived.</p>
<p>Ah, of course. He had immortal demon blood too. Given time, he could regenerate like Badigadi.</p>
<p>Except—no. A shiver ran up my spine as it all clicked. This was the &quot;final plan&quot; Geese had talked about. Had this been his plan from the start? Or had he changed tack partway through? I&#x27;d thought something was off. I&#x27;d thought it was strange when the armor didn&#x27;t regenerate. He&#x27;d deliberately prevented it from regenerating. Then, Alec had put it on at the bottom of the ravine and restored himself. Maybe yesterday, when Geese was playing dead, he&#x27;d been preparing to drop the Fighting God Armor and part of Badigadi into the ravine and resurrect Alec…</p>
<p>Dammit. There was still more I had to do. There was another battle I had to fight. I was <em>sick</em> of this. Couldn&#x27;t it just be over? Like, give me a break already! An opponent I already beat once coming back for a second round?</p>
<p>Maybe it was my fault. I hadn&#x27;t made sure that Alec was dead. I&#x27;d beaten him and thought that meant I&#x27;d finished him off for good, only to leave him there. I could have burned him, at the very least! But no, I&#x27;d left him there, and now here we were. What else should I have done in that situation, though? What more could I have given than what I already had?</p>
<p>Well…whatever. What was done was done. What was I going to do <em>now</em>? The Version Zero was gone. I had no backup. Ghislaine, Isolde, and Ruijerd, and I remained, with me teetering on the verge of mana drain. I had no weapon and no armor. My hand was empty. There was no hope of winning.</p>
<p>What should I do? How was I supposed to beat North God Kalman III wearing the Fighting God Armor?</p>
<p>Was it time to ask Orsted to step in? As if. What was any of this <em>for</em>?</p>
<p>I had to at least weaken him…but how?</p>
<p>As I gaped at him, Alec looked back at me. He didn&#x27;t seem at all surprised that I was here. It was like he&#x27;d expected me to be waiting for him.</p>
<p>&quot;Rudeus Greyrat…&quot; he said. &quot;I apologize for calling you useless. You are a formidable warrior. I would not have thought it from your appearance, but you are a worthy opponent for me. Thanks to you, I have ascended to a new level of power. You have my gratitude.&quot;</p>
<p>I turned my weary body toward the golden armor. If I ran, he&#x27;d only catch me. I didn&#x27;t even have enough strength to buy time. I&#x27;d go down fighting, then. I&#x27;d struggle with everything I had left. Focusing only on that thought, I stepped forward—</p>
<p>&quot;Uh?&quot; I was sprawled on the ground.</p>
<p>&quot;I am invincible now,&quot; Alec said. It was seeing the other three fallen around me—Ruijerd, Ghislaine, and Isolde—that made me realize Alex had thrown us all down. He&#x27;d knocked us all down in one hit.</p>
<p>&quot;This is my thanks to you for making me stronger, Rudeus. I will let you live.&quot;</p>
<p>Searing pain shot through me at last. My legs were broken. He was too fast. I hadn&#x27;t seen it coming. I hadn&#x27;t had the Eye of Foresight open, but still. I hadn&#x27;t been able to react at all. Neither had any of the other three. The Eye of Foresight wouldn&#x27;t have made any difference. Maybe this was the true power of the Fighting God Armor. When the wearer was stronger, it built upon their strength… Never mind, that wasn&#x27;t right. It wasn&#x27;t like Badigadi had been weak. He was plenty strong too. It was simply that when the wearer changed, the armor&#x27;s performance changed as well. It changed its form to align itself with them… It really was the ultimate armor.</p>
<p>&quot;Farewell,&quot; Alec said, walking away.</p>
<p>There was no time to be shocked. I immediately said a spell to heal the other three. They were unconscious. Near death, but not yet dead. Was this Alec&#x27;s idea of mercy? Damn it all. He still didn&#x27;t take me seriously. But hey, that wasn&#x27;t a bad thing. After healing the other three, I cast Earth Fortress to cover them, then went after Alec. I had no plan for what I&#x27;d do when I caught him. Had Sylphie made it back to the village? What was Orsted going to do? I didn&#x27;t have any answers, but Alec was heading toward people I had to protect. Toward Eris, Sylphie, Norn, and all the Superd. I couldn&#x27;t allow them to be slaughtered. I had no reason not to go after him.</p>
<p>My legs weren&#x27;t cooperating too well. They trembled, refusing to respond how I wanted them to. But even then, I managed to run. I kept going, following the golden armor.</p>
<p>***</p>
<p>The Superd village was too quiet. It was so quiet I wondered when I arrived if everything was already over.</p>
<p>&quot;Why?! Why is there no one here?!&quot; Alec bawled. I passed through the palisade gate and into the village and found it empty. The Superd were gone, as were Julie and Aisha, and Cliff and the others who&#x27;d been carried back here for their wounds. Even Sylphie, who was supposed to have been</p>
<p>relaying my message to Orsted, was gone. Eris, too. There wasn&#x27;t a trace of any of them. All the people had vanished into thin air.</p>
<p>&quot;How can this be?! Isn&#x27;t this what Rudeus was defending?!&quot;</p>
<p>It was. I was defending this place.</p>
<p>This was bizarre. Everyone had been here, right before I left! It had been…how much time had passed? It was about three hours from here to the ravine. I&#x27;d used the Version Zero to get there, and I&#x27;d been in a big hurry, so it had only taken an hour. Then we&#x27;d fought Badigadi, searched for Geese, and come back…so five, maybe six hours? Five or six hours ago, everyone had been here. Being in a hurry I hadn&#x27;t looked around much, but I was sure they&#x27;d all been here.</p>
<p>Except—hold up. Hadn&#x27;t there been…too many people? Hadn&#x27;t there been some people who had no place being here?</p>
<p>&quot;Curses… You had me completely fooled…&quot; Alec turned around. &quot;Rudeus Greyrat!&quot; Fury radiated from him in waves.</p>
<p><em>You&#x27;ve got it wrong. I don&#x27;t know any more than you.</em></p>
<p>Why would I have come chasing after such a dangerous opponent if Orsted wasn&#x27;t even here? That&#x27;d be moronic. I&#x27;d have run away into the forest, counting my blessings that he&#x27;d let me live.</p>
<p>&quot;Orsted and the Superd were never here, were they?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Um, no, the Superd were… You saw Ruijerd before, didn&#x27;t you?&quot; Getting the feeling he might attack at any moment, I backed away. I had no idea what was going on now. Maybe this was all a bad dream. Maybe the Abyssal King had survived or something, and everything since we&#x27;d defeated Badigadi had been a dream.</p>
<p>&quot;I was going to let you live. No longer. If you are so desperate to fight me to the last, I shall grant your wish…&quot;</p>
<p>Crap. What&#x27;s going on? I had to run. I had no reason to fight, I had to run. I was about to turn around—then, ice ran down my spine.</p>
<p>My feet stopped moving. Had Alec done something? No, that wasn&#x27;t it. He was rooted to the spot too.</p>
<p>&quot;Wh-what&#x27;s this, this cold?&quot; He sounded scared. He was looking around wildly. He had the Fighting God Armor. Why was he so scared?</p>
<p>Why?</p>
<p>Well, because it was a curse. A curse to inspire fear. A curse that specifically didn&#x27;t work on me. Only, I could tell that the source of the curse was currently seething with murderous rage, and that rage was tied up in some major trauma for me. <em>That</em> scared me.</p>
<p>That murderous rage took shape as it emerged from the back of the village. Silver hair, and those awful eyes, the whites shining beneath the gold of his irises. He came walking toward us slowly with a terrifying look on his face.</p>
<p>&quot;Rudeus.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Sir Orsted…why…?&quot;</p>
<p>It was Orsted. He had his helmet in one hand and tossed it to me now. I hurried to catch it.</p>
<p>&quot;When Sylphiette informed me what had happened, Cliff Grimor was already close to full mana drain. I saw that he would be unable to seal Badigadi and the Fighting God, so I went and begged another man&#x27;s aid. Hence, my arrival was delayed. Forgive me.&quot;</p>
<p><em>No, not that, I wasn&#x27;t asking why you&#x27;re late. I want to know why there&#x27;s no one here.</em></p>
<p>&quot;This, however… This, I did not anticipate,&quot; Orsted said, then looked at Alec, North God Kalman III, who stood there in the Fighting God Armor. &quot;I shall handle the rest.&quot; He stepped forward, and Alec took a frightened step back. I had no idea what was happening. I only called after Orsted.</p>
<p>&quot;But Sir Orsted, your mana…&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Enough. There has been enough of that,&quot; Orsted said, shaking his head. &quot;I too have made up my mind.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Made up your mind…? About what…?&quot;</p>
<p>He looked at me. His mouth quirked in the thinnest shadow of a smile, then set with almost imperceptible determination. With the most terrifying face in the world, he said, &quot;I wish to see for myself what it is like to fight alongside trusted friends.&quot;</p>
<p>I hadn&#x27;t quite followed the beginning and end of the conversation, but for some reason, his words struck me. I understood he&#x27;d resolved himself to this battle.</p>
<p>&quot;Okay,&quot; I said at last. &quot;Then I&#x27;ll leave you to deal with the rest.&quot; I stepped back. There was nothing more for me to say. I was supposed to be thinking that I couldn&#x27;t allow Orsted to fight, and yet I could feel a little smile tugging at my mouth. I&#x27;d misjudged a little. Not one thing in particular, exactly. It was just that Orsted had let me in more than I&#x27;d thought. He thought of me as his ally on an emotional level, not just in his calculations. He wanted to fight alongside his friends. Not allies. Friends. From now on, he would not be alone, but with me. He wouldn&#x27;t use me, but rather stand at my side. Now I knew we couldn&#x27;t lose. I&#x27;d thought I&#x27;d failed to achieve a goal, but I&#x27;d won something else.</p>
<p>&quot;Now then, North God Kalman III, Alexander Rybak.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;So it&#x27;s you… You&#x27;re Dragon God Orsted.&quot; When Orsted called his name, Alec raised the King Dragon Blade Kajakut. He was wielding both the Fighting God Armor and the King Dragon Blade. They made for a devastatingly powerful combo. He wouldn&#x27;t consider tossing just one of them to the side, would he? Was there <em>anything</em> I could do here?</p>
<p>&quot;Perfect.&quot; Orsted, it seemed, thought differently. As Alec raised his blade, a smile of confidence flashed across his face. That smile was terrifying enough to turn everything around it to ice.</p>
<p>&quot;With both the Fighting God Armor and the King Dragon Blade, there&#x27;ll be no excuses when you lose, will there?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;You—!&quot; Alec was out for blood now. &quot;Do you mock me?!&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I do not.&quot; Orsted put his hands together and then slowly drew them apart. Something emerged from the palm of his left hand: it was a sword, and when I saw it, my knees began to knock together. I&#x27;d only seen that</p>
<p>sword once before. Orsted had only called it the Dragon Blade. All I knew was that it consumed a massive volume of mana.</p>
<p>&quot;My sole desire is to beat you down utterly and to break you.&quot; He held the sword out, pointing it at Alec&#x27;s eyes.</p>
<p>Alec&#x27;s fury rose to the surface, the air crackling with his wish to see Orsted dead. He raised the King Dragon Blade.</p>
<p>&quot;Try it, then!&quot; he shouted.</p>
<p>Dragon God Orsted faced off against North God Alexander in the Fighting God Armor. The true and actual final battle had begun.</p>
<p>***</p>
<p>About ten minutes later, around a quarter of the forest around the Ravine of the Earthwyrm was gone. Amid the now scorched and barren wasteland, scattered with mounds of splintered trees, there knelt a boy missing both his arms. A sword pressed into this throat. The boy looked up at its wielder in blank shock. A man with silver hair and distinctive eyes stared back. He didn&#x27;t have a scratch on him. Looking at him standing there unscathed, you&#x27;d think there hadn&#x27;t been a battle at all. The only clue was a spattering of dirt on his clothing.</p>
<p>&quot;Choose. Become my follower or die.&quot;</p>
<p>The Dragon God against the North God in the Fighting God Armor.</p>
<p>That match-up could have been a truly legendary battle. A pair of opponents of that caliber could have gone down in history for good. Sadly, the actual battle was nothing so grand. It was too devastatingly one-sided for that. Honestly? It was tough for me to put it into words. I watched it, getting caught in the melee and narrowly escaping death as I did so, but they moved so fast I could hardly see anything. Even with the Eye of Foresight, I couldn&#x27;t tell what either of them were doing. The only thing I saw for sure was that Orsted always had the upper hand. I could tell that every time Alec tried to turn things around, Orsted smashed him into utter submission. He was completely outmatched. Even with the Fighting God Armor and the King Dragon Blade, he couldn&#x27;t touch a hair on Orsted&#x27;s</p>
<p>head. The armor was smashed to pieces, which were now beginning to regenerate, but they had detached from Alec&#x27;s body. The King Dragon Blade lay on the ground nearby along with his arm.</p>

<p>Alec had long since lost all will to fight. He looked up at Orsted, his face petrified with fear. Tears streamed from his defeated eyes, and his mouth hung half open. The boy who&#x27;d boasted of becoming a hero was gone. In his place was a whimpering puppy, its spirit entirely broken.</p>
<p>After a long silence, at last he spoke. &quot;I will become your follower,&quot; he said.</p>
