<p><strong>F</strong>OR THE THIRD TIME, I met Orsted at the cottage on the outskirts of Sharia.</p>
<p>&quot;…and that is how Ariel wound up convincing Lord Perugius to help her, and how we discovered we wouldn&#x27;t be able to access the magic circles located within the Asuran border.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Hm.&quot; Orsted grinned.</p>
<p>It looks so sinister when he does that. But I guess it&#x27;s probably just his normal smile.</p>
<p>&quot;I see. You did well, then.&quot; If the wrinkle in his brow was any indication, he was plotting something even as he complimented me.</p>
<p>Nah, his face always looks like that.</p>
<p>&quot;But I would advise you to never return to the Library Labyrinth again. That Demon King holds grudges.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Urk… Okay, I understand.&quot;</p>
<p>Sadly, I wasn&#x27;t spared a reprimand for my failure there. Orsted even scowled at me as he said it. No, actually—I think he was just exasperated with me. His expression seemed to say, &quot;How in the world did you end up causing so much trouble in a library, of all places?&quot; But it wasn&#x27;t really my fault now, was it? I had no idea Luke was going to bawl like a baby over a book.</p>
<p>&quot;I don&#x27;t suppose there&#x27;s any way they would accept an apology?&quot; I asked.</p>
<p>&quot;That would be futile. Demon Kings don&#x27;t operate on common sense.&quot;</p>
<p>From my scant interaction with this Demon King, it seemed like they might actually be willing to hear me out, but Orsted didn&#x27;t seem to think so. Admittedly, we&#x27;d made a bit of a mess. We wrecked a number of bookshelves as we escaped, even though we had no other choice. I wanted to express how sorry I was at least, but I was giving up on ever going back, as Orsted advised. Perhaps never showing my face there again was the best apology I could give.</p>
<p>Nah, in the case of that Demon King, the best thing I can do is probably write in my diary. Daily updates would be impossible, but I would try to do it as much as possible.</p>
<p>Anyway, that aside…</p>
<p>&quot;What do you think about this teleportation circle business?&quot; I asked.</p>
<p>The group had all suspected my involvement for a moment. They came to their senses immediately after, but that was still enough to put a seed of doubt in their heads—to make them think I was hiding something.</p>
<p>&quot;The Man-God&#x27;s work, I am sure. Seems like his first attempt to thwart us failed.&quot; Orsted nodded to himself, confident in his explanation.</p>
<p>He was in unusually good spirits. He kept mumbling something like, &quot;Just one more person…&quot; I had no idea what he meant, but would be grateful for any enlightenment.</p>
<p>&quot;If you don&#x27;t mind, could you fill me in on what you mean by the Man-God failing?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Hmph. Indeed.&quot; Orsted adjusted his posture and glared at me, eyes molten. If he scowled any harder, they might start glowing and shooting laser beams at me. Pew, pew! &quot;Perugius confirmed that these teleportation circles of his are unusable, yes?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Correct, boss-man sir.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Boss-man sir…?&quot; Orsted paused before continuing, &quot;There are not many of these circles within Asura&#x27;s borders. Most were placed</p>
<p>so that royals and nobles could escape if they ever found themselves backed into a corner. Among them, several are already nonfunctional, and those are the ones Perugius has made use of.&quot;</p>
<p>Huh, interesting. So they&#x27;re like a secret escape route for the royal family.</p>
<p>&quot;Thus, you have your answer,&quot; he said.</p>
<p>I see. So that&#x27;s my answer… Like hell it is! That&#x27;s no answer at all!</p>
<p>&quot;What do you mean by that?&quot; I demanded, falling on my knees and bowing my head. &quot;Please explain! I need something more concrete to work with here!&quot;</p>
<p>Orsted scowled menacingly.</p>
<p>Okay, not menacingly. That&#x27;s just how his face always looks.</p>
<p>&quot;Simply put, these teleportation circles are in places where your average civilian cannot happen upon them. Many are protected by soldiers. Someone who can go in and destroy them must be someone of respectable authority, either a noble or a high-ranking aristocrat.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Okay, I get where you&#x27;re going with this. And?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;…Use your head a little.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Yes, sir.&quot;</p>
<p>Okay, let&#x27;s go over this. The culprit, who was either a royal or high-ranking aristocrat with the authority to enter a restricted area, had suddenly cut off their own lifeline by destroying all the circles that acted as escape routes for them. Not to mention these were already non-functioning circles that Perugius might use to travel. The possibility that the Man-God orchestrated all this seemed astronomically high. A normal citizen had no reason to destroy a magic circle. That meant one of his apostles was either a royal or</p>
<p>someone in a position to manipulate the royal family. The most likely candidates for that role were…</p>
<p>&quot;First Prince Grabel or High Minister Darius. One of them is the Man-God&#x27;s apostle?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Indeed. The spread of those circles across the kingdom implicates the involvement of High Minister Darius, since his private soldiers are scattered throughout the nation.&quot;</p>
<p>Ooh, now I am starting to see the bigger picture! I had no idea he had a private army dispersed throughout Asura, but it makes sense!</p>
<p>&quot;So we can be reasonably sure High Minister Darius is the Man-God&#x27;s apostle, then?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Yes. There is a possibility it might be the first prince, but that changes nothing. We&#x27;ll have to kill them both regardless.&quot;</p>
<p>Well, the first prince is Ariel&#x27;s enemy, so that makes sense… But does that justify us murdering a prince? Guess it doesn&#x27;t matter. If Orsted says it needs doing, I&#x27;ll have to do it.</p>
<p>&quot;That means there is just one apostle left unaccounted for,&quot; said Orsted.</p>
<p>&quot;Just one? That means you are certain Luke is an apostle now?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;There can be no doubt.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;What about Ariel?&quot; I asked. &quot;Is she a possibility?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;No.&quot;</p>
<p>Okay, come on. Enough with the vague answers. Exasperated, I said, &quot;And what do you base that on?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;There are certain people the Man-God cannot manipulate.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Okay, so, uh…how can you tell that she&#x27;s one of them?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;…Intuition. Based on many years of experience,&quot; said Orsted. Intuition, huh…</p>
<p>He&#x27;d paused briefly before he answered, which meant he was certain Ariel wasn&#x27;t an apostle but couldn&#x27;t tell me his real reasoning. I decided not to pry any further. There were more important questions on my mind.</p>
<p>&quot;What happens if your intuition turns out to be wrong and she is an apostle?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;If that should happen, I will take responsibility and dispose of her myself.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Dispose of,&quot; as in &quot;kill&quot;? That was harsh, especially considering how close we&#x27;d grown over the past few weeks, including the incident where I&#x27;d walked in on her while she was changing. But if Orsted was willing to stake that much on her not being the Man-God&#x27;s apostle, I should probably trust him.</p>
<p>Hm. In that case, maybe I should share information about him with Ariel? His curse didn&#x27;t seem to have as much of an effect on her, and if she wasn&#x27;t one of the Man-God&#x27;s apostles, it might be better to disclose everything to her. That way, she could work with us to keep an eye on Luke.</p>
<p>Nah, best not to do that. Like Sylphie, she trusted him implicitly. She would never believe that he would work for her destruction. And Luke was only doing what he thought was best for her. Bringing up the Man-God would be kicking the hornet&#x27;s nest. Luke wasn&#x27;t Ariel&#x27;s enemy. Being manipulated by the Man-God hadn&#x27;t changed his allegiance. He was only doing things that sounded like a good idea to him, despite the fact that they were anything but.</p>
<p>Right now, Orsted considered him a mere spy who observed my actions and reported them to the Man-God. He wouldn&#x27;t do anything to directly harm Ariel. However, he might end up acting on the Man-God&#x27;s advice to do something that seemed like it would help Ariel on the surface but would ultimately lead to her doom. That&#x27;s what truly</p>
<p>made him dangerous. I could understand Orsted&#x27;s instinctive desire to kill him.</p>
<p>&quot;Sir Orsted,&quot; I said suddenly.</p>
<p>&quot;What?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;There is something I would like to clear with you, just to be on the safe side, regarding how I should approach our battle with the Man-God. Do you mind if I pick your brain?&quot;</p>
<p>His forehead wrinkled. &quot;Hm? All right.&quot;</p>
<p>I started outlining his war with the Man-God.</p>
<p>First, we knew the Man-God could see the future. His view of it was both expansive and precise. He also had the ability to manipulate people to change the course of that future. However, he could not see events relating to Orsted. The Dragon God&#x27;s secret arts were stronger than the Man-God&#x27;s prescient vision. Whenever Orsted got involved in an affair, the Man-God would see a false reflection of the future. Thus, he knew Orsted was involved whenever he sensed something slightly amiss or the future experienced a dramatic shift, but he couldn&#x27;t see exactly how Orsted had brought about those changes. All he could do was guess. If he couldn&#x27;t work out Orsted&#x27;s objectives or what he had planned, the Man-God couldn&#x27;t respond accordingly and influence the future in a way that benefited him.</p>
<p>Orsted&#x27;s curse meant none of the Man-God&#x27;s apostles had been able to get close enough to spy on his activities, so his movements had gone undetected. At the same time, that curse also limited the scope of what he could accomplish. It was only now, after I had joined him, that he had more options at his disposal.</p>
<p>As far as the Man-God was concerned, I was currently an invisible pawn on the chess board. But if I took concrete action, he could suss out what Orsted was planning. That was why I was keeping my movements discreet, so I could avoid showing my hand to Luke, who was acting as the Man-God&#x27;s eyes and ears. I also withheld information from Sylphie and Ariel, since I knew how much they trusted him and that they would answer whatever questions he asked.</p>
<p>They say you can&#x27;t put doors over people&#x27;s mouths, so I&#x27;ve tried not to say anything to anyone, far as I can help it.</p>
<p>I planned to keep Orsted&#x27;s aims and actions as secret as possible. That might make me look suspicious to the others, as it had this time, but this would lead us to victory for sure. We would keep our intentions under wraps while defeating the Man-God&#x27;s apostles as we worked to achieve our objectives. I would serve Orsted for the rest of my life, and a hundred years from now, he would come out on top.</p>
<p>&quot;…and that&#x27;s my interpretation of the situation. Is that correct?&quot; &quot;Yes. It is.&quot; Orsted nodded.</p>
<p>In that case, everything I had done up until now was technically correct. Perugius had called me weak, but we were on the path toward our goal. For now, we knew that Luke and Darius were the most likely candidates to be the Man-God&#x27;s apostles. That left one other.</p>
<p>&quot;I wonder who the last person is,&quot; I said.</p>
<p>&quot;I know not. But judging by the Man-God&#x27;s past patterns, it is highly likely to be someone extremely skilled at either martial arts or magic.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Someone skilled at martial arts or magic…&quot;</p>
<p>Uh, he did say it wouldn&#x27;t be anyone in my family, right? Which means Eris and Sylphie are out of the running, fortunately.</p>
<p>Come to think of it, my future self&#x27;s diary mentioned a North Emperor and Water God in Asura Kingdom. Ariel also mentioned the first prince was employing a North Emperor.</p>
<p>&quot;Could it be the North Emperor or Water God?&quot; I asked.</p>
<p>&quot;Auber and Reida, hm? Yes, there is a good chance. When you leave for Asura Kingdom, be wary.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;You won&#x27;t be coming along?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I will be tailing behind you, of course, but we will not be operating together.&quot;</p>
<p>The way he said &quot;tailing behind you&quot; sounded sinister, as if he was going to shadow me like a puppet master. Well, that means I can consult him if anything crops up. It&#x27;s not so bad.</p>
<p>&quot;All right,&quot; I said. &quot;In that case, Luke, Darius, Auber, and Reida are the ones I should watch out for.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Indeed. You may kill Darius, Auber, or Reida if you like. As for Luke…keep an eye on the situation and use your judgment. If necessary, dispose of him.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;You want me to decide whether to kill any of them?&quot; I gaped.</p>
<p>&quot;Yes. I leave it to your discretion.&quot;</p>
<p>Did he seriously think I was someone capable of making those kinds of decisions? Sorry, silly question. Of course he did. I hadn&#x27;t shown any hesitation when I attacked him and tried to claim his life, after all.</p>
<p>&quot;Well,&quot; I said, &quot;what shall we do until it&#x27;s time to depart?&quot;</p>
<p>Orsted shrugged. &quot;Make preparations.&quot;</p>
<p>Preparations, right… But what does that mean? &quot;What should I be preparing, exactly?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;First, get your equipment ready. You will likely be facing the Man-God&#x27;s apostles in battle while you&#x27;re in Asura. With your strength, I am sure you will have no trouble, but it would be wise to bring some form of protection.&quot; He turned and looked out of the cottage, where my Magic Armor lay in shambles. Zanoba was currently repairing it, but there was nowhere for us to store it in the city, so we had left it here. &quot;That thing doesn&#x27;t measure up to the armor of the Fighting God, but it is still a spectacular piece of work. I am sure you must have labored hard to create it.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Well, yes…but we did get quite a bit of advice from the Man-God on its construction.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Oh? Then he dug his own grave. What do you call it?&quot;</p>
<p>I blinked at him. &quot;Call what?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;The armor.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Oh. Magic Armor.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I see… What an uninspired name. Shall I give you a new one for it? Let&#x27;s see…&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;No,&quot; I said, cutting him off, &quot;but thank you.&quot;</p>
<p>Orsted narrowed his eyes and chuckled. His smile was as unsettling as ever. Our taste in names (or lack thereof) aside, I wondered how Zanoba and Cliff would take it if they knew someone as supremely powerful as the Dragon God Orsted had praised their creation.</p>
<p>&quot;If you plan to continue using that thing in the future, you should consider improving it. It currently drains you of all your mana in a single battle.&quot;</p>
<p>I frowned. &quot;But even if we were to make a smaller, more efficient version, it wouldn&#x27;t be finished in only two weeks.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Then we&#x27;ll have to shelve that idea for another time,&quot; Orsted said, stroking his chin.</p>
<p>Wonder if he&#x27;d be willing to lend a helping hand. In which case, I guess the Dragon God Society logo will end up being slapped on it.</p>
<p>&quot;Not being able to use Battle Aura certainly is inconvenient,&quot; Orsted murmured. &quot;For now, I&#x27;ll see if I can&#x27;t prepare a few magic items for you to use.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Oh, that would be great. Thank you.&quot;</p>
<p>So Orsted was going to provide me with not just the best working environment and pay, but the best equipment as well? Damn. Makes sense though. He did set me up with this robe too. The difference between him and the Man-God&#x27;s inscrutable, completely hands-off approach was day and night.</p>
<p>&quot;Speaking of which… I&#x27;ve been hearing a lot about the Fighting God&#x27;s armor lately. What is it, exactly?&quot; I asked.</p>
<p>&quot;Demon-Dragon King Laplace&#x27;s greatest masterpiece, and also his worst failure.&quot;</p>
<p>Laplace&#x27;s masterpiece? So he&#x27;s the one who made it, huh?</p>
<p>&quot;The armor itself glows golden with magical power and bestows immeasurable strength upon its wearer. However, the mana it contains is so great that it has given the armor a mind of its own. It assumes control over the wearer, forcing them to fight until they die. It&#x27;s a cursed armor.&quot;</p>
<p>Cursed armor, huh? Guess the dragonfolk have a knack for making those kinds of things. Laplace had made all manner of cursed items, from the Superd&#x27;s spears to this golden armor… Nothing he&#x27;d created was any good.</p>
<p>&quot;Having said that,&quot; Orsted continued, &quot;the armor is currently sleeping deep in the middle of the Ringus Sea.&quot;</p>
<p>Orsted seemed to know anything and everything. It made him a truly convenient resource. Nonetheless, I couldn&#x27;t depend on him for everything; I had to find some things I could do independently. Sadly, there was only about two weeks till we were set to leave. There wasn&#x27;t much I could do.</p>
<p>I couldn&#x27;t get complacent simply because I was Orsted&#x27;s subordinate. He was a bit too aloof about matters. Or, more precisely, he seemed to think he could always try again if the first attempt failed. Perhaps he aimed to develop magic that could allow him to return to the past after reading my future self&#x27;s dairy. Or maybe he&#x27;d already experienced a time slip like that himself.</p>
<p>Come to think of it, he once said something along the lines of &quot;trying again next time&quot;, and as soon as the words left his mouth, he made this awkward face as if he realized he&#x27;d slipped up.</p>
<p>Maybe he&#x27;d been through these time slips not once, or twice, but a number of times now. I had no idea why he would keep that under wraps, but since he hadn&#x27;t mentioned it, he probably wouldn&#x27;t answer me even if I asked.</p>
<p>But even if Orsted could simply do things over again next time, there was no next time for me. You only get one life to live…or so I&#x27;d like to say, but that probably wasn&#x27;t the most convincing coming from me, given my experience with reincarnation. Even so, after talking to my future self, watching his last moments, and reading his diary, I could sense how full of regrets he was. I couldn&#x27;t just bank on wiping my slate clean and starting over if I messed up. Or rather, I felt like I would be betraying the person I&#x27;d been up till now if I kept up that mentality.</p>
<p>Which is why I need to put everything I have into this.</p>
<p>But how, specifically?</p>
<p>Of course, I could hone my fighting and magical skills, but I didn&#x27;t think that more practice would suddenly make me that much stronger. Were that possible, I would happily work out a strict regimen to boost my abilities, but it wasn&#x27;t. On top of that, anything new I achieved in only two weeks of preparation would be unreliable and half-baked. It was better to continue building on the abilities I already had.</p>
<p>Aside from that, I decided to set aside time to run some mock battles. I&#x27;d felt like something was lacking for a while now. Practice and training were important, but nothing could replace testing the techniques I&#x27;d learned in battle. Sparring, as one might say if this was boxing. Or exhibition matches, if you preferred fighting game terminology.</p>
<p>My sparring partner would be Eris. She was now a Sword King and far better than me at melee combat, so she would put up a good fight. If anything, I was more worried that she wouldn&#x27;t find me a challenge. At least I could use my Quagmire and Deep Mist spells to give her some new battle experience. As much as she boasted of her battle prowess, she was still vulnerable to traps that targeted her weaknesses.</p>
<p>Also, I would ask Zanoba and Cliff to try repairing and improving my Magic Armor. It needed to be smaller and more fuel-efficient, even if that meant reducing its capabilities. They probably couldn&#x27;t get it done in two weeks, but it would serve me in the long run, so I wanted them to start on it now. With Orsted&#x27;s help, we could surely finish the project over the next couple of years. It seemed he&#x27;d also provide any equipment we needed, so I was set on that front, at least.</p>
<p>So that was how I planned to improve my training and gear. Now I just needed to think about what was left. With so little time left, I needed to plan carefully. So I decided to schedule my next two weeks.</p>
<p>First, I would announce my upcoming long absence to the family. It wasn&#x27;t a topic I wanted to broach, in part because I would likely wouldn&#x27;t be around when Roxy went into labor, but I couldn&#x27;t avoid telling them forever.</p>
<p>Next, I needed to get in touch with Cliff. In addition to the improvements I wanted him to make on the Magic Armor, I had one other request for him. Namely, I wanted him to conduct experiments on Orsted&#x27;s curse.</p>
<p>Come to think of it, I wonder if Orsted knows what&#x27;s going on with Zenith.</p>
<p>&quot;By the way, sir…&quot; I started to say.</p>
<p>&quot;What is it?&quot;</p>
<p>I explained Zenith&#x27;s condition to him and brought up the book I discovered in the Library Labyrinth that referred to a Blessed Child who could remove curses.</p>
<p>&quot;That Blessed Child doesn&#x27;t seem to be among the living now,&quot; I said, &quot;but do you know of any other way I might cure her?&quot;</p>
<p>Orsted fell into silent contemplation. After a bit, he finally spoke, his voice gentler than usual. &quot;It&#x27;s true that you may be able to return her to normalcy if you use this Powerless Blessed Child&#x27;s abilities. However, their skills are no substitute for an actual cure. If you try to force her mind back to what it was before, it may backfire and things might go in the opposite direction.&quot;</p>
<p>So there&#x27;s decent odds it&#x27;ll only make her worse, huh?</p>
<p>Then again, after everything she&#x27;d been through, it was a miracle she was even alive. If trying to meddle with her mental state meant possibly making it worse, it was probably wiser to just keep an eye on her for now, instead. Her condition wasn&#x27;t a health concern at the moment.</p>
<p>Guess I just gotta be patient and watch over her.</p>
<p>&quot;All right. Well, with that out of the way, I will begin preparations to set out for Asura Kingdom,&quot; I said.</p>
<p>I cleared up all the questions I had, so all that&#x27;s left is doing the best I can in the time I&#x27;ve got!</p>
<p>***</p>
<p>The following day, we had our family meeting as I planned.</p>
<p>In fact, I feel like we&#x27;ve been having a lot of these family meetings lately.</p>
<p>This time it was to announce my departure for Asura Kingdom. I told them I would be gone for about three to four months to help Ariel.</p>
<p>The reaction to that was indifference.</p>
<p>&quot;Okay, well, good luck with that. Oh, but before you go, I&#x27;d appreciate it if you could make some soil for the garden,&quot; said Aisha. She was more concerned about her dirt than my well-being.</p>
<p>&quot;So Princess Ariel will be dropping out of the academy then…&quot; Norn muttered. Like Aisha, she didn&#x27;t seem too worried about me either. &quot;I wonder if they&#x27;ll have a farewell party…?&quot;</p>
<p>This is…odd. Last time we did this whole song and dance, they seemed a bit more—I don&#x27;t know—emotional? I want a tear-filled farewell again. I want to be able to hug my sobbing sisters and console them by doing my best Terminator impression and saying, &quot;I&#x27;ll be back!&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Hey, Aisha,&quot; I said. &quot;You know, uh, I might not be coming home this time…&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Huh? Every time we do this, you always act like you won&#x27;t be coming home, but then you show back up on our doorstep like it&#x27;s no big deal.&quot;</p>
<p>I&#x27;d barely escaped death each time, but perhaps my little sisters didn&#x27;t see it that way. Or perhaps they were trying to be considerate and not make me worry before I left. Whatever the case, I would do my best out there. I would be satisfied if the two of them could live their lives peacefully in the meantime.</p>
<p>&quot;Besides, this means there&#x27;ll be another woman joining our household,&quot; said Norn.</p>
<p>&quot;Exactly,&quot; Aisha agreed, &quot;which makes us feel silly for even worrying. And this time, you&#x27;ll have Miss Sylphie and Miss Eris going with you. That gives us extra peace of mind.&quot;</p>
<p>As if on cue, Eris retreated to her room to begin packing for the journey. Earlier, when I&#x27;d first said where I would be going, she said, &quot;Oh? Then I&#x27;m going too.&quot; She hadn&#x27;t even hesitated.</p>
<p>&quot;Speaking of,&quot; Aisha said, turning to Norn, &quot;who do you think he&#x27;ll bring back this time?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Hard to say for sure. Maybe one of the girls serving Princess Ariel? Miss Ellemoi or Miss Cleane perhaps?&quot;</p>
<p>My two sisters were saying some really rude things, but for the record, I didn&#x27;t intend to take any more wives. For one, I had hardly ever spoken to either Ellemoi or Cleane. I thought of saying so, but on the other hand, I didn&#x27;t really trust the head between my legs.</p>
<p>But I seriously doubt anything like that is going to happen this time. I&#x27;ll have Sylphie and Eris with me, after all.</p>
<p>Exactly. I had been alone in the past couple ventures, which had left me emotionally devastated. I drifted with the currents because I had no one to hold onto. I needed a levee to stop the overflow. Sylphie and Eris would make perfect dams. All I had to do was enlist their help and the floodwaters would recede.</p>
<p>&quot;I will pray for your safety,&quot; said Lilia. She and my mother acted no different than normal.</p>
<p>&quot;Miss Lilia, um, about Lucie…please take good care of her.&quot; Sylphie&#x27;s expression was heavy with guilt.</p>
<p>&quot;Yes, my lady. I will take care of everything while you&#x27;re away.&quot; Lilia bowed her head.</p>
<p>&quot;I know it&#x27;s not good to leave her behind like this, but I just…&quot;</p>
<p>Lilia shook her head. &quot;You need not worry. This is the very reason you have a maid like me here.&quot;</p>
<p>Lucie had started to speak simple words, such as names of family members or pets, like: Mama, Asha, Lala, Oxy, Beebee, Dillo. My heart trembled with emotion, watching how hard she worked to get the words out. She had yet to call me &quot;Dada.&quot; She would say &quot;Rudy&quot; sometimes, but not &quot;Dada.&quot; I hadn&#x27;t spent much time with her lately, so my name would probably be the last one she learned. And now, Sylphie and I were going to leave her behind to go on a trip.</p>

<p>&quot;Yeah, I figured.&quot;</p>
<p>I had experienced that numerous times in my practice battles against Eris. She usually charged at me as she attacked, but she also often kept me barely within range. From that position, she could counter any magic I tried to sling at her, and if I tried to fall back, she would chase me down. I had lost almost thirty times before realizing what she was doing.</p>
<p>&quot;Allow me to quiz you. When you&#x27;re up against an opponent whose area of attack is in front of them, where is the most advantageous position on the field?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Behind the enemy?&quot; I guessed.</p>
<p>Roxy nodded. &quot;Precisely. They&#x27;re going to lunge forward with their attack, so their center of balance is going to be tilted forward. Even if they try to defend their rear with an attack, its power will be severely reduced. If you can withstand that, you&#x27;ll have a chance to counter them. Thus, the key is to slip out of their attack range and flank them!&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Hm, makes sense.&quot;</p>
<p>So it was better to charge forward instead of falling back. The most dangerous move was actually the best path to survival. Roxy&#x27;s brilliance didn&#x27;t surprise me; she wasn&#x27;t my teacher for nothing. As an adventurer, she had probably seen her fair share of such situations. She&#x27;d probably taken down a number of powerful,</p>
<p>demon-like beasts at close range. Calling her a god was no exaggeration.</p>
<p>My eyes sparkled as I stared at my wife, and she awkwardly averted her gaze. &quot;Ahem, well, I&#x27;m sure it will be difficult with Eris as your opponent. I certainly couldn&#x27;t do it. So please don&#x27;t ask me to demonstrate for you.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;No, I am certain that if anyone could do it, you could!&quot; I gushed.</p>
<p>&quot;No, I really couldn&#x27;t! So stop looking at me like you think I&#x27;m some kind of invincible superhuman!&quot;</p>
<p>I&#x27;m not looking at you like that. My eyes are just sparkling because you&#x27;re a goddess.</p>
<p>Regardless, I finally had my answer. I shouldn&#x27;t try to escape my opponent by falling back every time. I needed to charge forward at least occasionally, get the drop on my opponent, and halt their momentum with a counter of my own. That would force them to second-guess themself, to make them doubt their usual method of charging in for the kill and give me the advantage instead. If I could make them think it was a risk to close too much distance between us, that would actually make it so I could fall back and gain the upper hand. Granted, it wouldn&#x27;t be that simple with Eris. I would just have to keep losing to her while cautiously testing my options.</p>
<p>&quot;Ahem.&quot; Roxy cleared her throat, interrupting my thoughts. &quot;Well, Rudy, since your departure is fast approaching, I think it&#x27;s about time you decided on a name for the baby.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Isn&#x27;t thinking up a baby&#x27;s name before going off on an adventure kind of an ill omen?&quot; I said.</p>
<p>&quot;That&#x27;s a superstition born of a human hero&#x27;s story, right? It has nothing to do with the Migurd Tribe.&quot;</p>
<p>Oof, she&#x27;d rejected that outright. But an ill omen was still an ill omen. Still, if my goddess said it was nothing to worry about, there</p>
<p>was no need to buy into the superstition. I would do as my goddess bid me.</p>
<p>&quot;In our village, it&#x27;s the tribe leader&#x27;s job to pick a name,&quot; said Roxy. &quot;And you&#x27;re the leader of our household, right? So hurry up and make your decision.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Are you sure you want me to make that choice all on my own?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Of course. While you&#x27;re gone, I&#x27;ll be lovingly stroking my belly and calling our baby by whatever name you give it. That will bring me some happiness in your absence.&quot;</p>
<p>As she spoke, she caressed her stomach. I cupped my hand over hers and followed her motions. It was strange, thinking how this girl I had known for over a decade now held my baby within her. I&#x27;d experienced this same puzzling sensation before with Sylphie, and now I was again with Roxy. Happiness swelled in the depths of my chest. It was such a pleasant feeling, one I wanted to enjoy again and again.</p>
<p>&quot;Ehehe,&quot; I snickered.</p>
<p>&quot;What is it, Rudy? That laugh sounds just like Sylphie.&quot;</p>
<p>Just like Sylphie, huh?</p>
<p>&quot;Nothing, really. Thinking about how much I like your tummy.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I&#x27;m not as slender as Sylphie, nor as fit and muscular as Eris…but if you still like my body, then you&#x27;re welcome to touch it as much as you want.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Really?&quot; I asked, although I&#x27;d already been doing just that for the past few minutes.</p>
<p>&quot;Half of the baby inside me belongs to you after all.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;What about you? How much of you belongs to me?&quot;</p>
<p>Roxy paused before saying, &quot;Everything, at least on the outside.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;But I can&#x27;t claim the baby inside too?&quot;</p>
<p>She shook her head. &quot;Half of the baby belongs to me. I won&#x27;t budge on that.&quot;</p>
<p>Hm, makes sense. I knew she was wise. That&#x27;s right, a child belongs to both of its parents. And Roxy belongs to me.</p>
<p>&quot;Let&#x27;s see, what should we do about the name…&quot; I mumbled.</p>
<p>&quot;Hm, well, a Migurdian name would be something like… Lola?&quot;</p>
<p>The Migurd sure did seem to like names that started with Ro or Lo, but since our child was only half-Migurdian, there was no reason we had to stick to tradition. &quot;I think it would be best if we took something from our names, Rudeus and Roxy, and combined them,&quot; I said.</p>
<p>&quot;That&#x27;s a good idea. Then… Rodeus? Or Luxy… I don&#x27;t think our names pair up very well.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Nonsense,&quot; I insisted, &quot;we are a perfect match for one another.&quot;</p>
<p>We couldn&#x27;t simply slap our names together like that, though. Maybe we could change a vowel. Start the name off with, say… Re or Le instead.</p>
<p>Re… Rerere…</p>
<p>Oh, crap. That sounded just like old man Rerere from Tensai Bakabon. I could imagine our child humming &quot;Rerere&quot; to themselves as they swept the floor with their bamboo broom. There was nothing wrong with wanting things clean, but that was definitely not what we needed right now.</p>
<p>I liked the sound of the name that Roxy recommended a moment ago—Lola. It made me picture a young woman who was anxious to experience the feverish passion of love. But that wouldn&#x27;t work either. I wanted something more…more Roxy-like. Something that sounded wise and yet endearing at the same time. I loved the way she turned when I called her name, and how she peered up at</p>
<p>me, wearing a perfect poker face as she asked, &quot;Yes? What do you need?&quot; And that was exactly the kinda name I wanted for our child.</p>
<p>Hmm, hmm… Hmm.</p>
<p>La, Li, Lu, Le, Lo…which of them would be the best fitting for her baby?</p>
<p>I got it!</p>
<p>&quot;If it&#x27;s a boy, we&#x27;ll call him Loro, and if it&#x27;s a girl, we&#x27;ll call her Lara. How&#x27;s that?&quot; I proposed.</p>
<p>&quot;A fine idea. Loro and Lara. I like the sound of those names.&quot;</p>
<p>Of course you do! I practically ripped those names straight out of Adventurers of Lolo, after all. With some slight alteration.</p>
<p>&quot;Doesn&#x27;t that make you happy, Loro? Or Lara? Your father was kind enough to decide on a name for you.&quot; Despite having the appearance of a middle schooler, the expression Roxy wore as she cooed at her stomach was like that of the holy mother.</p>
<p>Divine! She&#x27;s utterly divine! Which means our baby will be a goddess&#x27;s child!</p>
<p>&quot;Rudy,&quot; Roxy said, interrupting my thoughts.</p>
<p>&quot;Yes?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I know I acted like I wasn&#x27;t worried at all a few days ago, but…I expect you to come home safe and sound, okay? I want both of us to be able to hold this child together.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Yes, ma&#x27;am!&quot;</p>
<p>She didn&#x27;t have to tell me twice.</p>
<p>Those indulgent days passed by quickly, and soon enough, we had to depart for the kingdom. There were eight of us in the party. Ariel&#x27;s group consisted of Luke, Sylphie, Ellemoi, and Cleane. Then there was Eris, Ghislaine, and me. We had a single carriage, which</p>
<p>required two of our five horses to pull it. Ariel&#x27;s trappings were rather modest for the second princess of a country as great as Asura.</p>
<p>To the outside world, it would look like we were gearing up to sneak into the country. In reality, we planned to access a forbidden teleportation circle to transport ourselves in. Despite the secretive nature of our mission, there was a whole crowd at the entrance of the city waiting to see us off. This group included the vice principal, officers of the student council, the general manager of the Magicians&#x27; Guild, the leader of the magical implement workshop, and a handful of other heads of organizations, along with representatives for nobility and royalty from the Three Magic Nations. They all swooped in one after the other to bid Ariel farewell.</p>
<p>These guys don&#x27;t understand the definition of covert, do they? Just because you&#x27;re not throwing a party doesn&#x27;t mean it&#x27;s okay to gather en masse.</p>
<p>Well, regardless, their presence here was proof that Ariel&#x27;s efforts to make connections in Ranoa had borne fruit. Perhaps a day would come when I would need to make use of those connections myself. Orsted was insanely powerful, but he didn&#x27;t have very good relations with others. I was on my own on that front. I decided to mingle with the others and pay my respects to them.</p>
<p>And thus, we set off for Asura Kingdom.</p>
