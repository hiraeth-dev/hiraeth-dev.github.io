<p><strong>T</strong>HE NEXT DAY, I went out on a date with Zanoba. Our destination was an open plain just to the north of Fort Karon, which also happened to be the most likely site for the coming battle.</p>
<p>Zanoba had delivered his invitation by barging into my room first thing in the morning and announcing, &quot;There&#x27;s somewhere I&#x27;d like to take you.&quot; Since he evidently wanted the details of his plans to be a surprise, I tagged along without asking any questions—only to end up here.</p>
<p>To be fair, my heart was racing at the moment, just not in a pleasant way. This area was contested territory. There was no telling when we might encounter some enemy detachment.</p>
<p>&quot;Hey, are you sure it&#x27;s a good idea for us to be out here?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Hm? Why so jumpy, Master Rudeus?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;We might run into the enemy at any moment, right? Aren&#x27;t they sitting right on our doorstep?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Strange words, coming from a fearless warrior who challenged the Dragon God himself! We can simply annihilate any force we happen to encounter.&quot;</p>
<p>I&#x27;m sorry, did you just call me a fearless warrior? I think that&#x27;s literally the last thing I would call myself. Perhaps you have me confused with my lovely wife, Eris? <p>&quot;In any case,&quot; Zanoba continued, &quot;I highly doubt we&#x27;ll stumble on their scouts here, close enough to be visible from Fort Karon.&quot;</p>

<p>A rolling field spread before us, dotted with patches of tall wild grass and sizable boulders. There were dips and hills in the terrain, but overall, it sloped downward as you moved away from Fort Karon. From our current position, we had to look up at the fort from below. Plus, the nearby river flowed from south to north, so you&#x27;d have to fight the current to make any progress on the water. They really had placed that fort in the perfect spot.</p>
<p>&quot;How do we know they&#x27;re going to position themselves right here?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Because this area is just close enough for the archers&#x27; volleys to reach us.&quot;</p>
<p>The fort looked pretty far away from here, but I had to take Zanoba&#x27;s word for it. It sounded like those archers had impressive range. Of course, our guys would be shooting down at them from the ramparts of the fort, so we&#x27;d still have the advantage there no matter what.</p>
<p>&quot;Accordingly, I would like to alter the terrain here to make it impossible for them to properly array their troops.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Ah, okay. Now I get it.&quot;</p>
<p>If I made the terrain out here difficult to traverse, the enemy would be forced to deploy their forces a little further from the fort. That would leave them in an awkward spot where our archers could still hit them, but their archers couldn&#x27;t fire back. And if I could make it difficult for them to move forward through this area, it would be much easier to pick them off from above as they advanced.</p>
<p>All in all, it was a smart preemptive move.</p>
<p>&quot;Well then, Master Rudeus—have at it, if you please.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Sure thing. What kind of terrain can I get you today?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Okay, one valley coming up…&quot;</p>
<p>In the end, I spent most of the day out in that field, thoroughly reshaping its terrain. I started by opening a number of yawning trenches in the ground—each about ten meters deep, five meters long, and twenty meters wide. Then I covered some of them with a thin &#x27;lid&#x27; of soil, turning them into simple pitfall traps. The trenches were too huge to be easily filled, and I&#x27;d placed them fairly close together. If the enemy was planning on hitting us with catapults or something, they&#x27;d have a hell of a time rolling them into range. Oh, and the walls were far too steep to climb, naturally. They had little hope of clambering inside them to use as defensive positions or anything.</p>
<p>While I was at it, I whipped up a stone wall, enclosing the natural rivers that already surrounded Fort Karon, and created an extra moat on the outside for more layered protection. This would make it tough for the enemy to see what we were up to from a distance. Even if they managed to push past my pitfalls, they&#x27;d have a slightly harder time getting at the fort itself.</p>
<p>&quot;My thanks, Master Rudeus. Your work is as superb as ever.&quot;</p>
<p>It took a solid day of work to get everything completed, but I had been very thorough. It sure as hell wasn&#x27;t going to be easy for anyone to march an army through this field.</p>
<p>&quot;Maybe we can relax a little now, huh?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Oh, I wouldn&#x27;t say that,&quot; said Zanoba calmly. &quot;I&#x27;d imagine you could destroy our fort from the far side of these traps, couldn&#x27;t you?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;True enough.&quot; I could see the fort from here. That meant it was well inside my effective range.</p>
<p>&quot;Then,&quot; he said, &quot;it seems prudent to assume that other mages could attack us from that range as well.&quot;</p>
<p>True, I didn&#x27;t actually know what kind of range your average magician had on their spells, but any higher-ranked mage could manage it for sure. And it was possible the Man-God had arranged to send some King- or Sainttier mage our way.</p>
<p>&quot;Alternatively, our opponent might put their mages to work filling in your traps,&quot; Zanoba suggested.</p>
<p>Most of my work today had consisted of constructing those pitfall trenches. For a bunch of holes in the ground, they were effective obstacles. But they were also…nothing but a bunch of holes in the ground. They might be dealt with almost instantly if the other army happened to have an earth mage in their ranks.</p>
<p>&quot;In either scenario,&quot; Zanoba continued, &quot;I believe the first phase of the battle will require you and Miss Roxy to counter or disrupt the enemy&#x27;s spells.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Oh. Yeah, that makes sense.&quot;</p>
<p>We did have two excellent mages on our own side, didn&#x27;t we? If the enemy tried to mess with my landscaping efforts, the two of us could just counter their spells from a distance.</p>
<p>&quot;I hope to elaborate further at a later date,&quot; said Zanoba, &quot;but essentially, the traps you laid today form one part of our larger plan.&quot;</p>
<p>When the enemy saw my traps, they would array their forces on the</p>
<p>other side and try to find some way to advance. Basically, they could either use mages to alter the terrain or try to push through with a massive human wave. In the former case, I&#x27;d counter their spells; in the latter, our archers would pick them off from the fort.</p>
<p>It seemed like a solid strategy. I couldn&#x27;t imagine the enemy would overwhelm us easily, at least.</p>
<p>I was starting to feel almost confident about our chances here.</p>
<p>The next three days or so passed uneventfully.</p>
<p>The Magic Armor Version One had been delivered to the fort, and I took the time to piece it together. It was fundamentally designed for closerange combat, though, so I probably wouldn&#x27;t equip it unless the enemy made it to the fort&#x27;s walls. I didn&#x27;t want to burn through all my mana stomping around in it, given that I might have to fight one of the Seven Great Powers right after this.</p>
<p>After all that, I spent most of my time reinforcing the fort under Zanoba&#x27;s direction. Mostly, this just involved sealing holes and strengthening the walls. None of these were jobs that required a lot of mana, so I was happy to help out.</p>
<p>While I was patching things up, Roxy gave magic lessons to the troops —not only the combat mages, but the ordinary foot soldiers as well. Even if they just managed to learn a basic spell or two, it might save their life in a pinch.</p>
<p>Perhaps because of her reputation as a former court magician, Roxy seemed to be pretty popular with the garrison in general. The troops treated her with obvious respect. On the other hand, I felt like people were starting to avoid me. Not like they were hostile or anything; they were more intimidated. I guess they&#x27;d been a bit spooked by the way I&#x27;d totally transformed the terrain in a single day. Every time I strolled around the fort, soldiers would jump out of my way like startled rabbits. When I asked someone a question, they&#x27;d answer politely; but it was really rare for anyone to speak to me first.</p>
<p>It was kind of depressing, honestly. Especially since Zanoba and Roxy already seemed to have earned their trust. Maybe they just had better social skills than me? There was always the aggressively talkative approach, but I</p>
<p>wasn&#x27;t sure if it would help this time…</p>
<p>Well, I hadn&#x27;t come here to make friends or anything, so it wasn&#x27;t the end of the world. Just kind of a downer.</p>
<p>It wasn&#x27;t all bad here, anyway. The people weren&#x27;t too friendly, but the food was delicious. That was actually a side effect of Pax&#x27;s close ties with the King Dragon Realm. Although they hadn&#x27;t sent him an army of reinforcements, they provided material support for Shirone&#x27;s war effort. For the most part, it came in the form of food supplies. Sanakia rice was the staple of the King Dragon Realm diet. You could find it in Shirone as well, but in this fort it was the main component of our meals. Its flavor was a bit different from the &quot;Aisha rice&quot; we were developing back in Sharia. To put it bluntly, it wasn&#x27;t quite as good. Aisha had been experimenting to improve our home-grown variety according to my tastes, after all.</p>
<p>Still, rice was rice, and I got to eat it every single day. I was halftempted to enlist as a soldier of Shirone, if this was how they fed them.</p>
<p>Too bad that would mean having Pax for a boss.</p>

<p>We had three days at most, then. Maybe two.</p>

<p>Was I actually capable of doing this?</p>
