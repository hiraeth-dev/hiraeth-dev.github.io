<p><strong>C</strong>LIFF GRIMOIRE, the grandson of the Millis Church&#x27;s reigning Pope, was a highly gifted young man with a particular talent for magic. Unfortunately, Cliff was also hot-tempered, egotistical, and a pompous braggart. As a result, he had no friends whatsoever. Cliff was sixteen years old at present; in other words, he&#x27;d come of age a little more than a year ago. But no one had celebrated that milestone with him.</p>
<p>Still, the young man did have his virtues. For all his boastful talk, he worked very hard to succeed, rather than leaning on his natural talents. There were some, at least, who noticed this and respected him for it.</p>
<p>Cliff had come to the Ranoa University of Magic for a simple reason: he&#x27;d gotten mixed up in an ugly power struggle back home. Following the attempted assassination of a Blessed Child near the city of Millishion several years ago, an internal conflict within the Millis Church had grown increasingly intense and violent. Cliff&#x27;s grandfather, who happened to be the Pope, had shipped him off to the far side of the world for his own safety.</p>
<p>Cliff remembered his grandfather&#x27;s parting words to him perfectly: &quot;You have the potential to be a great man someday, Cliff. Don&#x27;t let yourself grow self-satisfied; know your flaws, and work to overcome them.&quot;</p>
<p>The young man knew that much was expected of him. And at the time, he found that reasonable enough. He was a prodigy, after all. Perhaps not as talented as the brilliant young swordswoman Eris, whom he&#x27;d seen defeat a group of trained assassins in the blink of an eye; but a prodigy nonetheless. He&#x27;d always believed himself to possess special gifts.</p>
<p>The Kingdom of Ranoa, which Cliff reached following a long and difficult journey, proved to be a harsh land. The food didn&#x27;t agree with him, the climate was severe, and many of the locals behaved in ways he found strange and off-putting.</p>
<p>Still, he trusted that his sheer talent would see him through any challenge. He was a Special Student, the grandson of the pope, and the man who&#x27;d one day take charge of the entire Millis Church; surely that meant he was a cut above the rest.</p>
<p>To Cliff&#x27;s surprise, however, he was embarrassed twice in his first year at the University.</p>
<p>The first humiliation came at the hands of a young man named Zanoba Shirone. Zanoba was a Blessed Child, bestowed with certain divine gifts at birth. He was a somewhat unstable individual, true. But his physical strength was genuinely astonishing. Cliff had once seen Zanoba seize a man three times his weight by the head, lift him off the ground, and effortlessly toss him to one side.</p>
<p>Despite his fearsome capabilities, Zanoba had enrolled at the University of Magic, where he studied spellcraft like all the others. By Cliff&#x27;s standards, his progress was painfully slow, but it wasn&#x27;t like a Blessed Child had much <em>need</em> of magic. In fact, some scholars theorized that magic had originally been developed by the ancients as a means to help common people imitate the divine powers. And, of course, a Blessed Child was a human manifestation of those very powers. There was hardly any reason for one of God&#x27;s elected to trifle with casting spells.</p>
<p>Eventually, Cliff had approached Zanoba and pressed him for an explanation. &quot;Why are you even bothering to learn magic, Zanoba?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;That&#x27;s simple enough,&quot; the young man had responded. &quot;I&#x27;m pursuing a goal that means everything to me.&quot; Reaching into a box he carried with him, Zanoba retrieved a single figurine… which he then proceeded to speak about at <em>great</em> length. The majority of this monologue meant nothing to Cliff, but it was clear that Zanoba had nothing but praise for the quality of the little figure&#x27;s design and manufacture.</p>
<p>&quot;I wish to apprentice myself to the man who made this figurine, and to spread such wondrous figurines across the world. For this to happen, I&#x27;ll have to learn to make figurines myself! Before I&#x27;m reunited with my master, I <em>must</em> master at least the basic spells necessary for this purpose. I&#x27;d be too ashamed to face him otherwise! And of course, I have a few figurines I&#x27;m dying to create with my own two hands.&quot;</p>
<p>The man had a dream<em>.</em> This was something that Cliff himself lacked. He had given up on his own dream some time ago. Given his position in the world, there&#x27;d been no other choice but to do so. Still… Zanoba, too, was a person of some importance. As a Blessed Child, he carried the hopes of his countrymen on his shoulders. Once he returned to Shirone, he would surely have no real leeway to choose his own path in life. And yet, he still clung to some thread of hope—planning for the <em>possibility</em> that some day, he might be free. If he ever had the chance, he wouldn&#x27;t hesitate to choose his own destiny.</p>
<p>Such were Cliff&#x27;s impressions, at any rate. They were based on assumptions that were not entirely accurate. He knew nothing of the events that had taken place back in Shirone, or of Zanoba&#x27;s actual standing there. Still, his interpretation left a deep impression on him. He found himself looking at Zanoba with real respect—even admiration.</p>
<p>&quot;Who is this &#x27;master&#x27; you keep talking about, anyway?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;He is a magician known as Rudeus Greyrat.&quot;</p>
<p>Cliff found himself at a loss for words. <em>Rudeus Greyrat.</em> It was a name he&#x27;d filed away in a dark corner of his mind, ever since the day Eris had rejected him. He&#x27;d never expected to hear it again in this place, spoken by a man he&#x27;d just come to respect.</p>
<p>It was a harsh blow to his ego.</p>
<p>The second of Cliff&#x27;s humiliations came at the hands of two older students.</p>
<p>As one might expect, Cliff believed himself to be the single most powerful mage enrolled in the University. There were plenty of people who could overwhelm him in a close-range brawl, of course, but he thought himself clearly superior as a magician at the very least. He was a genuine prodigy, while the others were merely students. Even the professors were often no match for his skills. In short, he thought himself essentially invincible.</p>
<p>It took all of two months for him to be rudely disabused of that notion. His defeat came at the hands of two beastfolk girls, said to be among the strongest students in the University. Their names were Linia and Pursena.</p>
<p>It was hard to say who exactly provoked the fight. Cliff was a sharp-tongued young man, and he&#x27;d spoken to them with open arrogance. Linia and Pursena were less aggressive than they&#x27;d once been, but they weren&#x27;t about to let a cocky first-year talk down to them. Cliff didn&#x27;t even recall exactly what he&#x27;d said to finally set them off. But the fight itself he remembered very clearly. He&#x27;d attempted to cast an Advanced spell; Pursena had quickly fired off Beginner-level magic, interrupting his incantation and restricting his movements. Linia then drew in close and beat him black and blue.</p>
<p>In the aftermath of this very public defeat, Cliff retreated to his room to cry in solitude. He told himself that it wasn&#x27;t a fair fight. He&#x27;d been outnumbered, after all. He hadn&#x27;t really lost.</p>
<p>But a few days later, he learned that another student named Fitz had previously defeated both Linia and Pursena in an instant. And that news came as a <em>real</em> shock.</p>
<p>There was always someone better out there. Obvious as it might sound, Cliff had never learned this lesson personally until now. The fact that he knew so much Advanced magic did not, in itself, make him powerful in combat. This, too, was something he had just begun to understand.</p>
<p>Cliff took all this very hard. Yet from that day he redoubled his efforts to improve himself. He was too proud to learn from his professors, let alone from other students. Instead, he tried finding his own ways to refine his craft. It proved a struggle, but he kept at it, doggedly seeking to eliminate his weaknesses.</p>
<p>In time, however, he entered his second year at the University… and received another pair of shocks in quick succession.</p>
<p>The first shock was the enrollment of Rudeus Greyrat.</p>
<p>The boy wore shabby grey robes, and the uncertainty on his face betrayed a lack of confidence. He was servile and submissive to everyone he met, putting himself down at every opportunity; he also routinely leered at every nearby woman. There was nothing manly or appealing about him whatsoever.</p>
<p>He was, in other words, virtually the opposite of what Cliff had pictured when he heard Eris and Zanoba speak of &quot;Rudeus.&quot; Was this even really him? Could it be someone else with the same name? It seemed like a legitimate possibility.</p>
<p>But Zanoba acknowledged Rudeus as his &quot;master,&quot; and the boy knew about Eris as well. And so, Cliff concluded that he simply had to be a fraud. Somehow, he had deceived both Zanoba and Eris with a pack of lies and a few devious tricks.</p>
<p>The evidence seemed to support this theory. When challenged by Linia and Pursena, the boy instantly bent and scraped to avoid a conflict. If he was a truly powerful magician, he surely wouldn&#x27;t have hesitated to put them in their place.</p>
<p>In conclusion, Cliff reasoned that Rudeus would be exposed as the fraud he was soon enough. Linia and Pursena were fearsome fighters, and Zanoba was a diligent young man with divine powers at his disposal. Bluffing and trickery would only get you so far in an environment like this one. There were rumors flying around that Rudeus had defeated Fitz. But this was presumably either a misunderstanding of some sort, or a lie that Rudeus himself was spreading. If he had won somehow, he must have resorted to some underhanded trick. Cliff felt quite confident of this.</p>
<p>However, Rudeus soon demonstrated that his skills were real. He could cast magic freely without the need for incantations. In no time at all, he made Linia and Pursena his loyal subordinates, and somehow won even more admiration from Zanoba. Even Fitz seemed to recognize his skills: soon they were seen studying together at the library every other day. And despite Rudeus&#x27; obvious abilities, Cliff had even seen him attending <em>classes</em>—lectures on elementary Divine and Barrier spells. He had no real need to learn such basic magic, surely, but he seemed to have an innate hunger for knowledge of all kinds.</p>
<p>Rudeus Greyrat was just as devoted as Cliff was, and considerably more talented. More importantly, his actual achievements were far more impressive.</p>
<p>This would normally have been very painful for Cliff to admit. But for some reason, he found himself readily able to accept the facts. Perhaps it was because he&#x27;d already met Zanoba, and lost to Linia and Pursena. He could admit, to himself at least, that this Rudeus was destined for greater things than he was.</p>
<p>This didn&#x27;t mean he <em>liked</em> the boy, of course. That was a very different matter.</p>
<p>The next and final shock was of a somewhat different nature.</p>
<p>It struck Cliff without warning one evening, as he was walking back to his dorm and happened to look upward.</p>
<p>He found himself gazing at a goddess. She was leaning on a windowsill with a listless expression, letting her luxuriant golden hair flutter in the breeze. The setting sun cast a red glow on her shapely face.</p>
<p>Cliff was instantly smitten. He&#x27;d fallen in love at first sight. He had always been drawn to beauty of this sort. Back in his more childish days, when he&#x27;d dreamed of living as an adventurer, he&#x27;d also pictured himself marrying a gorgeous woman. In fact, a pretty young Healer who sometimes paid visits to the orphanage where she grew up had been a big part of the reason Cliff developed such a strong interest in adventuring.</p>
<p>All of a sudden, the woman in the window glanced down at Cliff. With a small smile, she waved her hand.</p>
<p>It was all so… picturesque. So <em>perfect.</em> Cliff was deeply, deeply moved.</p>
<p><em>I was born to meet this woman,</em> he thought. <em>And she was born to meet me.</em> In that instant, his first love Eris was demoted in his mind to the status of an acquaintance.</p>

<p>Just as importantly, though, she&#x27;d started to take her first clumsy steps as a silent spellcaster. Master Fitz was right on the money when he suggested that learning magic at an early age was the best way to master that skill.</p>

I encouraged her to keep trying, telling her not to worry about her mistakes. Success never comes easily, and giving up after one failure is a good way to turn yourself into a sulky shut-in loser.</p>

<p>A week later, I was researching teleportation with Master Fitz at one of our regular library sessions.</p>
<p>Recently I&#x27;d begun to understand that teleportation bore certain similarities to Summoning magic. The magic circles used were very much alike, and the color of the magical energy they released when activated was almost identical.</p>
<p>They were totally different in one respect, though. It was totally impossible to summon a human being. There was simply no known way to do it, even with the most advanced and complex of techniques. You could call forth fiends, spirits, and even plants, yes. But not a person. I&#x27;d pored through countless records, myths, and ancient histories without finding a single reference to anyone summoning a person. There were many races in this world, including the various tribes of Demonkind, but this rule seemed to apply to all of them equally.</p>
<p>This didn&#x27;t have any direct relevance to what we wanted to know, of course. Maybe it wasn&#x27;t a meaningful insight. But there was something about it that nagged at me. You couldn&#x27;t summon a flesh and blood person. Fair enough. But what about their soul?</p>
<p>I didn&#x27;t voice these thoughts, but I did file them away quietly. If I ever met a real expert in this field, I&#x27;d have to ask them about the possibility of summoning the spirit of a dead man from another world.</p>
<p>&quot;Master Fitz, could you try to find out if there are any professors who know a lot about Summoning for me?&quot;</p>

<p>Come to think of it, I&#x27;d noticed a distinct lack of Summoning classes on the list of courses offered here… although Enchantment was technically a subcategory, from the sound of things. Had I read something about that in one of my textbooks? &quot;Well, it can&#x27;t hurt to poke around and see what you find, at least.&quot;</p>
<p>To be honest, a small seed of uncertainty was growing inside me at this point. I didn&#x27;t let it show, of course. I was probably mistaken. The Displacement Incident had occurred when I was ten—a whole decade after I was reincarnated in this world. Surely those two things weren&#x27;t connected? Ten years had passed without anything happening, after all…</p>
<p>With a hint of anxiety still lingering in my mind, I left the library and headed for my dorm by the light of the setting sun. The latest snowfall had mostly melted away; patches of red-brown earth were visible across the courtyard, and the paved stone path was clear.
