<p><strong>Extra Chapter: The Monkey and the Wolf</strong></p>
<p><strong>Geese</strong></p>
<p><strong>M</strong>Y EYES OPENED.</p>
<p>I rose, cracking my neck and checking all my parts were working. No tingling in my limbs, no indigestion. No weird growths on my skin. Apart from a mild grumble in the belly, I was fit as a fiddle.</p>
<p>I went out of my tent and stretched, feeling my back crack as I yawned. I watched the sun rise. The direction of the sun told me the direction I was facing. I compared that with my map and the ridgeline to confirm my current location. I&#x27;d checked yesterday too, before the sun set, but things can look different from morning to evening, y&#x27;know? It&#x27;s important to check two or three times. It&#x27;s mostly the idiots who don&#x27;t confirm where they are who get lost.</p>
<p>&quot;West today, huh,&quot; I muttered to myself as I worked out where I needed to go. No one was around to reply.</p>
<p>Last night the Man-God came into my dreams again. Told me to go west with the rising sun, rest at the roots of the third tree on Fenyl Boulevard, then get on the fifth carriage that passed. I&#x27;d ride in the carriage for a while, then get off at the town it arrived in and stay at the New Leaf Inn. That&#x27;d keep me out of the hands of Ruquag&#x27;s Mercenary Band, he said.</p>
<p>Doesn&#x27;t make much sense, does it? Now, if you&#x27;re an average guy, you&#x27;d probably start to feel a bit suspicious of all that. It&#x27;s not like the Man-God ever tells you why you gotta do everything just so. So at some point in the course of your life, you wind up doing something a little different to what He told you, and bam, they got you. I get it, I really do. Back in the day, I used to pull stuff like that myself.</p>
<p>These days though, I live by the Man-God&#x27;s words. That&#x27;s the right way to live, I reckon. Far as I&#x27;m concerned, the Man-God&#x27;s word is law.</p>
<p>Yeah, all right, I hear you. Obviously just because I do what He says doesn&#x27;t mean everything always turns out perfect. Sometimes His advice</p>
<p>gets me into some pretty gnarly situations. Ain&#x27;t even uncommon. But you know what I say to that?</p>
<p>So what? I mean come on, think about it. Whether I do what He says or not, sometimes shit happens. Life&#x27;s not all sunshine and roses. One thing I can say for sure though: so long as I obey Him, I don&#x27;t die. How do I know that? I&#x27;m weak as they come, but I&#x27;ve gotten through some majorly dangerous situations and lived to tell the tale. Look, I&#x27;ve watched a lot of tough guys way beyond my level slip up and die. It&#x27;s pathetic, actually. These guys are always swaggering around like they&#x27;re king badass, then when they&#x27;re about to die they start blubbering. Help me, I don&#x27;t wanna die, save me, Mommy!</p>
<p>I get it, everyone&#x27;s a little pathetic, it&#x27;s fine. But the guys who go down like that are always the dudes who go around bragging about how death don&#x27;t scare them. Bonafide hero types, every single one of them. Don&#x27;t it make ya sick?</p>
<p>Look, people try to avoid death—that&#x27;s nature. Our instincts tell us dying&#x27;s bad, that it&#x27;s freakin&#x27; scary. And don&#x27;t get me wrong, I&#x27;m scared. I don&#x27;t wanna die. And that&#x27;s why so long as the Man-God gives me advice that keeps me alive, that&#x27;s all I need. He&#x27;s the reason I&#x27;ve survived this long. You could say He&#x27;s my guardian angel. Or whatever the evil version of that is.</p>
<p>The story of how I got my chance to pay Him back for all this starts a few years ago. I was passed-out drunk in a tavern in Asura like usual when the Man-God spoke to me. Said He had a request. Now, his &#x27;requests&#x27; pretty much never end well. The last time He had one for me, my hometown got wiped off the map. I wept enough for a lifetime and screamed until my voice gave out. This time, no doubt in my mind, it&#x27;d be just as bad. He likes to make you think he&#x27;s on your side and then break you. Back when my hometown got destroyed He showed up just to laugh at my stupid shocked face.</p>
<p>I expected all that, but this time something was different. I didn&#x27;t get this far in life without knowing how to read people. I could tell the Man-God was in a serious bind, and He&#x27;d come looking for help. That&#x27;s why I decided to accept. I did think it might be an act, but the dude&#x27;s not exactly an actor… Plus, if He really was in a jam, I had no hesitation about lending a hand. A debt&#x27;s a debt, after all, and I owed Him big.</p>
<p>The Man-God said that Rudeus had betrayed Him. In reality He&#x27;d probably shown up to have a good laugh at Rudeus like He&#x27;d done with</p>
<p>me, and it hadn&#x27;t gone how He&#x27;d wanted. Anyway, He said Rudeus was His enemy now. That he&#x27;d sided with the Dragon God Orsted, I think it was. Number two out of the Seven Great Powers. A real big shot. The details aren&#x27;t important—all I needed to know is that Boss had allied himself with this big-shot god, and now he was making trouble for the Man-God.</p>
<p>The Man-God can see the future. He can see so far ahead that the Demon Eye of Foresight might as well be blind. You&#x27;d think that&#x27;d make beating his enemies child&#x27;s play…but apparently it wasn&#x27;t that simple. He didn&#x27;t tell me all the ins and outs, but He did tell me two things.</p>
<p>First, He could only see the futures of three people at a time. Second, He couldn&#x27;t see Orsted&#x27;s future. If Orsted went and interfered with any of the three people whose future He&#x27;d already seen, those futures would change. From the Man-God&#x27;s point of view, if Orsted—and only Orsted—messed with their futures, it&#x27;d be like absolutely nothing had changed. From His white room He could see the whole wide world, but Orsted was a gaping hole in His vision.</p>
<p>Now, he said, Rudeus had inherited this little quirk of Orsted&#x27;s. He was under the Dragon God&#x27;s protection or something. Orsted had some kind of curse on him that made people fear him and see him as an enemy, so there weren&#x27;t too many people like that. No one went calling on him for aid, and he didn&#x27;t have any allies. But with Boss as his go-between, all of a sudden he could get loads of people on his side. Now, how do you think that&#x27;ll work out for the Man-God?</p>
<p>The funny thing is, the Man-God can see His own death. One day, without warning, His vision flipped. He used to see Himself towering over where Orsted where he lay fallen, kicking him as he lay there. Now it was Orsted doing the laughing and the kicking.</p>
<p>Why could He only see that moment? Well, probably because at that moment, Orsted and the Man-God were both in the same place. He saw that vision through his own eyes, and that meant He could see Orsted too. Look, I don&#x27;t worry about the details of how the Man-God&#x27;s powers work. What mattered was that Rudeus was now a threat. The Man-God needed Rudeus taken out quick, and He&#x27;d already tried a bunch of plans to kill him. No matter what He tried, though, nothing worked. In the Asura Kingdom, He&#x27;d tried pitting the North Emperor and the Water God against him, but neither of them got the job done. Not only did Orsted come out fine, but He couldn&#x27;t even take down Rudeus. Rudeus went on his merry</p>
<p>way, still recruiting.</p>
<p>So the Man-God hatched a plan. If three disciples weren&#x27;t enough to take down Orsted, He&#x27;d just make more. We&#x27;d copy Rudeus. Orsted couldn&#x27;t build alliances himself, but with Boss as his go-between he rustled up a whole network of helpers. The Man-God could only work through three disciples at a time, but so long as He got one of those disciples to gather allies, He&#x27;d end up with a lot more than three followers.</p>
<p>Sweet idea, huh?</p>
<p>And I&#x27;d been singled out as the man for the job of wrangling those allies. I did wonder why He went with me… But then, the Man-God&#x27;s usual playbook when He finished using someone was to trample on everything they love and throw whatever was left in the trash, so maybe I was the last guy He had left.</p>
<p>Once I was done building our army, He&#x27;d wait for the perfect moment, then have them all strike at once. Bye-bye, Rudeus.</p>
<p>And that&#x27;s how I ended up here, running my tail off finding people to turn to the Man-God&#x27;s cause. My deadline was the Man-God&#x27;s &quot;right moment.&quot; Not much time left, but I wasn&#x27;t doing bad. Not that finding allies was easy.</p>
<p>Here&#x27;s how we tag-teamed it: the Man-God told me, &quot;That guy!&quot; and then I went and met them, buttered them up with my best smoothtalking, and then told them to be at the &quot;meeting point&quot; for the &quot;right moment.&quot;</p>
<p>Everyone the Man-God had sent me after so far was sketchy as hell. They could get the job done for sure, but they were all a little cockeyed, or they only seemed to half follow what I was talking about, or they had some weird issues, or I just couldn&#x27;t get a read on them at all… I mean hey, that&#x27;s probably why they&#x27;d sit still while a guy like me was talking.</p>
<p>Main problem was that there weren&#x27;t a lot of them. Including me, I could count us all on two hands.</p>
<p>What they lacked in numbers, they made up for in muscle. From world-famous warriors to dudes who&#x27;d fit right into Millis fairytales, they were all top of the class. I tried to suggest that maybe we should just hire a couple of hundred generic types who&#x27;d work for gold or whatever instead, but that got the thumbs down. The Man-God was nervous about traitors. He wasn&#x27;t wild about this business of taking on people whose futures He couldn&#x27;t see.</p>
<p>Fair &#x27;nuff.</p>
<p>The Man-God isn&#x27;t exactly popular. You didn&#x27;t have to be a genius to know what would happen if Rudeus showed up to win hearts and minds. Boss might not look it, but he&#x27;s got a knack for getting people to follow him. Worried about something? He&#x27;ll be there worrying with you. Got a problem? He&#x27;ll be there to solve it with you. No matter how much you lag behind, he&#x27;ll wait for you to catch up, and even though he&#x27;s got crazy power levels he&#x27;s nice to guys who don&#x27;t.</p>
<p>That&#x27;s why we couldn&#x27;t rely just on numbers. The Man-God was right.</p>
<p>Also, sorry to say, I&#x27;m not the charismatic type. I can&#x27;t work a crowd.</p>
<p>Every ally was a potential enemy, so we couldn&#x27;t take on too many. We&#x27;d also be more likely to end up with idiots who didn&#x27;t listen to the plan. That was all you needed to turn a winning position into a losing one. So we stuck with a select few. If nothing else, these guys wouldn&#x27;t turn traitor. They turned out to be pretty useful, for all their quirks.</p>
<p>With their help, we&#x27;d suss out Rudeus and Orsted&#x27;s weak points.</p>
<p>Hmmm…</p>
<p>Maybe I&#x27;m speaking outta line, but I reckon He could probably be a bit more trusting. Y&#x27;know? It wouldn&#x27;t even matter who they were, if we had numbers on our side, it&#x27;d really increase our options. You don&#x27;t get the big money unless you take a few risks.</p>
<p>At the end of the day though, he&#x27;s the boss, I&#x27;m the disciple, his word goes. The chief did have a few words for me this time, though. Why didn&#x27;t you kill Rudeus when you had the chance? You could&#x27;ve poisoned him!</p>
<p>Yeah, sure. The thing is, I gotta be true to myself. How to put it? Well, betraying Boss would, if you look at it from the right angle, be the same as betraying Paul, right? I could never have betrayed Paul, so how could I murder his son, right? Man&#x27;s gotta have a code, y&#x27;know?</p>
<p>The Man-God didn&#x27;t buy it, but I know myself. Say I&#x27;d tried to poison Rudeus or whatever, I reckon I&#x27;d have choked before I went through with it. Along the way, I&#x27;d have gotten cold feet. But after he turned traitor, there&#x27;s no more fear of that. I&#x27;ve made my mind up for real now. Rudeus Greyrat is my enemy.</p>
<p>So here I am, present-day, getting ready to set off for yet another day of sniffing out hidden talent to join the case.</p>
<p>How many was I up to now? Three? Four? Every one of them so far was worth an army by themselves. I never thought I&#x27;d get to meet this caliber of guy, let alone talk to them. Far as I was concerned, they were all the stuff of legends and way outta my league. When I started talking to them, though, they were surprisingly…I mean, okay, it shouldn&#x27;t have come as a surprise. But they were all just…guys. Ordinary guys. Even if they had some personality issues.</p>
<p>Especially the first one. He was famous enough—even you might know him. But damn, he really was just another guy.</p>
<p>***</p>
<p>So here we were, a little while after the Man-God came to me with His request. Before I set off, the Man-God had some busywork for me.</p>
<p>I grabbed a blade of a demon sword lying rotting in the back of a storehouse of old junk in the Asura Kingdom and then a hilt from a burial mound in the King Dragon Realm, then took them to a blacksmith who handled demon weapons and had them reforged. I went and got a hold of this alcohol made by a pretty shifty tribe on the Demon Continent. Few other odds and ends. I didn&#x27;t know what any of it was for, mind you. Though I mean, from what the Man-God told me, and imagining what was coming best I could, I could see how that stuff might be handy. Better safe than sorry, as they say. Better to be over-prepared. I also did a little snooping, but I can&#x27;t beat the Man-God when it comes to gathering intel so a lot of that work went to waste.</p>
<p>After all that, I headed north on the Man-God&#x27;s instructions.</p>
<p>Unlike Rudeus, I didn&#x27;t have any ancient Dragon Tribe displacement relics, so I was limited by travel time. But there were a few other teleportation circles around, funnily enough. I didn&#x27;t know if Orsted knew about them or not. He didn&#x27;t seem to be using them, so I used them to get around. There are only a few of &#x27;em and they couldn&#x27;t get you absolutely everywhere, but they came in handy.</p>
<p>On the Man-God&#x27;s say-so, I went to the town closest to my final destination, stocked up on cold weather gear, then plodded out into the snow that was just beginning to pile up.</p>
<p>I was heading for a ravine in the middle of a forest, and the forest was home to monsters. I&#x27;d definitely run into some. A guy like me had no business going in alone without any weapons or defense.</p>
<p>But I had a few tricks up my sleeve. The Man-God told me that if I entered the forest at the right time, then did the right thing at the right moment, I could get from A to B without being bothered. For example, He said, &quot;When you reach a cave beneath a great Tournel Tree, stop and count slowly to twenty before moving on.&quot; I did as He told me, checking underneath each Tournel Tree I passed. There was no chance I&#x27;d miss it. If the Man-God said there was a cave, it&#x27;d be there.</p>
<p>There&#x27;d be no sign it worked and no explanation why I oughta do it. I&#x27;d stand there in front of a little hole maybe just big enough for a kid to hide inside in the softly falling snow and count slowly to twenty. I wouldn&#x27;t look inside, or pull anything out of it, nothing would come crawling out. If everything went perfect, best-case scenario, nothing would happen. Without a hope in hell of understanding what I was doing, I&#x27;d hurry off on my way.</p>
<p>Oh, but if I hung out just a second longer, something real bad would happen.</p>
<p>Now I&#x27;m no slouch so I could guess what&#x27;s what. I&#x27;m an S-ranked adventurer. I knew what kind of monster made its nest in this hole. This was where Snowbucks, these beasts kinda like giant deer, lived when they were infants. They spent the winter there, then came out in spring. They holed up to protect themselves against their natural predators…basically every other flesh-eater and monsters. The head honcho in this forest? Well, that&#x27;d be the Iceclaw Tiger. They burrow through the snow after their prey, then pounce when you&#x27;re least expecting it. I never noticed a thing, but hell, I probably was being stalked by an Iceclaw Tiger. This here, though, was an easier, tastier meal. May the little baby Snowbuck rest in peace.</p>
<p>Anyway, that&#x27;s how it works when you can see the future. Things might be dangerous, but there&#x27;s no need to worry about dying. Nothing unexpected happens. You might get a few scrapes, some bruises, but you always get the job done.</p>
<p>I got through the forest that way.</p>
<p>Just outside the forest, I found the ravine. A cold wind cut through it; the cliff walls were all sheeted in ice. Chunks of it floated in the river</p>
<p>that ran along the bottom.</p>
<p>&quot;Brrr…&quot; I shivered.</p>
<p>Cold didn&#x27;t do it justice. I wanted to get the hell out of here right quick. But I swallowed that feeling and set off. I walked for half a day alongside the icy ravine until I found a path leading down the cliff face. I followed it down, then continued further up the ravine until I found him.</p>
<p>He sat leaning against a huge boulder, cradling his sword. A campfire blazed in front of him, where a hunk of meat on a spit sizzled as it roasted. I didn&#x27;t need to ask to know what kind of meat it was. I could see the carcass lying just behind the man and his fire.</p>
<p>It was covered in white scales the color of snow and had enormous talons and fangs: a Snow Dragon. An S-ranked monster. These monsters were sudden mutations of the A-ranked White Drake. They were twice the size of a White Drake, breathed ice, and could use high-level water magic. Their wings weren&#x27;t for flying, but for helping them jump. They used their muscular legs to kick off the walls of the ravine and leap down on their prey.</p>
<p>They weren&#x27;t technically dragons, but they were still closer to a dragon than a White Drake. They were as strong as dragons, hence that name—Snow Dragon, ya see? They were seriously rare, and tyrannized and devoured whole flocks of White Drakes. Not the kind of monster you went hunting alone.</p>
<p>This guy seemed to have taken this bad boy down all by himself. I wasn&#x27;t surprised or anything. I knew he was the sort of guy who could do it. And now we were gonna have a talk.</p>
<p>When I got near enough to him, a shiver shot up my spine. This guy would kill me. He didn&#x27;t have to warn me. I knew that past this point I&#x27;d be stepping in range of his sword, and I&#x27;d best be ready to face the consequences. My face felt like it was going to cramp up, but I forced myself to show a smile. A smile that would hide my fear and radiate confidence. Then, smile fixed in place, I went up to him. It felt kind of wrong for me to be looking down on this guy, but he was sitting down. What was I supposed to do?</p>
<p>&quot;Yes?&quot; he said.</p>
<p>It was a challenge, but his voice was deathly calm. He wasn&#x27;t trying to threaten or intimidate me, just asking indifferently after my sudden appearance like you might ask someone&#x27;s name.</p>
<p>So I answered, &quot;I&#x27;m Geese.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I didn&#x27;t ask your name,&quot; he replied.</p>
<p>All right, read that wrong. I wondered where to start. I had a lot to talk to him about. To start off with, though, I decided to just shut up and stand there. Guys like this hate smooth talkers. They had their own method of persuasion.</p>
<p>For y&#x27;all following along, that method&#x27;s &quot;violence.&quot; You know. That thing that I&#x27;m no good at. And this guy in particular—his violence was impeccable. Great stuff, world-class. No need to break that out here, though. I sure wasn&#x27;t about to start any. Silence would work fine.</p>
<p>&quot;What the hell&#x27;s going on here?&quot; he growled.</p>
<p>See what I mean? I kept my lips zipped and he started talking all by himself. He wasn&#x27;t done. &quot;The other night some bastard calling himself god man or whatever showed up in my dreams saying he wants me to help him out. Said if I heard him out, he&#x27;d make my dreams come true. He tells me about this place as proof. When I showed up, I found this thing.&quot; He jerked his thumb at the Snow Dragon carcass behind him.</p>
<p>Hey now, Lord Man-God, You didn&#x27;t say nothin&#x27; about calling him here. If I&#x27;d been told to show up here then found a beast like that waiting, I&#x27;d think I&#x27;d been tricked.</p>
<p>&quot;When I was a lad, I ran up against a Snow Dragon and barely got out with my life,&quot; he said. &quot;I was going to go back and kill it one day, but along the way I forgot about it. Would you believe it? I show up and here it is.&quot;</p>
<p>Ahah, so that&#x27;s Your game, I thought. I got it now. The Man-God was a pro at this kind of thing. Making your dreams come true, or close to it. Anyway, this guy didn&#x27;t seem to feel like he&#x27;d been tricked. Even after having a Snow Dragon set on him.</p>
<p>Oh, right, of course. One of those hero types.</p>
<p>&quot;So I killed it, and now you show up,&quot; he went on, then pointed at me. &quot;A monkey face… Hey, you said you&#x27;re called Geese, right?&quot;</p>
<p>Finally, he looked up at me and for the first time, I saw his face. He didn&#x27;t look especially strong. I spend all my time trying to read people, so I can usually tell from their faces whether they&#x27;re strong or weak. It&#x27;s not like I judge it off how rough they look. It&#x27;s all in the expression. People who&#x27;re strong usually put it all out there. They work hard every day, so</p>
<p>they don&#x27;t think of it like hardship. It&#x27;s normal business to them. They have a clear image of their own capabilities and they don&#x27;t waver. That means they don&#x27;t usually put up a front.</p>
<p>This dude wasn&#x27;t showing off, but he was wavering. Someone had come along and torn everything he thought was true into tiny pieces. Now he was exhausted, out of patience, and at his limit. That was what his face told me. Ohhh, I got it. I see. He&#x27;d gotten his ass kicked, and recently. Beaten! Half dead. This is someone who thought that wasn&#x27;t possible, or at least thought he had a few years left before he declined to that point.</p>
<p>He&#x27;d had his world rocked thoroughly, so that now he didn&#x27;t know what to think. Sapped of confidence, he&#x27;d come here to lick his wounds. Oh, yeah, I know just what your deal is. I&#x27;ve seen it a hundred times before. None of them quite so much a cut above the rest as you, but all strong enough in their own right. The look of a big, unbeatable guy despairing after someone cut them down a notch or two isn&#x27;t a sight I&#x27;ll soon forget. The thing is, just &#x27;cause you&#x27;re feeling down doesn&#x27;t mean all&#x27;s lost, buddy.</p>
<p>This guy was still a master of his craft. I had no doubt I could use him.</p>
<p>&quot;Explain,&quot; he demanded, so at last, I opened my mouth. There was so much I had to say to him. After the Man-God gave me his profile, I&#x27;d worked out a bit of a speech. Hence why I stayed mum till now. Guys like him, they really fly off the handle like they&#x27;re having a fit when you start jabbering on trying to smooth talk &#x27;em. The art of talking&#x27;s all in making sure you&#x27;re clear and to the point.</p>
<p>&quot;First off… Right, yeah, so I&#x27;m here as a delegate for the Man-God.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Dele-what?&quot;</p>
<p>Say what? You ain&#x27;t heard &#x27;delegate&#x27; before? Man, I can&#x27;t abide unlearned types… Yeesh, all right, ya got me. I didn&#x27;t go to school, either.</p>
<p>&quot;Look, the Man-God will grant your dreams. In return, he&#x27;s got a teensy favor to ask. He&#x27;s gathering allies. I&#x27;m here as I guess what you&#x27;d call the errand boy, getting the band together.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Hah, dreams, eh…?&quot; he said. &quot;You and your boss know what my dream is, then?&quot; He stroked the hilt of his sword.</p>
<p>the fancy struck him that sword would be out before I could blink and then my head could kiss bye-bye to my body. Or maybe it&#x27;d be my left and right eyes making their farewells. This dude&#x27;s body language was telling me loud and clear: if I didn&#x27;t talk seriously, I was dead. If I gave an answer he didn&#x27;t like, also dead.</p>
<p>Fortunately, I knew what his dream was. The Man-God told me everything ahead of time. I knew why this sad-sack loser was lurking by himself out here. If that info was wrong, though… I mean, it&#x27;d be just like him to tell me wrong.</p>
<p>Oh, Holy Man-God, do not forsake me. Even I, Your humble servant, won&#x27;t find it funny if I die here.</p>
<p>&quot;The Dragon God Orsted,&quot; I said. It felt like the temperature around us plummeted, but that told me that I was bang on the money. If he had no reaction that I could pick up on, I&#x27;d be as good as dead. We were officially in business. I&#x27;d told him something I shouldn&#x27;t know. While his mind reeled from the shock, I kept talking so he didn&#x27;t have a chance to start thinking again.</p>
<p>&quot;You wanna defeat the Dragon God Orsted. He beat you once, long ago, so you trained to become the strongest there is, and after a fashion you got there. But then, you found yourself chained up by the restrictions you set on yourself, no longer even trying to go after your goal. Your ultimate enemy. The Man-God&#x27;s going after Orsted too.</p>
<p>&quot;Only, well, He&#x27;s not going for glory; he just wants him dead. By any means necessary, eh? And you&#x27;re the means, see? Only…sorry, mate, but you don&#x27;t have a chance all on your lonesome. I&#x27;ll be inviting a few more to the party.</p>
<p>&quot;Whoa there, don&#x27;t glare at me like that! Was anything I said just now wrong? You know full well you&#x27;re no match against Orsted alone.</p>
<p>&quot;But I reckon you want to try, eh? All this time, you&#x27;ve wanted it. Otherwise you&#x27;d never have run off from your house where you lived all those years, left behind everything you depended on all that time, ditched your family to come live like a vagabond out here. You coulda had a cushy job in government. You coulda gone wherever you pleased. Am I wrong? Eh?</p>
<p>&quot;So what I&#x27;m offering you is the right to challenge Orsted. You could roam around out here till the day you die and you might never run into him.
