<p><strong>A</strong>FEW DAYS had passed since I took Roxy as my wife. Lately, my fear that another disaster was about to strike had gradually begun to fade. The future seemed brighter than that, though I had still had a lot of concerns about Zenith.</p>

With the current medical technology in this world, there really was nothing they could do to restore her memories. Perhaps it was because of healing magic that the medical treatment in this world was so unbalanced.</p>
<p>Regardless, we took steps to draw up a rehabilitation plan formulated specifically for someone with amnesia. I didn&#x27;t know if it would help, but it was better than doing nothing. If I had the opportunity, it might be a good idea to search for a magical implement that could help with recovering memories. Granted, I had no idea if such a thing even existed.</p>

<p>As for my other wife, Roxy had taken up one of the smaller rooms on the second floor. The smallest, to be exact. I suggested she pick a more spacious one, but she apparently liked cramped spaces, which I understood. I didn&#x27;t mind them, either.</p>
<p>Roxy became a professor at the university. At the same time, I went around introducing her to everyone and announcing my return, but we&#x27;ll save that story for another time.</p>

<p>Another month passed, and finally, on a day with heavy snow, Sylphie gave birth. It was a normal delivery with no real complications. Neither breech nor premature. The only issue was that the blizzard outside was so strong that the doctor we called couldn&#x27;t make it in time. <p>As someone experienced in delivering babies, she was able to move swiftly, with Aisha as her assistant, never asking me for a thing. If an emergency arose, healing magic would be our ace in the hole.</p>
<p>Although, notedly, my nerves were completely shot. Healing wasn&#x27;t even in my head at that point. It was all I could do to grip Sylphie&#x27;s hand in mine as her face contorted in pain.</p>
<p>&quot;Seeing you like this brings back memories of when the mistress gave birth to Norn,&quot; said Lilia.</p>
<p>That gave me flashbacks, too. Norn had been a breech baby, with both mother and baby in danger during delivery. Paul had been useless, completely choked up. I&#x27;d managed to keep my cool and assist the delivery back then, but look at me now. I&#x27;d been much more capable in the past than I was now—not much different than how I&#x27;d been in my previous world.</p>
<p>&quot;Don&#x27;t worry, Mistress Sylphie will be just fine. There&#x27;s no need to stress,&quot; Lilia said as she worked briskly, handling everything with such practiced expertise that I was floored.</p>
<p>But no matter how she tried to soothe my nerves, my mind wouldn&#x27;t settle. The only thing I could do was cling to Sylphie&#x27;s hand and say, &quot;Breathe in…and out. In…and out,&quot; wiping the sweat from her brow as I did so.</p>
<p>The anguish on her face was clear, even as she giggled in the face of my panic. &quot;Um… Rudy, you can relax a little, you know?&quot;</p>
<p>Aisha snorted with a laugh of her own, which earned her a swift smack from Lilia.</p>
<p>Sylphie watched the two of them and giggled again.</p>
<p>&quot;Ngh?!&quot;</p>
<p>Just as the room seemed to relax, the first wave came.</p>
<p>&quot;Mistress Sylphie, we&#x27;re ready now. Push!&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Nnnngh…&quot;</p>
<p>I watched quietly as she struggled. The only thing I could say was, &quot;You can do this.&quot; I felt like there was something I should be doing, too, but there wasn&#x27;t anything I could do.</p>
<p>Sylphie matched Lilia&#x27;s calls to push, her face clenching each time, until…</p>
<p>The baby was born.</p>
<p>She let forth a fierce cry as she was delivered safely into our world. A little girl—an adorable one with the same hair color as me. Lilia lifted her up and handed her to Sylphie, who held the newborn tight and sighed with relief.</p>
<p>&quot;I&#x27;m so glad… Her hair&#x27;s not green,&quot; she whispered.</p>
<p>I mussed Sylphie&#x27;s hair—hair that had once been green but was now a beautiful white.</p>
<p>&quot;Yeah.&quot;</p>
<p>Even if our baby had been born with green hair, I wouldn&#x27;t have blamed Sylphie for it. How could I? Green was my favorite color in this world; the color of both Sylphie&#x27;s hair and Ruijerd&#x27;s. Even Roxy&#x27;s, in the right lighting, would shine emerald. I loved green. <p>&quot;You did wonderful, Sylphie.&quot;</p>

<p>While I had the resolve to love green hair, the rest of the world did not, considering it an ill omen. I thanked God for our good fortune that my daughter had the same hair color as me. <p>&quot;Here, Rudy. Hold her,&quot; said Sylphie.</p>
<p>&quot;Okay.&quot;</p>
<p>I took her in my arms. Her body was warm, her voice fierce as she cried. Her head was tiny, along with her mouth and nose—her entire body overflowing with life. My heart flooded with emotion when I thought about how this little girl was mine, my baby that Sylphie had given birth to.</p>
<p>&quot;…&quot;</p>
<p>Tears sprung up.</p>
<p>Paul was gone, but now we had a baby. He&#x27;d saved my life. If it hadn&#x27;t been for him, I wouldn&#x27;t be here holding my child. But in exchange, Paul would never again hold his own wife, his own daughters, or his grandchild.</p>
<p>Would he be bitter that he couldn&#x27;t be here? Or would he laugh and boast, &quot;This was all thanks to me&quot;?</p>
<p>Either way, I had to keep living. For my child&#x27;s sake, I couldn&#x27;t die. I had to protect Sylphie—my family.</p>
<p>Sylphie and I took the first two letters of our names and altered them slightly to come up with her name: Lucy. Lucy Greyrat. Aisha laughed, calling it a cheap name, and Lilia smacked her over the head again. I was just glad she was a girl. If we&#x27;d had a boy instead, I might have named him Paul.</p>
