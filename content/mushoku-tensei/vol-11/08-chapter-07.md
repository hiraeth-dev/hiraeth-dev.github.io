<p><strong>L</strong>IFE COMES AT YOU fast sometimes.</p>
<p>I was doing my usual training routine one pleasant summer morning, feeling good about things in general. I hadn&#x27;t seen Badigadi around for months but wasn&#x27;t too concerned. The man was impulsive at the best of times, so there wasn&#x27;t much point worrying about him.</p>
<p>That was what Elinalise always said, at least. And it had proven to be true so far.</p>
<p>When I finished up and went back into the house, I found Aisha and Sylphie in the hallway with serious expressions on their faces. They turned to look at me as I stepped in the door.</p>
<p>&quot;Oh…&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Rudy…&quot;</p>
<p>Something about the atmosphere in here was making me nervous. Did we have a problem or something?</p>
<p>&quot;Err…&quot; said Sylphie, scratching at the back of her ears with an awkward smile. &quot;Haha, wow. This is making me more nervous than I expected…&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;There&#x27;s no reason to hesitate, Sylphie!&quot; said Aisha. &quot;Go on! Be brave!&quot;</p>
<p>My wife stepped forward. After a moment&#x27;s hesitation, she crossed her hands in front of her stomach and spoke. &quot;Well, Rudy. It&#x27;s actually…been two months now. Since my last, uh, you know…&quot;</p>
<p><em>Her last…? Oh. Oh, wow.</em></p>
<p>&quot;And, well, I haven&#x27;t been feeling so well lately, and I was starting to wonder.&quot;</p>
<p>I couldn&#x27;t help myself from staring at Sylphie&#x27;s stomach. It didn&#x27;t look any different at the moment. Was this really happening?</p>
<p>&quot;So I went with Aisha to the neighborhood doctor, and…they said, uh, congratulations.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Oh… Ohhh…&quot;</p>
<p>My voice was trembling. So were my hands. And my legs, for that matter.</p>
<p>Congratulations? She was pregnant? We were actually going to have a kid. This wasn&#x27;t a dream, right?</p>
<p>An experimental pinch of my cheek made me wince. So much for that theory.</p>
<p>I swallowed loudly.</p>
<p>Right. Of course. Why wouldn&#x27;t she be pregnant? I was a man who could make things happen, when I really set my mind to it. This had always been part of the plan. I just hadn&#x27;t been expecting it to happen so quickly, since everyone said elves had a hard time getting pregnant.</p>
<p>I was a little startled—that was all.</p>
<p>&quot;Uhm, Rudy…any thoughts?&quot;</p>
<p>Sylphie was looking at me anxiously. I wasn&#x27;t sure what to say, though. This was all so sudden.</p>
<p>&quot;Can I, uh…touch your stomach?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Huh? Err, sure. Go ahead.&quot;</p>
<p>I reached down and stroked Sylphie&#x27;s belly. It was still slender, with no additional fat that I could feel. Her skin was warm to the touch and surprisingly soft. The same as always, in other words. But when I focused closely, I felt like I could feel a <em>slight</em> hint of a bump.</p>
<p>That was probably just my imagination, right? The kid wouldn&#x27;t be that big yet.</p>
<p>&quot;Right… Our kid&#x27;s in here…&quot;</p>
<p>When I spoke those words out loud, I felt a sudden surge of emotion building up inside me. What was this feeling? I had to repress the urge to start shouting incoherently.</p>
<p>I had a <em>kid</em> on the way. I was going to be a dad.</p>
<p>It didn&#x27;t feel real yet. But it still made me incredibly happy.</p>
<p>Was happy even the right term? The word felt so inadequate. What <em>was</em> I feeling right now? Could you even put this into words?</p>
<p>&quot;Brother dear? Isn&#x27;t there anything you&#x27;d like to say to your wife?&quot; Aisha&#x27;s words snapped me back to reality.</p>
<p>&quot;Muh?&quot;</p>
<p><em>Something I need to say? Like what? Congratulations? No, that can&#x27;t be right.</em></p>
<p><em>Maybe I should thank her. Yeah, that sounds better.</em></p>
<p>&quot;Thank you, Sylphie.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Huh?&quot;</p>
<p>Sylphie smiled, but she looked a little puzzled. Had I guessed wrong? What was the answer, then? I searched my memory for a hint. What had Paul said to Zenith, back when we found out Norn was on the way? Something like &quot;Well done,&quot; right? Or maybe &quot;Nice job!&quot;</p>
<p>I didn&#x27;t like those options very much, though. Did he think women only got pregnant when they tried really hard or something? Maybe. Maybe he <em>was</em> that stupid.</p>
<p><em>…Pregnant, huh? Yeah. Sylphie&#x27;s pregnant. I got this sweet, beautiful girl pregnant. Me, of all people.</em></p>
<p>The more I thought about it, the more my emotions threatened to overwhelm me. I was actually starting to tear up.</p>
<p>&quot;I&#x27;m sorry… I don&#x27;t, uh…think I know what to say. Sorry, Sylphie…&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Oof! Uhm, Rudy?&quot;</p>
<p>Instead of continuing, I&#x27;d thrown my arms around Sylphie. I wanted to lift her up into the air and spin her around a few times for good measure, but this wasn&#x27;t the time for that. She had a baby in her belly. I needed to be very, very gentle with her.</p>
<p>&quot;Hehehe. You did want children pretty badly, didn&#x27;t you?&quot;</p>
<p>My wife wrapped her arms around me as well, and started to pat me on the back.</p>

<p>I gave her one more gentle squeeze, then finally released her. Stepping back, I stared down into her eyes. I could see my face reflected in them, and it wasn&#x27;t a pretty sight. I had tears running down my cheeks.</p>
<p>Sylphie closed her eyes. I kissed her and stroked her hair, enjoying the softness of her lips. This was what love felt like, wasn&#x27;t it?</p>
<p>&quot;Ahem.&quot;</p>
<p>Aisha cleared her throat, reminding me that we weren&#x27;t alone in the room. I&#x27;d started groping Sylphie&#x27;s breasts <em>and</em> butt without even realizing it.</p>
<p>&quot;Brother dear, we need to be gentle with the lady of the house for a while. You&#x27;re going to need to refrain from…intercourse for the moment.&quot;</p>
<p><em>She&#x27;s right. Bad Rudeus! Bad!</em></p>
<p>No matter how lovable my wife was, I needed to control myself from now on. Then again…she was less than two months pregnant, right? And we&#x27;d been doing it every three days up until now. It probably wouldn&#x27;t hurt to keep going just a little longer…</p>
<p><em>No! No. Keep it together, man.</em></p>
<p>&quot;Right. Of course.&quot;</p>
<p>Aisha smiled and lifted the hem of her skirt slightly upward. &quot;If you&#x27;re desperate, I&#x27;m always available to pick up the slack.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Not a chance in hell, kiddo.&quot;</p>
<p>She pouted a little at that. It was nice of her to offer and all, but even putting all the moral issues aside, I just wasn&#x27;t attracted to her. That suited me fine, anyway. The last thing I needed was to destroy my marriage by messing with the maid.</p>
<p>&quot;Well, then, brother dear, I&#x27;m going to go inform Princess Ariel of this development. I expect Miss Sylphie will need to put her work on hold for some time, after all.&quot;</p>
<p>That hadn&#x27;t even occurred to me, but she was right. You wouldn&#x27;t want a pregnant woman working as a bodyguard. Sylphie was going to need a leave of absence.</p>
<p>&quot;I&#x27;ll go,&quot; I said. &quot;I should really explain the situation myself.&quot;</p>
<p>Aisha sighed at me. &quot;Rudeus, you <em>really</em> need to stay with Sylphie for the moment. You&#x27;ve got lots to talk about, remember?&quot;</p>
<p><em>Do we? Oh, right. I guess we do. This kind of changes everything, after all.</em></p>
<p>&quot;With that settled, I&#x27;ll be going now.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Right. Okay. Thanks, Aisha.&quot;</p>
<p>My little sister left the house in high spirits, leaving me and Sylphie behind by ourselves.</p>
<p>A few minutes later, the two of us were sitting next to each other on the sofa.</p>
<p>I cautiously reached out to take Sylphie&#x27;s hand. She squeezed mine back and leaned her head against my shoulder. Neither of us said anything for a while.</p>
<p>I wasn&#x27;t sure where to begin, honestly.</p>
<p>The only words that were coming to me were variations of &quot;I&#x27;ll take responsibility for my actions.&quot; But we were already married, so that didn&#x27;t make much sense.</p>
<p>&quot;Uhm, Sylphie…&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Yes, Rudy?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I know this might be tough, but…we&#x27;ll do it together.&quot;</p>
<p>Laughing softly, Sylphie laid down on the couch and put her head in my lap. I used my free hand to stroke her head and rub behind her ears.</p>
<p>&quot;Hey, Rudy.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Yeah?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Do you want a boy or a girl?&quot;</p>
<p>The question took me by surprise. I&#x27;d almost forgotten babies came in two varieties.</p>
<p>&quot;I mean, not that we really get to choose,&quot; Sylphie added, smiling gently.</p>
<p>Hmm. Which <em>would</em> be better?</p>
<p>Maybe a boy would be nice, just to have an heir to the family? But it wasn&#x27;t like I was the head of some feudal clan or anything. We could pass down everything to a girl just as easily…not that we even had much of a fortune to be inherited at the moment.</p>
<p>Back in my old life, I probably would have said, &quot;A girl!&quot; with a creepy grin on my face. Maybe even suggested that we take pictures of her every single day to record her growth into adulthood. What a very foolish man I used to be.</p>
<p>Right now, though, I couldn&#x27;t find any reason to prefer one over the other. As long as it was a healthy, happy kid, I&#x27;d be satisfied either way.</p>
<p>&quot;You know, Rudy, I&#x27;m kind of relieved.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Why?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;It feels like I&#x27;m <em>really</em> your wife now.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;…&quot;</p>
<p>Just like in my old world, having children was a major reason why people got married here. Sylphie had probably been a little anxious about that part of things, since it was harder for her people to get pregnant. Not that I would have left her over something like that, of course.</p>
<p>&quot;Anyway, I guess this is going to be kind of rough on you too, huh?&quot; she said. &quot;Since we can&#x27;t, uh, do it for a while.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Hey, I&#x27;ll live.&quot;</p>
<p>I could put up with a dry spell under <em>these</em> circumstances. Unlike certain old men I could mention.</p>
<p>&quot;Feel free to kick me out of the house for good if I go off and sleep with another woman, all right? I&#x27;d deserve it,&quot; I said.</p>
<p>&quot;…Oh, I don&#x27;t think I&#x27;d be that angry. Maybe a little sad. But I&#x27;d understand.&quot;</p>
<p>Really? That seemed like an awfully mild reaction. I wasn&#x27;t going to betray her or anything, though. I knew I&#x27;d feel like total crap if <em>she</em> went out and cheated on me.</p>
<p>&quot;I think I&#x27;d get upset if you messed around with another guy, to be honest,&quot; I confessed.</p>
<p>Sylphie just laughed softly and smiled. It was an expression she only ever wore around me. No one else ever got to see it. And that made me really happy.</p>
<p>We spent some quiet time together.</p>
<p>In the evening, Aisha came back to our house with Norn in tow.</p>
<p>&quot;C-congratulations, Sylphie,&quot; said Norn, bowing politely.</p>
<p>&quot;Thanks, Norn,&quot; said Sylphie, smiling as she patted her on the head.</p>
<p>That got Norn smiling as well. She didn&#x27;t seem to mind being petted as much as you might think. Maybe she actually enjoyed it, coming from the right person. In any case, it was nice to see the two of them getting along so well.</p>
<p>&quot;Everyone wanted to come by and congratulate you, but I convinced them to postpone their visits for a few days,&quot; said Aisha in a calm tone of voice. She&#x27;d apparently assumed I would want to keep this an intimate family occasion for today.</p>
<p>I didn&#x27;t remember suggesting anything of the kind, but it seemed reasonable enough. Sylphie would probably be a little embarrassed or overwhelmed to have a lot of people congratulating all her at once. It was better to give it a few days.</p>
<p>&quot;Princess Ariel indicated that Miss Sylphie is <em>expected</em> to take a break from her duties for at least two years. She also said she would arrange for a leave of absence from the school. Great-Aunt Elinalise has volunteered to assume Sylphie&#x27;s bodyguard duties in the interim.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Is Grandma really going to be okay? I mean, she has that curse and everything…&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;She assured me that she could manage, Madam. I wouldn&#x27;t worry about her.&quot;</p>
<p>Elinalise did know how to take care of herself, and she had that magical implement now. Besides, she could always pull Cliff into an empty classroom or storage room if she <em>needed</em> to get busy during school hours.</p>
<p>&quot;Prince Zanoba says he&#x27;ll be paying us a visit five days from now, in the evening. He wanted to have dinner with us, so I&#x27;ll get things ready for that. Princess Ariel will be stopping by in ten days, also at night, but she indicated that she wouldn&#x27;t be dining with us. Cliff and Great-Aunt Elinalise will be coming along on that visit. Miss Linia and Miss Pursena indicated they&#x27;d wander by sometime, but I don&#x27;t have any details as to when that might be. Miss Nanahoshi offered a brief message of congratulations to you both. I wasn&#x27;t able to find Lord Badigadi, but I left a message for him.&quot;</p>
<p>Aisha had rattled off the whole list of our friends quickly and efficiently, in a steady tone of voice. It was like we had a personal secretary or something. The girl was definitely good at her job.</p>
<p>&quot;Got it. Thanks for letting everyone know, Aisha.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Of course, brother dear.&quot;</p>
<p>Aisha looked over at Norn with a prideful smirk on her face. Norn met her gaze with a frown.</p>
<p>Aisha still seemed to take a certain degree of malicious joy in showing her sister up like this. There was some lingering conflict between them involving their positions in the family. I was always telling Aisha that she was an equal member of the family, and that it didn&#x27;t matter if she had a different mother…but the two of them still fought constantly, over the most minor things.</p>
<p>They did say squabbling with someone can be a sign of how close you are. It was probably okay to let things be, as long as this didn&#x27;t turn into a cold war. They never said anything <em>really</em> cruel to each other when they fought, at least.</p>
<p>&quot;I&#x27;ve got to say, though,&quot; I murmured, &quot;Dad&#x27;s probably going to be shocked when he shows up and finds out I&#x27;ve got a kid coming.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Oh, yeah!&quot; said Norn, her face lighting up at the mention of Paul. She really did love her dad. I could see her putting down &quot;marrying my daddy&quot; on her list of dreams for the future. &quot;I can&#x27;t wait to see the look on his face!&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;He&#x27;s the type to spoil his grandkids rotten, so I bet he&#x27;ll be overjoyed,&quot; I said. &quot;He was really sweet when you two were born too.&quot;</p>
<p>Aisha and Norn both looked a little nonplussed for a moment. Neither of them had any memory of those days, of course.</p>
<p>&quot;Well, anyway! I&#x27;m really looking forward to it, Rudeus!&quot; Norn announced. Those unusually cheerful words put a smile on everyone&#x27;s face.</p>
<p>Sylphie and I were happily married. Paul and Zenith and Lilia would be with us soon enough. And my little sisters were here too. That was the life I&#x27;d dreamed of way back in the Buena Village days, and it was close at hand.</p>
<p>The <em>bad</em> news arrived two months later.</p>
<p>I received a letter, dated six months in the past. It had been sent by express delivery post. The sender&#x27;s name was Geese. And the contents, as was usual with express letters, were very brief.</p>
<p>&quot;Having trouble rescuing Zenith. Requesting help.&quot;</p>
<p>The instant I saw those words, the world flashed white before my eyes.</p>
<p>***</p>
<p>When I came to, I found myself in a pure white space. I&#x27;d been transformed back into the foul person I used to be, and felt a surge of anger and resentment wash over me.</p>
<p>I stared sullenly at the figure in front of me. It was the smiling Man-God, whose face was nothing but a blur.</p>
<p>&quot;Hey, there.&quot;</p>
<p><em>What the hell is going on?</em></p>
<p>&quot;What are you talking about?&quot;</p>
<p><em>That letter. The one from Geese. He said the rescue isn&#x27;t going well. What&#x27;s the deal?</em></p>
<p>&quot;Well, I expect it means the rescue isn&#x27;t going well. What do you want from me?&quot;</p>
<p><em>But that&#x27;s not what you told me! You said I&#x27;d regret it if I went to the Begaritt Continent! What was all that about, then? Were you lying to me?!</em></p>
<p>&quot;No, of course not. You&#x27;ll regret it if you go to the Begaritt Continent. It was true back then, and it&#x27;s still true now.&quot;</p>
<p><em>Ah, now I see. I get it. I&#x27;ll regret it if I go to the Begaritt Continent, but I&#x27;ll also regret it if I don&#x27;t go. Is that what you meant all along?</em></p>
<p>&quot;Oh, I don&#x27;t know about that. You weren&#x27;t too unhappy with your life as of yesterday, were you? You made lots of friends here. You met many interesting people, and you did a lot of growing up. Your condition was cured, you made friends with your little sisters, you even got married! And now you&#x27;ve got a kid on the way.&quot;</p>
<p><em>…Yeah, my life&#x27;s not bad right now. But that&#x27;s not the point! You told me not to go to Begaritt! You tricked me!</em></p>
<p>&quot;I really didn&#x27;t, though. Let me repeat myself one more time: If you go to the Begaritt Continent, you will definitely regret it.&quot;</p>
<p><em>What? But my family&#x27;s in trouble! Tell me why, at least!</em> &quot;Can&#x27;t do that, I&#x27;m afraid.&quot;</p>
<p><em>Damn it! I should have known better. You&#x27;re always like this!</em></p>
<p>&quot;You&#x27;re being awful harsh today. My advice has always proven helpful, hasn&#x27;t it?&quot;</p>
<p><em>Maybe, but that doesn&#x27;t change the fact that you misled me this time. Look, can you at least give me some of the details? What am I going to end up regretting? I can&#x27;t make this decision unless I know the risks and rewards!</em></p>
<p>&quot;Most people have to make their decisions blind, you know. You&#x27;re awfully demanding.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Well, if you actually think it through, a <em>few</em> of the consequences should be obvious. You spent the last year and a half as a student, right? <p><em>Tch.</em></p>

<p>&quot;Ahem. Rudeus, remain in Ranoa until the next mating season comes around. Linia and Pursena will pursue you aggressively. Choose one of them and begin a relationship with her. This will bring you greater happiness in the end.&quot;</p>
<p><em>What the hell?! You&#x27;re telling me to cheat on my wife now?! I&#x27;m happy with Sylphie! And those two are just good friends, damn it!</em></p>

<p>***</p>
<p>I woke up to find myself in bed. Sylphie was looking down at me with concern on her face.</p>
<p>&quot;Oh, Rudy! Are you okay? You were moaning in your sleep.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Yeah, I&#x27;m fine…&quot;</p>
<p>What had happened after I received that letter? I couldn&#x27;t remember the specifics very well. I recalled staring at the page in a stupor, but nothing else except my dream.</p>
<p>Things had been going too smoothly lately. I guess the shock had hit me hard.</p>
<p>Geese&#x27;s letter was alarming. Something had obviously gone wrong. Still, I had the Man-God&#x27;s words to consider. If I set out on now, there was a chance my family and I would pass each other on the road and I&#x27;d waste a couple years of my life.</p>
<p>Maybe this was too optimistic, but…there was a <em>chance</em> that Geese had just sent out that letter in a moment of panic. I mean, it wasn&#x27;t Paul who had written to me. It was Geese. My monkey-faced cellmate.</p>
<p>Why would <em>he</em> write me a letter like this? Because he was trying to rescue Zenith too? But Paul&#x27;s last letter hadn&#x27;t mentioned him. It seemed likely Geese had found Zenith on his own.</p>
<p>The letter was written six months ago. It was possible he&#x27;d been alone and feeling helpless at the time but had met up with Paul and the others since then. Maybe he&#x27;d even sent a similar letter to Paul.</p>
<p>They might have joined forces and rescued my mother a couple weeks later.</p>
<p>All of these were just possibilities, of course. I had absolutely no way of knowing what the situation really was. Not from this great a distance.</p>
<p>There was Sylphie&#x27;s child to think of too. No matter how fast you traveled, it had to be a solid year&#x27;s journey to reach the Begaritt Continent. I knew the road down to East Port from my last big journey, so it was possible I could cut the travel time down significantly. But even if I somehow managed to get there in six months, I wouldn&#x27;t be back home for at least a year.</p>
<p>That wasn&#x27;t going to work, right? I couldn&#x27;t just leave my pregnant wife all alone to go on some adventure.</p>
<p>&quot;This is about that letter, isn&#x27;t it?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;…&quot;</p>
<p>I couldn&#x27;t bring myself to answer. I&#x27;d promised Sylphie I wouldn&#x27;t disappear on her again. I&#x27;d given her my word.</p>
<p>It wouldn&#x27;t technically be &quot;disappearing&quot; if I explained everything beforehand. But that was just semantics. Even if we talked it through beforehand—or if I left her a thorough letter—it would still be agonizing for her to be left behind.</p>
<p>&quot;Uhm, Rudy…you don&#x27;t have to worry about me too much, okay? I&#x27;ve got Aisha here to take care of me.&quot;</p>
<p>There was just a hint of anguish on Sylphie&#x27;s face. She was anxious, of course. This was her first pregnancy. Her belly was already getting bigger by the day. Sooner or later, it would be hard for her just to get up and down the stairs. And there was a chance I might die on this journey. I might never come back to her.</p>
<p>She&#x27;d fought down that fear to speak those words.</p>
<p>When I said that, she smiled, though she still looked a little conflicted.</p>
<p>The words of the Man-God continued to linger in my mind. No matter what choice I made, he&#x27;d insisted, I was going to end up regretting it.</p>
<p>The next three days were long and difficult.</p>
<p>Every time I saw them, Sylphie, Aisha, and Norn had anxious looks on their faces. I&#x27;d already told them I wasn&#x27;t going to the Begaritt Continent, but the more I thought about it, the more uncertain I felt. I was torn between my two choices, and there weren&#x27;t that many people I could turn to for advice.</p>
<p>The first, Elinalise, nodded when I told her of my intentions. &quot;I think that&#x27;s wise, Rudeus. You&#x27;re better off staying behind for this one.&quot;</p>
<p>The way she phrased that surprised me. It suggested she had other plans. &quot;Are <em>you</em> going to go, Elinalise?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Sylphie&#x27;s my granddaughter, Rudeus. It&#x27;s only right that I take this job, for her sake as much as yours.&quot;</p>
<p>Apparently, she&#x27;d received an identical letter herself. And unlike me, she was ready and willing to go, even if it meant leaving her life here behind.</p>
<p>&quot;Aren&#x27;t you supposed to be guarding Princess Ariel, though?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;There&#x27;s very little real danger to her life while she&#x27;s enrolled in this school. I wasn&#x27;t doing much of anything, to be honest.&quot;</p>
<p>That was probably true <em>most</em> of the time, but you never knew when things might take a dangerous turn. That was the whole point of having bodyguards. But of course, that was Ariel&#x27;s decision to make, and Elinalise had basically volunteered herself as a goodwill gesture. I doubted the princess would object to her backing out.</p>
<p>&quot;What about Cliff?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I&#x27;ll have to leave him. He might hate me forever, but I don&#x27;t have much choice.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Why don&#x27;t you at least explain the situation? I&#x27;m sure he&#x27;d understand.&quot;</p>
<p>Elinalise shook her head with a melancholy smile. It didn&#x27;t look much like her usual smirk.</p>
<p>&quot;Cliff&#x27;s a pure-hearted young man. He has talent, drive, and vision. I wouldn&#x27;t be surprised if he becomes the pope one day. He&#x27;s better off remembering me as nothing but a youthful indiscretion.&quot;</p>
<p>Well, that just made me feel terrible for the guy.</p>
<p>Members of the Millis church were expected to stay loyal to a single person. If Elinalise just disappeared, it might shake the very foundations of Cliff&#x27;s faith. He was a strong-willed person, but it was hard to know what losing his religion might do to him.</p>
<p>&quot;And also…I&#x27;m the one who told you to stay here last time. That makes cleaning up this mess my responsibility, don&#x27;t you think?&quot;</p>
<p>Elinalise&#x27;s words were so firm and clear that I found myself at a loss for words.</p>
<p>Apparently taking that as agreement, she nodded. &quot;You just leave this to me and wait here, dear. I want to see a happy greatgrandchild waiting for me when I make it back.&quot;</p>
<p>It was clear that nothing I could say was going to change her mind.</p>
<p>Next, I turned to Zanoba for advice. His expression didn&#x27;t even change as I laid out the story.</p>
<p>&quot;I see,&quot; he said calmly. &quot;Well, I&#x27;m sure you&#x27;ll deal with this matter easily enough and be back before too long. I will remain here and continue to pursue my research, but I do hope you&#x27;ll return as quickly as possible.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I kind of thought you&#x27;d ask me not to go, Zanoba. Or demand that I bring you along.&quot;</p>
<p>When we parted back in the Shirone Kingdom, he&#x27;d wept and hung all over me. A part of me had been hoping for something similar. But this time, his attitude was very different.</p>
<p>&quot;Should you wish for me to accompany you, I would be loath to refuse. But I&#x27;m unaccustomed to lengthy journeys, and I fear I might prove a burden. And of course…&quot; He shot a glance over at Julie. &quot;I couldn&#x27;t bring the girl on such a trip.&quot;</p>
<p>Julie was still a young child. Leaving her here in Ginger&#x27;s care was an option, but that would mean putting their studies and research on hold. And if she came along instead, it would be dangerous to have her keep exhausting herself by using up all her mana.</p>
<p>&quot;Do you think I <em>should</em> go, Zanoba?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;That is your decision to make, Master.&quot;</p>
<p>He sounded almost dismissive now. I&#x27;d been hoping for some actual advice…</p>
<p>&quot;However, if I may make one observation?&quot; he said.</p>
<p>&quot;Hmm?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;The birth of a child does not <em>require</em> a father&#x27;s presence. If you&#x27;re worried about your parents, why not go to their aid? I&#x27;ll guarantee the safety of your wife and sisters in your absence.&quot;</p>
<p>There was real conviction in Zanoba&#x27;s words. It made sense that royalty would have a different perspective on this sort of thing, though. Most kings probably didn&#x27;t rush to watch their concubines giving birth.</p>
<p>&quot;I would of course prefer to have you at my side constantly,&quot; he said, &quot;but the choice is yours.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;You make some decent points, Zanoba. Thanks for the advice.&quot;</p>
<p>Sylphie wasn&#x27;t on her own here. She had Aisha, Zanoba, and Princess Ariel&#x27;s retinue.</p>
<p>She wasn&#x27;t alone. <em>We</em> weren&#x27;t alone.</p>
<p>What was I supposed to do at the end of the day? Stay or go?</p>
<p>Elinalise wanted me to wait here while she went to Begaritt alone. Zanoba wanted me to go, leaving matters here in his hands. Which path made more sense? Where was I <em>most</em> needed right now?</p>
<p>Zanoba&#x27;s logic seemed sound. As long as Sylphie stayed healthy, everything would be just fine. My presence wasn&#x27;t going to make a difference. Still, that attitude didn&#x27;t sit right with me. I wasn&#x27;t a king, and I didn&#x27;t want to act like one. It was obviously better for Sylphie to have me here, providing emotional support.</p>
<p>Sylphie had encouraged me to go, and told me not to worry…but this was her first pregnancy. Deep down, I knew she had to be terrified. She was probably fighting the desire to break down and beg me not to leave.</p>
<p>I was the one who&#x27;d told her how I wanted kids over and over again. I might not even have been that serious about it at the time, but she&#x27;d obviously <em>taken</em> it seriously. And now that she was actually pregnant, I was thinking about leaving her behind while I traveled halfway around the world. That felt like a serious betrayal.</p>
<p>On the other hand…I had to admit that I&#x27;d been putting off my responsibility to help Paul for a long time now. I&#x27;d put my own happiness first for years. Hell, I&#x27;d prioritized fixing my &quot;performance&quot; problems over the search for my mother.</p>
<p>Maybe this was a wake-up call. Maybe it was finally time for me to pay the piper.</p>
<p>…I couldn&#x27;t make up my mind. Both options would cost me greatly.</p>
<p>It was now the fourth day after the letter&#x27;s arrival. I&#x27;d spent most of that time brooding over my dilemma. I wasn&#x27;t sleeping well at all, and I couldn&#x27;t motivate myself to bother with my usual training routine that morning. I was just sitting around on the first floor, bleary-eyed, doing nothing in particular.</p>
<p>The mornings were chilly here, even in summer, and I was feeling downright sluggish. For a while, I just watched the sun rise.</p>
<p>&quot;…Oh!&quot;</p>
<p>After a while, I heard a small cry of surprise from behind me. Turning around, I saw that our front door was open, and Norn was standing in front of it. She had a large bag on her back—the same one I&#x27;d used in my days as an adventurer. It was packed to bursting point.</p>
<p>She was obviously preparing for a lengthy trip. But because she was only ten years old, it looked more like she was going on a picnic or something.</p>
<p>For a long moment, I just looked at her silently. Norn avoided my gaze. She looked like a kid who&#x27;d just been caught red-handed playing a prank on someone.</p>
<p>&quot;Where are you off to?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;…&quot;</p>
<p>Norn didn&#x27;t answer, so I repeated myself. &quot;Where are you going, Norn?&quot;</p>
<p>Biting her lip, she finally looked me in the eye. &quot;W-well… if you&#x27;re not going to help, Rudeus, I guess I have to go instead.&quot;</p>
<p>I studied her face for a moment. Go where? She couldn&#x27;t seriously mean the Begaritt Continent, could she?</p>
<p>Norn was still so <em>small</em>. This was a ten-year-old kid we were talking about here.</p>
<p>&quot;…&quot;</p>
<p>There was no way that bag had anything close to what she&#x27;d need for this trip. She probably had some money, but did she know how to spend it wisely? Did she even know the route she would take? How did she intend to deal with the dangers she&#x27;d encounter on the way? She might get kidnapped by slavers the minute she set foot out of this city.</p>
<p>&quot;Norn, I can&#x27;t let you do that,&quot; I said.</p>
<p>&quot;But I… I… Rudeus, please! Mom and Dad are in trouble!&quot; She was tearing up now, but she kept her eyes fixed on mine. &quot;Why…why aren&#x27;t you going to help them?&quot;</p>
<p>Why? Well, because I was going to have a child soon. I had my wife to think of.</p>
<p>&quot;You&#x27;re much stronger than me, Rudeus! You know how to travel! Why aren&#x27;t you going?!&quot;</p>
<p>She wasn&#x27;t wrong. I wasn&#x27;t as experienced as Elinalise, but I&#x27;d spent five years on the road as an adventurer. I had the knowhow, at least. And although there were plenty of people out there more powerful than me, I could hold my own in a fight.</p>
<p>As I was today, I could probably manage to travel across the Demon Continent even without Ruijerd&#x27;s help.</p>
<p>&quot;…&quot;</p>
<p>It was all true. I <em>could</em> do this if I wanted to.</p>
<p>I&#x27;d been weighing the pros and cons of going for days now, but that was because I could <em>afford</em> to choose. Norn didn&#x27;t have that choice. She wanted to go help, but she couldn&#x27;t. I, on the other hand, had the ability to reach the Begaritt Continent, help our parents, and make it back safely.</p>
<p>That was the whole reason Geese had sent me that letter and not someone else.</p>
<p>&quot;Okay, Norn. You&#x27;re right.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;R-Rudeus?&quot;</p>
<p>There were other people who could look after Sylphie for me. But I was the only one who could go save my parents.</p>
<p>It had to be me. I could cut through the Begaritt Continent to the city of Rapan. I could solve the problems Paul and the others had run into. There wasn&#x27;t anyone else I could entrust this job to.</p>
<p>&quot;I&#x27;ll go. Can you look after the house for me?&quot;</p>
<p>Norn&#x27;s face lit up. But an instant later, she squeezed her lips together tightly and nodded with the most serious expression she could muster. &quot;Absolutely!&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Don&#x27;t fight with Aisha. And help out Sylphie when you can, okay?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Of course!&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;All right. Good girl.&quot;</p>
<p>I felt terrible about doing this to Sylphie and our baby. If she dumped me over this one, I wouldn&#x27;t blame her. But that wasn&#x27;t how I should be thinking about this. I needed to trust my wife.</p>
<p>&quot;I&#x27;ll go to the Begaritt Continent, then.&quot;</p>
<p>I&#x27;d made up my mind now. I was going to save my parents.</p>
