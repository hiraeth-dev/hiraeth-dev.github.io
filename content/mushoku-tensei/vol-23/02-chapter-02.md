<p><strong>T</strong>HE DIVINE CONTINENT.</p>
<p>If you were to mark it on a map, it&#x27;d be on the northernmost edge, connecting the Central Continent to the Demon Continent. While it is called a continent, its land is adjoined to the Central Continent. You could even walk from it to the Demon Continent during the low tide.</p>
<p>Why would this landmass be treated as separate from both the Central and Demon Continents? Its height. This continent perched atop a steep, vertical precipice about three thousand meters above sea level.</p>
<p>People did not travel through it as a rule. It wasn&#x27;t impossible for those determined enough, but there were no roads worth mentioning. Given the winged monsters teeming within the walls of that cliff, any attempts at scaling it would be incredibly difficult. I&#x27;d heard stories about wanted fugitives from the Central Continent traveling through the Divine Continent in the hopes of reaching the Demon Continent and escaping their bounty hunters. I hadn&#x27;t heard any stories about them surviving the attempt.</p>
<p>You&#x27;d think being able to fly would make being a fugitive easy, but in this world, the sky belonged to the dragons. Forget planes; this world&#x27;s technology hadn&#x27;t even developed hot air balloons. It was the height of recklessness for someone to go into the air unprotected.</p>
<p>And this was the place Perugius wanted me to bring a month-old baby? Absurd.</p>
<p>&quot;I&#x27;d appreciate it if you could inform me of where I could find a teleportation circle linked to this Divine Continent.&quot;</p>
<p>We were back at my company&#x27;s countryside office. Eris was right behind me. Roxy and Sylphie were with Sieg in a different room. It didn&#x27;t seem like we&#x27;d be fighting Perugius at the moment, so I had Zanoba return home.</p>
<p>&quot;…&quot;</p>
<p>Orsted&#x27;s expression was as fearsome as ever, but behind that fearsome face, he seemed to be considering how to tell me a hard truth. Maybe there wasn&#x27;t a teleportation circle connected to the Divine Continent?</p>
<p>&quot;Perugius wouldn&#x27;t be satisfied if you used a teleportation circle.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Oh, that makes sense.&quot;</p>
<p>Come to think of it, Perugius did call it a &quot;trial.&quot; Perhaps giving Sieg a baptism at Aluce Hill on the Divine Continent wasn&#x27;t the only part of the trial; the perilous path toward reaching it might be important. If that meant going from here all the way to the Divine Continent on foot, we&#x27;d be wasting a lot of time.</p>
<p>&quot;Does that rule out teleporting to somewhere near the Divine Continent?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;It shouldn&#x27;t be a problem if you&#x27;re still outside of it.&quot;</p>
<p>So, bring the baby to the foot of the Divine Continent, do some rock climbing, and get him baptized by the people living at the top. A threecourse meal of tribulations. Forget the difficulty of the trek by itself, this was a one-month-old baby we&#x27;d be traveling with. He could get sick at any point along the way. At three thousand feet above sea level, altitude sickness could be a real possibility…</p>
<p>Yeah, this would be rough. Guess that&#x27;s what made it a trial, though.</p>
<p>&quot;Hmm…&quot;</p>
<p>You know, maybe bringing the floating fortress crashing down wasn&#x27;t such a bad idea.</p>
<p>&quot;Orsted, do you think I can complete this trial? With a one-monthold baby with me, that is.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Sure.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Your reasoning?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Sieghart, was it? That baby&#x27;s physique shows strong influence from the Laplace Factor. Children like that have a resistance to typical illnesses and environmental stresses.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Oh, I see.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;It&#x27;s an effect they wove into the reincarnation magic to make sure that Laplace&#x27;s future body would survive the harshest conditions. If a child has a strong Factor, they can survive the trip to the Divine Continent.&quot;</p>
<p>fine. You know, as long as I didn&#x27;t totally space out and let a roc swipe Sieg off my back and carry him away in its talons. Eris and Roxy should be coming with me, so they&#x27;d make up for any lapses in attention I could have.</p>
<p>&quot;I feel a bit guilty about asking so much. Especially right after what happened with Geese…&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I understand.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I appreciate the generosity…&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I still remember how you wiped a forest off the face of the continent to protect your family. I wouldn&#x27;t put it past you to bring Chaos Breaker crashing down before Laplace&#x27;s revival. That&#x27;s an asset, and I will need it.&quot;</p>
<p>Figures. To Orsted, both Perugius and I were combat assets. He didn&#x27;t want his pieces taking one another out.</p>
<p>&quot;I&#x27;m relieved to see you being so accommodating. We&#x27;ll get into preparations right away.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Sure.&quot;</p>
<p>With our goal made clear, I looked behind me. Eris was there, with her arms crossed like usual.</p>
<p>&quot;Is that all right with you, Eris?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Makes no difference to me,&quot; said Eris. She then shot me a look that I hadn&#x27;t seen much of lately: a piercing glare. &quot;Don&#x27;t you think you should discuss it with Sylphie?&quot;</p>
<p>I couldn&#x27;t help but smirk, as it wasn&#x27;t something I expected to hear from Eris, but the nod I gave her was sincere.</p>
<p>&quot;U…understood.&quot;</p>
<p><strong>Sylphiette</strong></p>
<p><strong>I</strong> WAS AT A LOSS. I didn&#x27;t know who to ask what to do or how. I didn&#x27;t even know what I wanted to come of it. It was painful to be so clueless.</p>
<p>just a moment about how much easier it would be if Perugius would simply take Sieg away. The thought shook me to my core.</p>
<p>It was just a fleeting thought, but it confirmed something—the root of my anxieties wasn&#x27;t the idea of Sieg being Laplace. But that begged the question: What was it I feared? Why was I uneasy? I didn&#x27;t know. All I could do was hold Sieg in my arms and shudder.</p>
<p>Even when we were told to go to the Divine Continent and give him a baptism, my mind was blank. It really was like I&#x27;d reverted to my old self—to the little girl who was bullied by all the kids in Buena Village. Rudy saved me back then; he chased off the bullies and taught me all there was to know. Things like magic, and how to read and write. What about now? Would Rudy still save me?</p>
<p>When I was a foolish little child, I had complete faith in Rudy and knew he would save me. Things were different now. I loved Rudy, and I had faith in him. But I knew he was still only human. He might have looked infallible, like he could do anything, but the truth was plenty of things were beyond him. He was scared of all kinds of things, of course, and he was more than capable of making simple mistakes.</p>
<p>Like forgetting to come up with a name for our baby. That surprised me, and even disappointed me, but it wasn&#x27;t like I got angry over it. Rudy was hard at work as Orsted&#x27;s subordinate. I knew how busy every single day was for him. I knew that he faced hardship in the Asura Kingdom, in Millis, on the Demon Continent. Everywhere he went.</p>
<p>People had their limits. I wanted to be considerate of that. I couldn&#x27;t expect someone to be a perfect family man while working under Orsted. That was why I swore to myself that I&#x27;d do it, so that Rudy could be free to pursue his work. I mustn&#x27;t beg Rudy for help. I had to make ends meet on my own.</p>
<p>Rudy wouldn&#x27;t come to save me. So, what was I supposed to do? How was I supposed to make it all work?</p>
<p>&quot;Sylphie.&quot;</p>
<p>While my head spun from the onslaught of questions that had no answer, I heard a voice. I was instantly snapped back to reality, catching the person who&#x27;d spoken my name in the corner of my eye.</p>
<p>&quot;Um… Sorry if I&#x27;ve got the wrong idea,&quot; Roxy began, asking with both hesitation and sincerity. &quot;But… Sylphie, do you think that maybe you&#x27;re less concerned about whether Sieg is Laplace, and more worried about how his hair is green?&quot;</p>
<p>By the time I realized it, my eyes met Roxy&#x27;s. Mine must have widened.</p>
<p>&quot;What…makes you say that?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I heard from Lilia that you used to be bullied by other children for having green hair.&quot;</p>
<p>Oh, right. Lilia! I wondered why I&#x27;d forgotten about her. A long time had passed since my hair changed colors; I reunited with Rudy, married him, and at some point, I&#x27;d started assuming Rudy was the only one around who still knew about the old me. Silly me, Lilia knew as well. I never gave it too much thought, but there was no way she wouldn&#x27;t have known.</p>
<p>Why didn&#x27;t I ever connect with her? No, Lilia did start conversations with me. I had just closed myself off and never tried to ask her.</p>
<p>&quot;You probably don&#x27;t remember, Sylphie, but back when I was in Buena Village, I met you once. I even spoke to your parents.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;About…what?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;About the color of your hair. It seemed they worried about it, too.&quot;</p>
<p>Huh. This was, well, a strange thing to hear.</p>
<p>For as long as I could remember, my mother and father never said a word about my hair color. Even when I got bullied and ran home crying and asked them why my hair was a different color from everyone else&#x27;s, they couldn&#x27;t give me a straight answer; they just looked sad, or guilty, or some other mix of emotions, and then they hugged me and told me it was all right, but it wasn&#x27;t all right at all—</p>
<p>&quot;What did you tell them?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;That I could guarantee you weren&#x27;t a Superd. I told them that everything would be all right if they explained it to their neighbors and raised you with love.&quot;</p>
<p>Ah. So that was why my mother and father hugged me and told me that it was all right, over and over again.</p>
<p>Of course, they weren&#x27;t all talk; I knew that my mother and father really did love me. They raised me as best they could. I might not have known it then, but I knew now.</p>
<p>&quot;I thought you&#x27;d be all right since Buena Village didn&#x27;t have a culture of discriminating against demons, but that attitude doesn&#x27;t always reach the children…&quot;</p>
<p>Roxy paused to pat my chest.</p>
<p>&quot;Either way, if you have any concern about Sieg being treated differently because of his hair color, just leave it to me. As you can see from looking at me, I&#x27;m very clearly a demon. I&#x27;ve got plenty of experience with handling discrimination!&quot;</p>
<p>Hearing this from Roxy made her seem more reliable than ever. Rudy must have had a lot of respect for that side of her…</p>
<p>Still… Yeah, she was right. I wasn&#x27;t alone anymore. I had Lilia and Roxy. Raising children might not have been Eris&#x27;s specialty, but she gave it her all without giving up or pushing it on to someone else.</p>
<p>&quot;Let&#x27;s all travel to the Divine Continent together. I&#x27;m a bit concerned about leaving Lilia alone to watch the house, but there are plenty of people she can rely on.&quot;</p>
<p>After making her suggestion, Roxy gently rubbed my back. I felt lighter, as if she was lifting a weight off my shoulders.</p>
<p><strong>Rudeus</strong></p>
<p><strong>W</strong>HEN I RETURNED from my discussion with Orsted, I found Sylphie&#x27;s attitude had changed a bit. She was no more talkative than before, but the light had returned to her eyes. Roxy looked even more determined than usual, so I wondered if she gave Sylphie a pep talk. Man, Roxy was reliable.</p>
<p>I talked a bit with Sylphie, too. I told her Orsted said that Sieg&#x27;s health was strong enough to withstand the trip, and that I&#x27;d do everything in my power to protect him. I also put in one last apology for forgetting to name him. Her response to that was tepid, so it didn&#x27;t seem like she&#x27;d forgiven me yet.</p>
<p>I considered telling her that she could rest at home instead of coming along for the trip, but I decided against it. It&#x27;d be a huge shock if I had suggested separating mother and child. We&#x27;d go on this trip together. She wasn&#x27;t fully recovered from giving birth, but I knew it was better this way.</p>
<p>Roxy and Sylphie were traveling to the Divine Continent with us for sure. I figured Eris was a given as well. That left the house with just Aisha, Lilia, Zenith, and the children. Arus and Lara were still small, but they were already a handful.</p>
<p>I unloaded my anxieties about it once I returned home, to which Lilia responded with a heartening, &quot;We&#x27;ll be fine.&quot; Aisha was more practical, saying, &quot;We&#x27;ll borrow some hands from the Mercenary Band if we need to, so don&#x27;t worry about it.&quot; It sounded like they could make it all work, so for now, I relaxed.</p>
<p>We dedicated three days to preparing for the journey.</p>
<p>On the first day, we confirmed our route and schedule with Orsted, learned about the peculiarities of the Divine Continent, put in our orders for equipment, and a few other things. Fortunately, the office was already connected to a series of ancient teleportation circles around the world.</p>
<p>Our plan for the first day was to travel to an ancient circle from the office, soar from the ancient circle to the foot of the Divine Continent, and then scale the cliff face. Once that was cleared, traveling for around a half day to a full day would land us in Aluce.</p>
<p>Aluce was the name of a skyfolk town, with Aluce Hill referring to a hill nearby. After staying a night in the town, we would climb the nearby Aluce Hill and have Sieg receive his baptism. After that, all we&#x27;d have to do is set up a teleportation circle somewhere and head home.</p>
<p>At minimum, it&#x27;d take three to four days. With some leeway, I&#x27;d put it at around six.</p>
<p>Since we&#x27;d be climbing to a high altitude, we might need safety gear. The human body wasn&#x27;t well adapted to survival in low-oxygen areas. After I brought it up with Orsted, he provided a solution immediately.</p>
<p>Orsted handed me a few necklace-shaped magical implements. Apparently, they&#x27;d nullify the harmful effects of thin air. They were originally invented by a race that traversed the miasma-filled valleys of the Demon Continent, so their primary effect was to nullify bodily harm that</p>
<p>came from highly toxic areas. It seemed like they&#x27;d work for our climb to the Divine Continent as well.</p>
<p>Man, Orsted could really pull anything out of that pocket of his. He might&#x27;ve secretly been a robot cat from the 22nd century. Nah, Orsted&#x27;s face was too scary for kids&#x27; merch…</p>
<p>Two days before our departure, Lucie became depressed. When I asked her why, she said all her moms were going away and that she already felt lonely.</p>
<p>She hadn&#x27;t been getting much attention lately, given Sylphie&#x27;s emotional state. I guess this was only natural. I felt guilty over making a child pay the price for her parents&#x27; problems, but parents were human too. We get depressed sometimes.</p>
<p>For the rest of the day, I spent as much time with Lucie as I could afford. She talked about how dealing with the newly born Sieg was a bit tough. I couldn&#x27;t tell her to be a big sister and suck it up at her age, and I certainly didn&#x27;t want to, but I explained that the other kids would have tough times of their own. I hoped I could count on Lucie&#x27;s help when those times came. I also said that if she ever needed help, her dear ol&#x27; dad would do everything he could to provide it.</p>
<p>Lucie pouted at the first half, but she looked like she was fully invested by the end. I&#x27;d like to think I reached her.</p>
<p>That evening, I caught Lucie tending to Sieg from the side of the crib he slept in. Considering how often she just stared blankly at him early on, this took me by surprise. I&#x27;d figured she must wish he wasn&#x27;t here. And yet, she fetched Lilia or Aisha whenever Sieg started crying, and if Lara or Arus got cranky, she&#x27;d dash over to comfort them. She&#x27;d taken my words to heart.</p>
<p>When I was her age… I mean, my previous life, of course. Back then, I could never have acted the way she was now. I probably made a fuss over how unfair it was that my siblings got all the attention and gave my parents a headache.</p>
<p>Lucie was still young, but she amazed me.</p>
<p>Before I knew it, it was the day of our departure.</p>
<p>Me, Eris, Roxy, Sylphie, and baby Sieg. The four parents of the family would all be on this journey. I was surprised we hadn&#x27;t traveled together already. Well, I suppose it wasn&#x27;t our first trip; we all went to see Ariel&#x27;s coronation together. Anyway, while I knew it was a bit insensitive to Sylphie and Sieg, I felt a little excited.</p>
<p>&quot;Well, we&#x27;ll be off, now,&quot; I said.</p>
<p>&quot;Gotcha!&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Take care, now.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Um… See you later.&quot;</p>
<p>Lilia and Aisha nodded as usual. Only Lucie looked the slightest bit reluctant as she held on to Aisha&#x27;s hand. She was doing her best to not let that emotion show on her face.</p>

<p>I really ought to spend a bit more time with her once the situation with Geese settled down.</p>
<p>***</p>
<p>A few hours after our departure, we arrived at the Divine Continent&#x27;s border. We were on the furthest northeastern edge of the Central Continent.</p>
<p>A vertical precipice that we strained to see the top of towered in front and over us. On either side, the ocean stretched into the distance.</p>
<p>The cliff face was more than simple bare rock. A number of the local inhabitants believed that gods resided within this cliff, so its surface was dotted with ladders and footholds. According to Orsted, a shrine dedicated to the worship of these gods could be found around two hundred meters up.</p>
<p>Further above that, there were stakes driven into the cliff face to assist with climbing. They&#x27;d been installed by someone who&#x27;d tried to climb the precipice long ago. It wasn&#x27;t clear whether he ever made it all the way in one piece, but most opined that he fell without reaching the top.</p>
<p>There was a road on the right. Calling it a road was a bit generous, as it wasn&#x27;t much more than the remains of a barely walkable series of footholds…but hey, if someone walked on it, then it counted as a road. It was patchy, but it continued all the way to the Demon Continent. While it was no doubt a harrowing path, it was apparently a safer bet than trying to climb upward. Quite a few people had traversed it from here to the Demon Continent and vice versa.</p>
<p>&quot;That…sure is high!&quot; said Eris as she looked up at the precipice. There was a hint of excitement in her voice; her arms were crossed, as though declaring that this was no obstacle to a first-rate adventurer. She might have had the bravado of a port-town kid trying to live up to his ancestors, but sadly, this was the end of the Central Continent, not the End of the World.</p>
<p>&quot;…&quot;</p>
<p>Sylphie had a look of incredible unease. Given her current mental state and a fear of heights, I couldn&#x27;t blame her.</p>
<p>&quot;Um, Rudy?&quot; asked Roxy in a shaky voice as she looked up at the precipice. &quot;How are we going to climb this?&quot;</p>
<p>Her voice seemed to plead for me to have a plan. Which, of course, I did. Come now, would I really go rock climbing with a newborn baby without a plan?</p>
<p>&quot;This way, everyone,&quot; I said, leading the group toward a part of the wall that was relatively bare of footholds. Not that the presence of footholds would make a difference, but I didn&#x27;t want to make life harder for any travelers coming after me.</p>
<p>First, I used earth magic to create a box that could hold about four adults with plenty of breathing space. One that was heavy but sturdy. I then added an entrance, as well as some windows to let some light in and let us check our surroundings.</p>
<p>&quot;All aboard.&quot;</p>
<p>Once I confirmed that everyone was inside, I closed the door.</p>
<p>&quot;The heck is this?&quot; asked Eris, who tilted her head and looked at me out of the corner of her eye.</p>
<p>&quot;Now, now, let the master do his work,&quot; I assured her. I placed a hand on the ground. The spell I&#x27;d readied was called Stone Pillar. I formed four pillars, fixed them firmly to the box, and pumped in some mana.</p>
<p>&quot;Eep!&quot;</p>
<p>The box slowly started moving. Upward.</p>
<p>&quot;Oh! Well, this does seem like a safe way to do it.&quot;</p>
<p>I felt a smug grin coming on at Roxy&#x27;s praise. This was an original spell of mine, Elevator. I used it once before, back on the Begaritt Continent.</p>
<p>I&#x27;d put even more thought into passenger safety since then. The pillars lifting the box up were kept sturdy with plenty of mana, ensuring they wouldn&#x27;t break on us. Creating pillars that could withstand the trip to three thousand meters required a massive amount of mana, so I did a baton pass every fifty meters by creating new pillars. I figured we&#x27;d be fine, but in case I got tired or ran low on mana on the way up, I could also make a hole in the cliff face and slot the entire box inside it, giving me a way to safely take a breather.</p>
<p>Sylphie glanced out the window while holding Sieg; a moment later, her face went completely pale. She walked over to me and plopped down by my side. Given how complicated things had gotten in the past few weeks, it warmed my heart to see she still relied on me.</p>
<p>&quot;Man… Talk about boring,&quot; said Eris, who had also sat down—but only because she&#x27;d grown bored of the view out the window.</p>
<p>&quot;It&#x27;s better this way. We can&#x27;t go rock climbing with a baby on board, right?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Hmph!&quot;</p>
<p>Eris turned up her nose. I took the fact that she didn&#x27;t hit me as a sign that she completely understood. I wouldn&#x27;t let myself hurt those two on this journey. Not a scratch. No amount of heroism was gonna make up for forgetting to name Sieg, though.</p>
<p>***</p>
<p>Several hours passed. My plan of swapping out the pillars every fifty meters was making our ascent go without a hitch.</p>
<p>Sylphie kept her eyes on Sieg the whole time. Roxy tried to lighten the mood with a little light conversation. Sylphie wasn&#x27;t her usual self, but she managed to respond. It was small talk; Roxy&#x27;s grumblings about her work, the latest happenings at school, the last prank Lucie pulled on her, asking about how Arus and Lara were doing. That sort of thing. I would&#x27;ve liked to join in, but those pillars weren&#x27;t exactly gonna form themselves, so I was stuck with that.</p>
<p>As for Eris, she took position by the window and gazed outside. The scenery was lovely. As the ground steadily slipped away, we got a better view of the flocks of giant creatures flying between the gaps of the clouds. Were those Blue Dragons? I&#x27;d never seen Blue Dragons up close…</p>
<p>By the time I replaced the pillars for the twentieth time and put us above the thousand-meter mark, avian monsters started to come into view. They were giant birds—probably around three meters long, with a wingspan exceeding six meters. They were also flying around our box and squawking at us. Their flock circled us, perched above us, pecked at the walls. Generally harassed us. Hard to tell if they were scared of this new object, or territorial and trying to destroy it.</p>
<p>Our box was built to be incredibly tough. It wouldn&#x27;t break from a few monsters&#x27; pokes. It did sway a little, though. Each time it did, the color drained from Sylphie&#x27;s face, Sieg would start crying, and Roxy would reassure them that everything was fine and that we wouldn&#x27;t fall. Not that Roxy knew enough to make that promise.</p>
<p>I knew we wouldn&#x27;t fall. If I ever thought we might, I&#x27;d have fixed the box to the cliff face and exterminated those monsters. With no reason to think they were a threat, I continued our ascent. The monsters managed to jam their necks through the window every now and then, but Eris would quickly lop them off, and that was the end of that. The soil of the box started to become stained with their blood, but hey, it wasn&#x27;t like we&#x27;d spent all our lives in the lap of luxury, you know? We weren&#x27;t sheltered and could all stand a bit of gore. Nobody complained.</p>
<p>After a while, I slotted the box into the cliff face, rinsed the walls off with a bit of water, and took a break. My somewhat late lunch came in a box that Lilia and Aisha had prepared as we departed. It was a sandwich. Two hardened slices of bread with some meat and vegetables stuck in the middle. It had a simple taste to it, not far off from my usual fare, but having a bite while looking over the vast scenery outside wasn&#x27;t half bad.</p>
<p>&quot;It&#x27;s nice to chill out like this every now and then,&quot; said Eris. She was gazing out the window while chomping on her sandwich sloppily enough to get crumbs everywhere.</p>
<p>&quot;Really, Eris. Mind your manners,&quot; chided Sylphie.</p>
<p>&quot;Yeah, I get it,&quot; said Eris, who clearly did not get it. It&#x27;d been a long time since I&#x27;d seen this particular fight.</p>
<p>&quot;Hey widdle Sieg, it&#x27;s Daddyyy. Time to get you in the bath, champ!&quot;</p>
<p>I was looking after Sieg while Sylphie ate. I changed his diaper and then made a little tub with earth magic to bathe him in.</p>
<p>This close, his hair really was green, and his ears were maybe just a bit longer than a human&#x27;s. His face was the perfect average of mine and Sylphie&#x27;s. Naturally. I would&#x27;ve been worried if he didn&#x27;t have any of my features.</p>
<p>He laughed when I brought my face in close and played peekaboo, and stared off into space when I moved away. When I picked him up, he&#x27;d stare deep into my face.</p>
<p>When Lucie was born, she looked incredulous at every movement she made, which made me worry that she might have been a reincarnation. I was on my fourth child now, so I&#x27;d stopped having those sorts of doubts. No matter how many children I had, I knew I&#x27;d love all of them.</p>
<p>When I offered my index finger to Sieg, he gripped it tight. He was pretty strong. Babies are born pretty strong, huh?</p>
<p>The moment after that thought crossed my mind…</p>
<p>&quot;Owwwchgggh!&quot;</p>
<p>I heard a snap accompanied by a sudden rush of pain. My instincts told me to yank my hand away from Sieg, but I took a breath, and then calmly used my left hand to peel Sieg&#x27;s hand off of my finger.</p>
<p>When I took a look at the index finger that the pain was coming from…</p>
<p>&quot;No way…&quot;</p>
<p>It was broken. Come on, seriously?</p>
<p>&quot;Sieg?&quot; shouted Sylphie as she sprang over in the next instant. When she saw my finger, her eyes widened. &quot;Huh? Rudy, your finger…&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Yep. It&#x27;s broken.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;…&quot;</p>
<p>Sylphie was at a loss for words. Eventually, she lifted her hands to mine and wrapped them around my index finger. A faint light glowed from within her cupped hands and the pain disappeared.</p>
<p>A silently cast healing spell. Bravo.</p>
<p>&quot;Thank you, Sylphie.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Don&#x27;t mention it…&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;He&#x27;s a strong one.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Yeah. He got me, too.&quot;</p>
<p>With that, Sylphie showed me her wrist. There was a vivid handshaped scar on it.</p>
<p>Hmm. Did this boy strangle any serpents while we weren&#x27;t looking? I was pretty sure that he hadn&#x27;t left our sight over the past month.</p>
<p>&quot;If he&#x27;s this strong as a kid, then he&#x27;s got a bright future as a swordsman.&quot;</p>
<p>He might even set out to slay a hydra or something… Wait, did his dad die in that story? I&#x27;d be another Paul.</p>
<p>&quot;You never know,&quot; Sylphie chuckled. &quot;After seeing Zanoba, I feel like it&#x27;s not a guarantee…&quot;</p>
<p>Despite Zanoba being a little wild when he was young, he grew up to have a perfectly healthy obsession with dolls. That&#x27;s probably what Sylphie was referring to. She might not have known, however, that he was a capable man on the battlefield. In raw force, sure, but also in courage and cunning.</p>
<p>&quot;I can teach him to use a sword!&quot; Eris chimed in, having finished wolfing her sandwich down.</p>
<p>There was once a time where I doubted if Eris could possibly be a teacher. I couldn&#x27;t deny that Norn and the other University of Magic students were learning plenty about swordsmanship from her. I wouldn&#x27;t refer to the things she taught as &quot;classes,&quot; but from what I heard, the expertise she passed on was valuable.</p>
<p>Still, compared to Ruijerd&#x27;s &quot;Do you understand?&quot; or Paul&#x27;s onomatopoeic grunts, she was leagues more helpful. I&#x27;d say her teaching style was close to Ghislaine&#x27;s. Common sense.</p>
<p>Eris considered it her duty to teach swordsmanship to the children, so she even prepared kid-sized wooden swords for them. Lucie was already swinging a sword under her tutelage. My kids were getting their extracurricular education in early.</p>
<p>&quot;Looks like our children will all know how to handle both swords and sorcery,&quot; said Roxy, who planned to teach them magic. Lucie had started practicing spell incantations little by little. When it came to learning magic, the sooner you started, the better. Kids that age had more mana than they knew what to do with.</p>
<p>We wouldn&#x27;t have any problems if I left the magic education to Roxy. The whole brood was bound to be Saint-tier magicians by the time they were adults.</p>
<p>&quot;I can&#x27;t wait to see how everyone grows up,&quot; I said to Sylphie. She broke out into a smile and agreed. It was a relief to see a smile on her face after so long.</p>
<p>We resumed our long ascent.</p>
<p>We stopped seeing the avian monsters at around two thousand meters. In their place, we saw monsters that looked like winged goats and snake-necked lizards. The lizards lived in the crevices of the cliff face. They popped their heads in through the window that faced the cliff and startled us all. Their long necks allowed them to maneuver their heads with an alarming precision, and they were coming for us. Or they would have been, had we not separated their heads from their bodies in five seconds flat.</p>
<p>They must have evolved necks like that to drag prey into the crevices of the precipice. Excluding those guys, our trip was uneventful. We bagged one of the goats for dinner and ignored the rest as we continued our climb.</p>
<p>***</p>
<p>I had now swapped the pillars over sixty times.</p>
<p>The world outside was blanketed in a thick fog. We had to be poking into the clouds by now.</p>
<p>It was already night. Our box was illuminated by my lamplight spirit, but I was fatigued, and conflicted as to whether I should stop for a nap or if I should continue climbing. Given our elevation, we should be close to our destination…</p>
<p>As that thought crossed my mind, the fog happened to clear, and with it, the view outside the window. Not just the one facing away from the precipice, but the one facing toward it, as well.</p>
<p>I stopped raising the pillars. Outside, a grassy field shimmered in the moonlight.</p>
<p>It was the Divine Continent.</p>
