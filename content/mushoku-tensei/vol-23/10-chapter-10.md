<p><strong>T</strong>HE NECROSS FORTRESS in the Demon Continent&#x27;s Gaslow Territory was known for being the most impregnable of all. Deep within its bowels, in the scarcely used dungeons, resided a prisoner.</p>
<p>&quot;Grrrrr!&quot;</p>
<p>The prisoner&#x27;s hands were bound by shackles, a metal ball attached to her feet. She&#x27;d even been given blue and white striped pajamas to wear. She looked pitiful.</p>
<p>&quot;Grrrrr!&quot;</p>
<p>The low growl that echoed hollowly against the walls was not, in fact, the girl&#x27;s voice. It was her empty stomach. It snarled to express all her displeasure, agreeing with the frustration she felt at her present circumstances. Then again, maybe it was just empty.</p>
<p>&quot;Come out!&quot;</p>
<p>The door to her cell suddenly swung open to reveal two towering figures, hidden in armor black as midnight. They forced her to her feet and dragged her out. She had no choice but to go with them. An angry, unpleasant sound reverberated down the hallway as she dragged the heavy metal ball behind her. The prisoner didn&#x27;t strain under the weight, however. She was stronger than she looked.</p>
<p>Led by the black knights, she made her way outside of the dungeons. Their path took them through a long corridor and up a flight of stairs. At last, their trip ended at the fortress&#x27;s throne room.</p>
<p>&quot;Hurry up. Move it!&quot;</p>
<p>Their hands shoved into her back, lurching her forward. She stumbled into the circular space, lit by purple candelabras. It was the kind of place you might expect a criminal&#x27;s punishment to be carried out. When she managed to lift her head, she spotted the throne ahead of her. It was a place the prisoner had once occupied in the past, but now, it was a demon king who sat upon it.</p>
<p>&quot;Atofe…&quot; murmured the prisoner.</p>
<p>The demon king was clad in the same midnight-black armor as her underlings. The moment the prisoner laid her eyes upon Atofe, her cheeks flushed red with anger.</p>
<p>&quot;What&#x27;s the meaning of this?!&quot; The prisoner roared with all the power she could muster, her voice erupting all the way from the base of her very, very empty tummy. Perhaps that was what gave it its power.</p>
<p>In contrast, the demon king before her—the most feared in the whole Demon Continent—merely adjusted her posture and scowled at her captive.</p>
<p>&quot;How wretched,&quot; said the prisoner. &quot;The departed Necross would lament to see you like this!&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Father told me to live how I want!&quot; Atofe shouted back.</p>
<p>&quot;Only because you&#x27;re a moron who will not listen to other people! He must have known it was the only way you <em>could</em> live. He gave up on you!&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I am no moron!&quot;</p>
<p>Demon King Atofe was absolutely furious, but the prisoner didn&#x27;t shrink. Instead, she snorted with derisive laughter.</p>
<p>&quot;Doubtless, you <em>are</em> a moron. A moron among morons. Even you must understand that. All someone has to do is dangle something promising in front of you, and you haven&#x27;t got the brains to think twice about it.&quot;</p>
<p>Atofe shook her head vigorously. &quot;That&#x27;s not true! Kal said I was smart! That I&#x27;m quick to learn things!&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Atofe, that was…&quot; The prisoner gave a meaningful pause, as if to draw out the moment. The next words she had to say would cut deep—and she knew these ones in particular were ones she must never utter to Atofe. &quot;…merely flattery.&quot;</p>
<p><em>&quot;Graaaaaah!&quot;</em></p>
<p>The demon king&#x27;s rage boiled over. The surrounding black knights flew to her, trying to pin her back, but she knocked them away easily. Still, the black knights were not to be deterred. They took up a formation similar to a rugby scrummage and pinned their master back.</p>
<p>The demon king flailed her fists through the air, screeching. &quot;You rotten little weakling! I&#x27;ll kill you! I&#x27;ll tear you limb from limb! You&#x27;ll bite it again before I&#x27;m through!&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Yeah, yeah. If it bothers you that much, learn to count.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Graaaaaah!&quot;</p>
<p>The prisoner&#x27;s taunting provoked the demon king into gathering her power to force back her knights.</p>
<p>&quot;Lady Kishirika, please cease this antagonizing! If you continue provoking Lady Atofe, she—&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Shut your mouths!&quot; Kishirika snapped back at them. &quot;I only came along with you because you promised me delicious treats, and look how you have treated me! I won&#x27;t be satisfied until I have fully voiced my complaints!&quot;</p>
<p>Yes, the prisoner Kishirika had actually been lured into a trap. One of their number had stripped off his trademark black armor and tempted her by saying, &quot;Little girl, we&#x27;ll give you some sweets if you&#x27;ll just come with us.&quot; That was how she&#x27;d found herself here.</p>
<p>It was true; <em>Kishirika</em> was the one who had fallen for the promise of something promising without giving it much thought. She&#x27;d fallen for the prospect of food hook, line, and sinker—it was only afterward that she realized she&#x27;d been deceived. Worse, the men hadn&#x27;t even made good on their promise. She&#x27;d gotten no treats at all!</p>
<p>&quot;You guys haven&#x27;t even told me what you took me captive for! What are you claiming I did wrong? I haven&#x27;t…&quot; Kishirika hesitated a moment. &quot;I haven&#x27;t done anything wrong, have I?&quot; She started fidgeting, rubbing her hands together. There were too many possibilities to rule out. Kishirika dabbled in all sorts of evil—too much, one might argue. Even she was self-aware enough to know that wrongdoing was most of what she did with her time. It wouldn&#x27;t be too surprising if someone was angry with her for it.</p>
<p>To her surprise, the demon king declared, &quot;Hmph! You haven&#x27;t done anything wrong!&quot;</p>
<p>It had taken mere seconds for her fury to subside. Atofe knew how futile it was to be angry with this particular captive.</p>
<p>&quot;Then tell me <em>why</em>!&quot; demanded Kishirika. &quot;No matter how unreasonable you are, you are not so evil you would capture me for no reason whatsoever! The only time you do something like this is when you have the wrong idea about something, or someone has deceived you</p>
<p>into…&quot; Her voice trailed off as realization dawned on her. &quot;So that&#x27;s it. Someone&#x27;s tricked you again!&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;No! No one has deceived me!&quot; Atofe shouted back, denying Kishirika&#x27;s charge.</p>
<p>&quot;That&#x27;s what deceived people say! All right then! If that&#x27;s what brought all of this about, tell me everything. There&#x27;s still time. I can save you before it&#x27;s too late and you do something irrevocable. So why don&#x27;t you remove these shackles first?&quot; Kishirika thrust her hands out in front of her, holding them up.</p>
<p>Atofe wasn&#x27;t looking at her. She stared off into the distance, lost in thought. &quot;Deception is committed through conversation. That wasn&#x27;t the case for us. We fought. We fought one another, and at the end, I admitted defeat.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;You liar! You mean to tell me someone as ridiculously competitive as you admitted defeat?!&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;The one who forced me to admit my defeat…is this man here!&quot; Atofe pointed her finger in the direction of a mage swathed in gray robes. He had a terrible expression on his face. The kind of smarmy, perverted type one might expect to find from a man who kept three wives waiting on him hand and foot. Or perhaps it was simply that he was trying too hard to smile.</p>
<p>&quot;It&#x27;s… It&#x27;s you…&quot; Kishirika stammered. &quot;Rubeus!&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Close, but not quite.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I-I guess it might be possible, with that ridiculous amount of mana you have, to be able to…&quot; Kishirika shuddered in fear. She had met this human mage just twice in the past. The first time she had laughed at how eerie the amount of mana he held was. The second time, she had laughed at his magical prowess in being able to fend off Demon King Atofe.</p>
<p>She wasn&#x27;t laughing this time. A man who could command Atofe and convince the demon king to capture Kishirika wasn&#x27;t funny. Not in the least.</p>
<p>&quot;Hehe.&quot; The mage chuckled nevertheless as he looked down at her, his lips resting in an unsettling smile. &quot;To tell the truth, there is something I want to give you.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Wh-wh-what could that be?&quot; Kishirika demanded, her voice trembling uncontrollably. &quot;Some kind of last rites?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Hahaha, something much better than that.&quot; He chuckled heartily and his grin stretched even wider.</p>
<p>&quot;I-I-I won&#x27;t be misled! You menfolk are always like this! Don&#x27;t try to trick me with your sweet talk!&quot; Although Kishirika tried to resist him, she had nowhere to run. The tremor in her voice betrayed her strong front as well. She began scanning the area, looking for some method of escape while she crossed her legs, trying to hold her bladder despite the fear.</p>
<p>&quot;I wonder if you&#x27;ll still be able to say that after you see this?&quot; The mage lowered the backpack he&#x27;d been carrying. His hand disappeared inside, soon producing a black box.</p>
<p>&quot;Eek!&quot; Kishirika shrieked. A black box?! Her terror swelled just imagining what could possibly be contained inside. What could it be? It was no ordinary black box—it was a black-black box. Black as midnight. She knew there had to be something terrifying in there! Why else would it be such a potent shade of black?!</p>
<p>&quot;Once you have this, you will <em>want</em> to do anything I tell you.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Wh-what?!&quot;</p>
<p>He opened the box. Contained snugly inside was a ring-shaped object as big as a fist. It was golden with a strange creamy white coating on it. It almost resembled mold, she thought. All of the hair on her body stood on end. For as creepy as its shape and color was, it emitted a sweet, saccharine smell.</p>
<p>&quot;Wh-what is that thing? What…do you mean to do with it?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Haha, this is what you do with it.&quot; The mage took it in his hand and approached her, bringing the object to her mouth. At the same time, two black knights flanking her clapped their hands down on her shoulders. There was no escape.</p>
<p>&quot;Say &#x27;aaah.&#x27;&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;N-no… Stop… Stooooop!&quot;</p>
<p><strong>Rudeus</strong></p>
<p><strong>T</strong>HE DEMON WORLD&#x27;S Great Emperor Kishirika Kishirisu nibbled on the doughnut I&#x27;d brought her while tears poured down her cheeks. &quot;Does</p>
<p>something so delicious really exist? I cannot believe it…!&quot;</p>
<p>Aisha had done me the favor of creating this, aided by the fresh eggs and sugar we&#x27;d secured from the Holy Country of Millis. Apparently, Nanahoshi had told her about it; through Aisha&#x27;s own diligent study, she had managed to recreate it. It was a simple matter to gather the necessary ingredients, since our house cooked up a lot of fried foods anyway.</p>
<p>&quot;I struggle to fathom this… Perhaps the entire reason I was born was to savor the flavor of this delicious creation!&quot;</p>
<p>It had been a long time since I last saw Kishirika, and at first she had been in a wretched mood. Now she seemed perfectly fine. Ah, the magic of doughnuts! I had actually asked Roxy to sample it as well before bringing one here, and it had been incredibly effective. <em>I don&#x27;t think I&#x27;ve ever seen her look that happy before.</em> Sadly, that meant I&#x27;d lost to a doughnut.</p>

<p><em>No, no,</em> I assured myself, <em>I&#x27;m the one who established the route for these goods to come here from Millis.</em> In that sense, I was the one who had created that smile. <em>Father-in-law, Mother-in-law, I am making your daughter happy, just as I promised.</em> I mean, they were also Aisha&#x27;s doughnuts, but still. Roxy&#x27;s reaction proved that doughnuts were super effective against demons.</p>
<p>&quot;Ah…&quot;</p>
<p>However, just as mana is finite, so too was the magic—or rather, the number of doughnuts. Upon eating twelve of them, Kishirika regarded me with great sorrow.</p>
<p>&quot;Is this really all you have…?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Yes.&quot;</p>
<p>There was a long silence.</p>
<p>&quot;If you give me more, I will grant you whatever you desire. How is that?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Those are precisely the words I wanted to hear.&quot; I flashed a smile at her.</p>
<p>Kishirika&#x27;s eyes widened in shock. She wrapped her arms around her body protectively. &quot;Khh… So it&#x27;s my body you want after all. No matter how delicious the food you offer, this body belongs to Badi. But after treating me to something so sumptuous, I… Khh!&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I&#x27;m currently upholding a vow of abstinence, so no need for that,&quot; I told her.</p>
<p>&quot;Really? Abstaining too much is bad for the body, you know.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Well, if I find myself unable to resist any longer, I will ask one of my wives instead.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Wives? Oh, that&#x27;s right. You&#x27;re already married. My, but you children of men do mature quickly…&quot;</p>
<p>Regardless, I hadn&#x27;t come all this way for chitchat. There was something I needed to ask her. Rumor had it that Kishirika would give a reward to those who brought her food, which was why I&#x27;d procured these doughnuts from Aisha.</p>
<p>&quot;First, Lady Kishirika, I would entreat you to use your power to inform me where Geese is.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Oh? Geese, you say?&quot;</p>
<p>I nodded. &quot;Yes. His most defining features are…&quot; I went on to describe the finer characteristics of the man, including what I believed to be his true name. It was the one he had signed his letter to me with, anyway.</p>
<p>&quot;Hm, yes, yes. I do feel like I have heard of this person before… Wait one moment.&quot;</p>
<p>She used her eye, while cream still liberally stained her lips. Her eye spun in place, almost like a slot machine, until it changed to the one she intended to use, stopping suddenly. This particular eye was known as an Eye of All-Seeing. With it, she glared into the distance, scrunching her face.</p>
<p>&quot;Oh? Hm… This is… Ah, yes, that looks delicious.&quot; She muttered to herself as she continued her scan of the far-off distance until at last, she froze. &quot;Found him.&quot;</p>
<p>That hadn&#x27;t taken long.</p>
<p>&quot;He&#x27;s at the eastern end of the Northern Territories, in Biheiril Kingdom. There, in the depths of a forest, he seems to be speaking with someone. My, but the man does have the face of a villain,&quot; Kishirika remarked before snickering. She leaned a bit further in the direction she found him, compelled by curiosity. &quot;Now, let&#x27;s see…who is he speaking with… Hm?&quot; Her expression instantly clouded over. &quot;I can&#x27;t see him anymore.&quot;</p>
<p>Kishirika&#x27;s expression suddenly turned deadly serious as she closed her eyes. She leaned her head back, letting her eyes rest for a few moments. It was only several seconds later that she finally opened them again.</p>
<p>&quot;This sensation…yes, I know it. Your current enemy is the Man-God…correct?&quot;</p>
<p>Gone was the jovial and mischievous atmosphere she usually had, replaced by something more solemn and reserved. Still, I answered her question honestly.</p>
<p>&quot;Yes.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;And if you&#x27;re fighting the Man-God, that must mean you have aligned yourself with the Dragon God?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Hmm…&quot; Kishirika folded her arms and lowered her head, taking a pensive pose. After several seconds, she glanced back up at the sky, like a thoughtful soul might gaze right at the moon. Granted, it was daytime outside and sunny to boot, with nothing but a few clouds streaking through the otherwise empty blue. &quot;And Atofe, you have aligned yourself with this boy?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Yeah.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;So that&#x27;s it. This must be destiny, I suppose.&quot;</p>
<p>Kishirika wasn&#x27;t being her typical silly, joking self. In fact, she seemed more like a sage. What in the world was going on with her? Had someone slipped something suspicious into those doughnuts?</p>
<p>&quot;Lady Kishirika, do you mean to say you know the Man-God?&quot; I asked.</p>
<p>&quot;Yup. The two of us have a bit of a history. Frankly speaking, I would like to avoid ever getting involved with him again.&quot;</p>
<p>I tilted my head. &quot;A history, you say?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Nothing that remarkable. Just that a mere 4,200 years ago, he manipulated me and Badigadi. He was after Laplace&#x27;s life.&quot;</p>
<p>Four thousand and two hundred years ago…? She was referring to the time of the Second Great Human-Demon War, right?</p>
<p>&quot;If I recall correctly, that was when the Fighting God battled against the Dragon God,&quot; I said.</p>
<p>&quot;Sure is. Badi donned the Fighting God Armor to protect me and faced off with the Demonic Dragon King Laplace.&quot;</p>
<p>I gawked at her. &quot;Wait…Badi as in His Majesty, Badigadi?&quot;</p>
<p>My shock was immeasurable.</p>
<p>Did this mean Badigadi was actually the Fighting God all along? Orsted had never told me anything about that, though I felt as though I had heard something similar somewhere before… <em>Ah, Randolph, I think.</em> So what he said was true? At the time, I couldn&#x27;t determine whether he was talking about the same guy or not.</p>
<p>&quot;It&#x27;s been a long time since he lost the Fighting God Armor… But if Badi shows up, best be careful. He still feels indebted to that rotten Man-God after all this time. He may end up being your enemy.&quot;</p>
<p>Badigadi was such a cheerful, bright man. I didn&#x27;t want to fight him if I could help it. Still, I had to make a mental note that he might end up on the other side. If at all possible, I hoped he would forget whatever debt he felt and join our side instead.</p>
<p>&quot;Well, since you have Atofe on your side, I doubt Badi will be a match for you in his current state. I&#x27;d ask you to spare his life if you can help it, though,&quot; said Kishirika.</p>
<p>Badigadi was Atofe&#x27;s younger brother and Kishirika&#x27;s fiancé. Family, in other words. Demons were rather quick to let go of grudges from my experience, but not so magnanimous that they would stand quietly by as their family was murdered.</p>
<p>&quot;Very well,&quot; I agreed. &quot;Although, he&#x27;s not one to be easily killed in the first place anyway.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;No, he&#x27;s not. The immortal demons&#x27; strong point is their tenacity.&quot; As she spoke, Kishirika snuck a glance at Atofe. The latter was posed proudly, but I had a feeling Kishirika wasn&#x27;t exactly complimenting her. &quot;And also…come just a little closer.&quot; She beckoned me with her hand.</p>
<p>I obeyed and leaned in. She cupped a hand to her mouth, presumably to whisper to me. &quot;Bring your face a tiiiny bit closer.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;What is it?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;There, take this!&quot; She suddenly thrust her fingers into my left eye. Indescribable pain shot through the entire socket.</p>
<p>&quot;Gaaaaaah!&quot; I screamed at the top of my lungs, trying instinctively to pull away. She grabbed me by the hair, pinning me so I couldn&#x27;t run away. I was using my Magic Armor—the Version Two—so where could I run?! This freaking hurt!</p>
<p><em>Oh, wait, I know what this is. Maybe it&#x27;s fine not to run.</em></p>
<p>&quot;Oh? Decided to behave yourself, have you?&quot;</p>
<p>I willingly let her do her work. It was painful, to be sure—a throbbing, splitting pain assaulted my entire brain. This had come without warning, and she was digging around in my eye socket, but at least I knew what she was doing. I had been through this before with my first eye.</p>
<p>&quot;There, finished,&quot; she declared, finally yanking her fingers out.</p>
<p>The intense, overwhelming pain remained, and I could see nothing out of the eye she had fiddled with. This was no reason to panic, however. I knew from past experience that it wasn&#x27;t permanent.</p>
<p>&quot;It&#x27;s my personal policy to reward a person with an eye whenever they treat me to something delicious,&quot; Kishirika explained.</p>
<p>I said nothing in response.</p>
<p>&quot;This will be your second eye.&quot;</p>
<p>I pressed a hand over my left eye as the pain slowly receded and knelt in front of Kishirika.</p>
<p>&quot;I&#x27;m not the least bit concerned with this battle of yours, but I do have a bit of a bone to pick with the Man-God after what he did. That&#x27;s why I&#x27;m giving this to you as a parting gift.&quot;</p>
<p>I lowered my hand from my eye. My vision doubled. It was a jarring sight, as if I was holding the palm of my hand over one of my eyes, giving me two different things to look at once. Boy, this was gonna cause some headaches.</p>
<p>&quot;It&#x27;s an Eye of Distant Sight,&quot; Kishirika informed me. &quot;All it allows you to do is see in the distance, but it should prove useful.&quot;</p>
<p>An Eye of Distant Sight, huh? I gave it a spin by closing my right eye and focusing mana into my left. It worked the same way as my right; by adjusting the amount of mana I used with it, I could gaze into the faraway distance.</p>
<p>I glanced down, and my vision passed through the fortress all the way to the entrance, where one of the black knights had removed his helmet to scratch the top of his head. I moved my head and focused mana into the eye again. My vision flashed, soaring through the sky, staring far off into the distance. It was almost like a camera with a zoom function.</p>
<p>Next, I saw a crater with a town nestled in the middle of it. However, I couldn&#x27;t make out the entirety of the town. When I tried to gaze even further into the distance, focusing more mana into my eye, I found my vision could go no further than some mountains. I could make out the detailed patterns on the rocks of the mountain and a Great Tortoise which threw its head up in a yawn, but no further. If there was something obstructing my view, my vision would stop there.</p>
<p>I cut off the flow of mana to my eye, and immediately it reverted back to my ordinary vision. This new demon eye merely allowed me to see far away. It wasn&#x27;t incredibly powerful, nor did it seem particularly easy to use. Still, I was already thinking of scenarios where it might be useful.</p>
<p>&quot;The way you are now, two demon eyes should be no difficulty for you to handle.&quot;</p>
<p>I told her sincerely and gratefully, &quot;Thank you.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Yes, yes. Well then, Rudeus! Feel free to rely upon me again should you need me! Provided it doesn&#x27;t involve the Man-God, I will be happy to help!&quot; Kishirika nimbly released the shackles from her hands then brought the side of her hand down in a chopping motion, removing the ball and chain trailing from her legs. Finally, she yanked off the striped pajamas she&#x27;d been forced to wear, revealing her usual bondage-style outfit.</p>
<p>&quot;Farewell, then! Off I—bwah?!&quot; Kishirika had leapt into the air, intending to make her escape, but she face-planted on the ground thanks to the firm grip Atofe had on her ankle.</p>
<p>&quot;Wait,&quot; said Atofe.</p>
<p>&quot;What do you want? You have some nerve, interrupting my smooth exit.&quot; Blood trickled down from Kishirika&#x27;s nose. She fixed Atofe with a glare.</p>
<p>Atofe stared down at her, not the least bit bothered. &quot;Do me a favor, too.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;What&#x27;s this? You took me captive for no reason and threw me into prison. I won&#x27;t be doing any favors for you. Unhand me. Shoo, shoo!&quot; She slapped Atofe&#x27;s hand away, using her other to wipe away the crimson trail trickling from her nose.</p>
<p>Atofe wasn&#x27;t one to be so easily rebuffed. She grabbed Kishirika by the collar. Atofe&#x27;s grip pulled the tight leather halter top tighter, lifting ever so slightly to reveal her flat breasts beneath.</p>
<p><em>Ooh!</em> I shook my head. <em>No, I&#x27;m Rudeus the Abstinent. I must resist such temptation! Khh!</em></p>
<p>&quot;Tell me where Al and Alex are. Rudeus needs strong fighters on his side, right? Those two would be perfect for this.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;What?&quot; Kishirika frowned. &quot;I gave Rudeus his reward just a moment ago and told him someone&#x27;s location. I even gave him a Demon Eye as a special extra thanks. I can give no more.&quot;</p>
<p>Al and Alex? I was pretty sure those were nicknames for the two surviving North Gods. Those close to them tended to call them by those nicknames. I didn&#x27;t remember bringing it up to Atofe that I had been</p>
<p>looking for those two, but they <em>were</em> her family. Perhaps she hadn&#x27;t needed any prompting to bring them up.</p>
<p>&quot;Tell me,&quot; Atofe demanded.</p>
<p>&quot;I told you already, no!&quot;</p>
<p>Kishirika seemed disinclined to fulfill Atofe&#x27;s request. It was good that I had learned Geese&#x27;s current location, but I still knew nothing about what he was scheming. I needed to increase my number of allies if at all possible. We needed all the help we could get.</p>
<p><em>If at all possible, huh?</em> An idea suddenly came to mind. <em>That&#x27;s right. I have this!</em> I suddenly remembered the sinister, skull-shaped ring on my finger—Randolph&#x27;s ring.</p>
<p>&quot;Lady Kishirika? My lady, please, take a look at this,&quot; I bid her.</p>
<p>&quot;Hm? What&#x27;s this? I feel as though I have seen this somewhere before…and it gives me an ill premonition.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Consider it Randolph&#x27;s request.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Mm… Randolph, eh? I remember now. That was his ring!&quot; Her expression was almost theatrical; all the color had drained from her face. &quot;Now I see, yes. His request, is it? He did certainly look after me. I always wondered why, each time he did so, he&#x27;d say, &#x27;You can repay me for this later. At some point in the future, all right? Kehehe!&#x27; All with that unsettling laugh. Each time I glimpsed that smile, I&#x27;d tremble with fear, wondering what vile thing he would ask of me.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Do as we ask, and you can consider all of your debts to him repaid.&quot;</p>
<p>Her face lit up. &quot;All of it, you say? Welp, guess I have no choice! Yes, wait just a moment!&quot; Again, she turned her eye to the empty air. It took only a few seconds of searching before she found what she was looking for.</p>
