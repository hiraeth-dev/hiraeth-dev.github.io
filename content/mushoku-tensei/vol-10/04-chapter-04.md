<p><em>RANOA KINGDOM, the Magic City of Sharia:</em></p>
<p>In a section of this city—heavily populated with students—was an old manor with many problems. A single step down the drive brought you to an untended garden, and then to a broken front door. The walls and ceilings had water damage, and the roof leaked when it rained. There was a fireplace that might or might not be in working condition, and the outer walls were shrouded in moss and shriveled vines. In short, it was less a house and more an abandoned ruin.</p>
<p>Even better? The house was haunted.</p>
<p>Surprisingly enough, a man named Rudeus Greyrat was attempting to move into the house. A former A-ranked adventurer and current student of the University of Magic, Rudeus had bought the house for himself and his future wife to live in. Peculiar taste, for sure. Not many people would choose such a place to begin their newlywed life.</p>
<p>One man took up this client&#x27;s call: Balda of the Large Hollow, an artisan and renovator, and an expert architect affiliated with the Magicians&#x27; Guild in the Duchy of Basherant. He had thirty years of experience that encompassed everything from designing the layout of a building to actually constructing it. Having acquired his skills in the Holy Country of Millis, he had a number of notable achievements under his belt, such as constructing a detached school building for the University of Magic.</p>
<p>Balda was a bit of a stubborn man, but a good one whose skills were undeniable. He always had a hammer by his side, and if he found something he didn&#x27;t like, even if it was a stranger&#x27;s house, he&#x27;d tear it down and rebuild it. Such was the temperament of a</p>
<p>craftsman. He&#x27;d beat anything into shape with his hammer, whether it be buildings or his own pupils. That was how he&#x27;d acquired another nickname: Balda the Hammer.</p>
<p>&quot;Aha. We&#x27;re here. You must be Quagmire! Heard you&#x27;re gettin&#x27; married!&quot;</p>
<p>The person who welcomed the artisan was the client himself, a man known on the streets as &quot;Rudeus the Quagmire,&quot; though more affectionately referred to as &quot;Quagmire&quot; by the artisan.</p>
<p>&quot;Yes. I&#x27;m in your hands, Mister Balda.&quot;</p>
<p>Balda knew Rudeus. Talhand was an old friend of his, and he&#x27;d heard of Rudeus through Talhand&#x27;s companion, Elinalise.</p>
<p>&quot;I&#x27;m glad I was able to buy a house for my new wife, but as you can see, it needs some work.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Well, why don&#x27;t ya let me have a look?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Be my guest.&quot;</p>
<p>As soon as they tried to enter the house, the artisan furrowed his brows. &quot;Hey now, what&#x27;s this here? This doorway&#x27;s in bad shape. Almost like the door got ripped right off its hinges.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;It didn&#x27;t fit right and couldn&#x27;t be opened, so we had no choice but to break it,&quot; Rudeus explained.</p>
<p>&quot;Tsk, honestly,&quot; the dwarf spat. &quot;You kids just like to go breakin&#x27; everything. You don&#x27;t have any respect for things.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I agree completely.&quot;</p>
<p>The client easily brushed off the artisan&#x27;s angry words. He spoke as if he had nothing to do with the door&#x27;s destruction. The artisan didn&#x27;t care much for that kind of attitude, but he held his feelings in. He&#x27;d heard that Rudeus the Quagmire was quite the terrifying individual if you provoked his anger.</p>
<p>&quot;So whatcha wanna do about the door?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;What do you mean?&quot; Rudeus asked.</p>
<p>&quot;Quality of materials, design, that kinda stuff. If you don&#x27;t got a preference, I&#x27;ll just use my own know-how,&quot; Balda explained.</p>
<p>&quot;I don&#x27;t have any particular preference when it comes to materials, but I would like to request a sturdy door. Also, please add a door knocker.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;&#x27;Course. This is the front entrance, after all.&quot;</p>
<p>After that they headed inside, where the artisan once again wore a look of mixed emotion. &quot;This place&#x27;s really had a number done on it.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I-It has?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;The floor&#x27;s oddly well made, but the walls and ceiling are pretty shoddy work in comparison. Almost like the basement&#x27;s the most important part of the house and everything else is just extra.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;You can tell all of that?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;&#x27;Course I can.&quot;</p>
<p>Balda&#x27;s eyes could easily tell what was well made and what wasn&#x27;t. The floor, stairs, second floor, dining area, kitchen and fireplace were all solid work. He could tell that a talented builder had exercised their architectural skills and magic abilities to create this a hundred years ago. But someone else had made renovations to the walls and ceiling. That&#x27;s where it&#x27;d gone haywire.</p>
<p>&quot;Well, this can be fixed real quick.&quot;</p>
<p>The artisan&#x27;s words were reassuring. Relieved, the client led him to a large dining area.</p>
<p>&quot;A large room, eh? Sunlight in here ain&#x27;t bad,&quot; said Balda.</p>
<p>&quot;How about the fireplace?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Let&#x27;s see.&quot; The dwarf&#x27;s eyes lit up at the fireplace that might or might not be useable. &quot;This is a nice fireplace. Bit old, but probably best we not go makin&#x27; adjustments to it.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Are you sure?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Here, look at this mark chiseled in here.&quot; Balda pointed to the emblem that Rudeus was sure he&#x27;d seen somewhere before. &quot;This is the mark of a genius craftsman. His name&#x27;s been lost to time, but in the Asura Kingdom, magical implements with this mark on them fetch a high price. Most of them are small gadgets, though. Who&#x27;d have thought the same person woulda created a whole fireplace somewhere like this?&quot;</p>
<p>The client thought back to the crest on the diary he&#x27;d found in this house just a few days before, finally realizing that it looked awfully similar to this one. It seemed the original owner of the house had built these things himself.</p>
<p>&quot;So, what do ya wanna do with this big room?&quot; Balda asked.</p>
<p>&quot;That&#x27;s a good question. What do you normally do with a room like this?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Well, it&#x27;s a big area. Plonk down a big table and you can use it for parties. Have the one on the other wing of the house as a spare. If somethin&#x27; comes up and ya can&#x27;t use this room, then you can use that one instead.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;So you wouldn&#x27;t use it most of the time?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Not normally, no. Then again, for most us livin&#x27; normal everyday lives, one big room is more than enough.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I suppose you&#x27;re right. Let&#x27;s use the room in the other wing as a lounge, then.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Aye.&quot;</p>
<p>The artisan and his client continued their exchange as they moved to the next room.</p>
<p>&quot;Ya got two kitchens here, too. Though the second one&#x27;s got no kiln.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I assume that means it wasn&#x27;t used, then?&quot; Rudeus asked.</p>
<p>&quot;Got a drainage pipe, so it was probably used for washin&#x27; and bathin&#x27;.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Oh, so a bathroom!&quot;</p>
<p>The artisan looked at the kitchen, then the washing area. He checked for deterioration and clogging in the plumbing, then nodded. &quot;This place is fine without any repairs. It&#x27;s pretty clean, for how much it&#x27;s been used. Though might not have been used much to begin with.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;There is one thing I&#x27;d like to consult you about,&quot; the client said, following up with his own suggestion.</p>
<p>The artisan&#x27;s eyes lit up. &quot;You think of some interestin&#x27; stuff. But I don&#x27;t have the materials for that, so it might cost ya.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I&#x27;ll create them myself with magic.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Got it all figured out, eh? Very well. Let&#x27;s see what we can do.&quot; And so the client entrusted the artisan with his idea.</p>
<p>***</p>
<p>The next day, ten of Balda&#x27;s underlings gathered together and the renovations began.</p>
<p>PART 1: DOOR</p>
<p>Early in the morning, a large door made of expensive wood shaved down to fit the frame was carried in. On the outside of the sturdy slab was a door knocker in the form of a lion, with a magic circle drawn into the edge of the door as a security measure.</p>
<p>&quot;It ain&#x27;t much, but if anyone tries to force the door open, a loud noise will echo throughout the house,&quot; the dwarf said. &quot;Could be an alarm clock, too.&quot;</p>
<p>The client laughed boldly at the artisan&#x27;s idea.</p>
<p>PART 2: WASHING AREA</p>
<p>Under the artisan&#x27;s skilled ministrations, this area was undergoing quite the change. First, a partition was put in to split the area in two. The stone flooring was replaced by tile and canted toward a drain in one corner of the room. In another corner, a square stone box large enough for three people to lie in was installed. The floor below was slightly indented so the box could be set in place. Then a window was installed close to the ceiling. What exactly was this room supposed to be?</p>
<p>PART 3: BASEMENT ROOM</p>
<p>The client and artisan stood in the darkness of the basement.</p>
<p>&quot;This is a nice basement area. The way it&#x27;s built, you&#x27;ll hardly ever get mice comin&#x27; in.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Yes. Well, about this hidden door here. Behind it, I&#x27;d like you to create a room like this.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Why&#x27;re you wantin&#x27; such a strange—ah, forget it. I won&#x27;t say nothin&#x27;. I&#x27;m a good follower of Millis, but it looks like <em>you</em> sure aren&#x27;t.&quot;</p>
<p>Machinery and materials were brought into the basement to fulfill the client&#x27;s wishes, and the stains on the corners of the hidden door were completely washed away.</p>
<p>***</p>

<p>&quot;Oh, I wonder what it is you want to show me. I am so excited!&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;No, I mean…um, you know. I&#x27;m not very—I mean, in the chest area. They&#x27;re kind of small.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Yes, ma&#x27;am. I&#x27;m sorry.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Yes, Miss Sylphiette. I will never do it again.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Ah, b-but if you really can&#x27;t hold yourself back…then hmm…&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Oho? You&#x27;re gonna have to speak up, kid, this old man&#x27;s ears aren&#x27;t like they used to be.&quot;</p>
<p>The two of them took a look at their new house.</p>
<p>BEFORE:</p>
<p>Moss clung to the the stones and ivy snaked its way across the outside of the house. The windows were shattered and the front door hung off its frame. The Rudeus Estate gave off an eerie aura, as if it were home to a witch.</p>
<p>NOW:</p>
<p>The previously moss-covered stones had all been cleaned and polished, and a new coat of pure white paint had been applied to the outer walls. The roof, previously so dull you couldn&#x27;t tell its original shade, was now a bright green. Sturdy dark-brown double doors had been installed in the entryway. The doors had sparkling, lion-shaped gold hinges that looked almost like guard dogs.</p>
<p>Seeing this, the wife covered her mouth.</p>
<p>&quot;What do you think?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Um, uh, what do I think?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I picked a color close to your original hair color for the roof. You might not have liked your hair, but I really did.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Huh? Oh, I see. Aah…&quot; She kept her hand pressed to her mouth, eyes full of admiration as she looked over the house.</p>
<p>&quot;Come on then, let&#x27;s go inside and see the rest of it.&quot;</p>
<p>The two of them went inside. A mat lay at the front entrance for them to wipe their feet on—a representation of the client&#x27;s feelings about this world&#x27;s culture of wearing shoes indoors.</p>
<p>&quot;To the right is the dining room. To the left is the living room. Which would you like to see first?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Um, I guess the &#x27;dining&#x27; one first?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;So you prefer the dining room! Very good. I&#x27;m sure you&#x27;ll take an even bigger liking to this place, once you see it. Come this way.&quot; The client&#x27;s nervousness was filtering into his speech, as if he were some kind of car salesman.</p>
<p>The two made their way from the foyer to a room on the left. The previously large, empty room had undergone quite the transformation. First, a long table had been placed within. It was bare at the moment, but it looked capable of seating ten people. The walls were covered in white wallpaper, and in the corner was a vase with a small arrangement of flowers. The large fireplace had been repaired with brand-new red bricks that accented the rest of the room.</p>
<p>&quot;Whoa, this is amazing.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;We&#x27;ll eat either here or in the living area,&quot; the man said.</p>
<p>&quot;What are we going to do with a table this long?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I&#x27;m sure we&#x27;ll use it when we invite people over.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Oh, that makes sense. You&#x27;re right. We&#x27;ll have guests over.&quot; The girl removed her sunglasses and scratched at the back of her ears.</p>
<p>He reached over and patted her on the head, an affectionate look on his face. No doubt the client was inwardly thinking not only of potential guests but of filling the seats at the table with their children.</p>
<p>&quot;Okay, then! To the living room.&quot;</p>
<p>They moved to the living room. Spread out before them was a large, welcoming, family-oriented space. Couches were installed around the fireplace. A table sat close by with a pitcher and some cups resting atop it. The artisan had displayed magnificent ingenuity in implementing the client&#x27;s desire for a relaxing house so naturally.</p>
<p>&quot;This is amazing. Can I sit on this?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Of course you can! Ah, but please don&#x27;t even mention that the cushions are hard, I already know. They&#x27;ll soften more with wear, I&#x27;m told.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I haven&#x27;t even sat down yet. In fact, Rudy, you&#x27;ve been talking weird for a bit now.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I&#x27;m just a little nervous.&quot;</p>
<p>His wife cautiously took a seat on the couch. &quot;It&#x27;s not really hard at all.&quot;</p>
<p>The client settled down beside his wife. Then he wrapped an arm around her shoulder and the two faced each other, gazes connecting. His wife softly closed her eyes and—</p>
<p>He pulled her back up onto her feet. &quot;Wh-why don&#x27;t we go see the next room? It&#x27;s the kitchen. The Rudeus Estate boasts a fantastic meal preparation area; come have a look!&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Uh, yeah!&quot;</p>
<p>Besides the existing stone kiln, the kitchen also hosted an assortment of the newest cooking equipment. There was a counter large enough to butcher an entire boar on, and a cooking stove with a gigantic generic pot. There were also casks, jars and earthen containers for storage purposes.</p>
<p>&quot;It&#x27;s so normal.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;It sure is.&quot;</p>
<p>As her husband&#x27;s expression turned solemn, the wife in turn gave her own solemn nod. Once that was over with, they were on to the next area—the washroom. They walked down the hallway and slipped in through the entryway. When they did, the wife tilted her head.</p>
<p>&quot;Oh? It&#x27;s quite small.&quot;</p>
<p>There was a large bucket and washboard in the room, and nothing else. It was more than enough space to do laundry in, but what caught her attention was the door at the back.</p>
<p>&quot;Have a look.&quot; The client led his wife through the door.</p>
<p>The sight that awaited her was an enormous bath.</p>
<p>BEFORE:</p>
<p>It was nothing more than a plain room with no stone kiln, too large to be used just for washing clothes in. A desolate second kitchen area.</p>
<p>NOW:</p>
<p>The floor was replaced with tile, and at the edge of the room was a large tub filled with warm water. It was angled such that the water trickled smoothly down the drain that had been installed. The room that had formerly been covered in stone was now a stylish bath.</p>
<p>&quot;Um, could this perhaps be…a bath?&quot; His wife asked.</p>
<p>&quot;I should&#x27;ve expected you&#x27;d figure it out. You know what a bath is, then?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Oh, yes. I had a little experience with them when I lived in the royal palace. But this is the first time I&#x27;ve ever seen one this big before. Is this what you call a hot spring?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;It&#x27;s a little different from a hot spring.&quot;</p>
<p>She couldn&#x27;t mask her surprise. The client watched her with a curious expression. You could almost hear his sinister inner voice saying, &quot;I&#x27;m looking forward to bathing together, heh heh heh&quot; just from the look on his face.</p>
<p>&quot;I put water in it just so I could show you, but normally we&#x27;ll keep it empty.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Okay. You can teach me how to use it later. Ahh!&quot;</p>
<p>He&#x27;d suddenly thrown his arms around her. Apparently, he was overcome with emotion at her words.</p>
<p>&quot;Jeez, what&#x27;s this about?&quot; she demanded.</p>

<p>Once their conversation was over, they took the stairs and ascended to the second floor. The ceiling had been beautifully restored with bright wooden panels, eliminating all concerns about being dripped on when it rained. The client took his wife straight to the door furthest in.</p>
<p>&quot;Right now, this is the only room I&#x27;ve remodeled on the second floor.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Ah, it&#x27;s amazing.&quot; His wife&#x27;s eyes widened with surprise as she entered. The most conspicuous thing in the room, of course, was the massive bed wide enough for three people to comfortably sleep in. There was only one pillow on it: the client&#x27;s favorite. &quot;Why such a big bed?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;That&#x27;s obvious, of course. It&#x27;s so we can really enjoy ourselves when we&#x27;re alone together.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Oh, so that&#x27;s it. I guess that makes sense. Hee hee hee.&quot;</p>
<p>They both wore toothy grins on their faces.</p>
