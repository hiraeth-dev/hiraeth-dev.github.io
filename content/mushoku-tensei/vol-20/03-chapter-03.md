<p><strong>Chapter 3: Cliff and the Institute of Magic Student Council</strong></p>
<p><strong>T</strong>HAT DAY, Cliff visited the teachers&#x27; office. Graduation loomed on the horizon, so it was time for the special students to submit their research reports. The subject of Cliff&#x27;s report was &quot;Research into the Suppression of Curses via Magical Implements.&quot; The teachers immediately started poring over the report and passing copies around, all while giving Cliff glowing praise. The submission soon sparked an impromptu Q&amp;A session and debate, whipping the staff room into a fervor. Cliff even heard one teacher say that the results of his research would make history. But the head teacher, Jenius, had something else to say.</p>
<p>&quot;I apologize that I cannot do more in light of such groundbreaking research…but the valedictorian has already been selected.&quot;</p>
<p>This year&#x27;s valedictorian would be someone named Brooklyn von Elzas from the Duchy of Neris. Cliff knew that name; it was someone he&#x27;d spent the past few years competing with over test scores. Cliff recalled that he&#x27;d never once lost to Brooklyn.</p>
<p>&quot;I&#x27;m sorry. Perhaps this might not be the place to speak of it, but you held the most outstanding grades out of the entire graduating class. You should be proud.&quot;</p>
<p>Cliff&#x27;s only response to this news was an &quot;all right, I see,&quot; before leaving the staff room. The old Cliff might have thrown an almighty fit at the teachers, but the past seven years had changed him. Furthering his studies, making new friends, and working as a priest had given him many new experiences. From those experiences came maturity. The school had their position to consider. Running a university wasn&#x27;t cheap. Countries were powerful. People were not equal. You had to accept your lot in life and press on.</p>
<p>Besides, Cliff didn&#x27;t see much value in the &quot;University of Magic Valedictorian&quot; title. Cliff had friends who lacked titles but were no less amazing. One in particular currently had the title of &quot;Right Hand of the Dragon God,&quot; but that wasn&#x27;t a job he&#x27;d applied for. It was simply the outcome of his actions.</p>
<p><em>Absolutely. The fruit of experience.</em> Thinking about it, Cliff couldn&#x27;t help but laugh at how foolish chasing mere titles was.</p>
<p>If he had one misgiving, it was that his research wasn&#x27;t finished. His thesis was called &quot;Research into the Suppression of Curses via Magical Implements.&quot; If he could have tweaked it just a bit, if he could have replaced &quot;Suppression&quot; with &quot;Removal,&quot; then Cliff would have had no regrets. But sadly, his incomplete research meant he couldn&#x27;t speak in absolutes. Still, he&#x27;d accomplished <em>something</em>. Both Elinalise and Orsted had thanked him for easing their curses. But the end goal still eluded his grasp.</p>
<p>&quot;…&quot;</p>
<p>Cliff sidled up to the windowsill and stared outside. The grounds of the University of Magic had hardly changed in the past seven years.</p>
<p><em>You know,</em> he thought, <em>I was a lot cockier when I first came here.</em></p>
<p>Back then, Cliff knew absolutely he was a genius. But the years had beaten him down, making him painfully aware that he was nothing special. Sure, compared to other students, his grades were exceptional. The old Cliff might have lorded it over others with a smirk. But the current Cliff didn&#x27;t feel like boasting or demeaning himself. The past seven years had been so rich for him, filled with so many once-in-a-lifetime experiences. His marriage with Elinalise, his research on curses, the bizarre doll in Rudeus&#x27;s manor, the battle on the Demon Continent, the Demon Eye he was given, the birth of Clive… There was just so much that had happened, so many things that he had to face with his whole heart to overcome. It was those challenges that made him the man he was today, not any inborn talent. Remembering that kept him grounded.</p>
<p>His experience might have been why Cliff was so well-regarded by his congregation when he worked as an apprentice Millis priest. They said that despite his youth, he had extraordinary empathy. Sometimes they even told him he&#x27;d grow up to be a fine priest. When the priest in charge of the Sharia church gave Cliff his priest certificate, he also gave him his blessing by saying, &quot;You&#x27;ll do fine anywhere you go.&quot; The priest would never have said that if Cliff were still the same boy he was seven years ago.</p>
<p>&quot;Phew…&quot;</p>
<p>A smile bubbled up from within Cliff. He still hadn&#x27;t become the man he once dreamed of being—he was better than that man. He preferred this version of himself.</p>
<p>&quot;Now, where to go from here…&quot;</p>
<p>His research report was submitted, and there was now little time left until</p>
<p>the graduation ceremony. Cliff had told Rudeus that he&#x27;d give an answer by graduation, but he had yet to settle on one. He wanted to return to Millishion. But he had a wife and child now. Cliff&#x27;s parents had died in a power struggle within the Millis Church. Specifically, his grandfather&#x27;s power struggle as the Millis pope. Returning to Millishion would absolutely put Elinalise and Clive in danger. And then Rudeus dropped a solution into Cliff&#x27;s lap. He wanted Cliff to assist Orsted as a member of the Millis Church. To forge an alliance. If he could do that, then Rudeus would offer all the help needed for Cliff to rise through the ranks. He&#x27;d see to it that Elinalise and Clive were protected.</p>
<p>It was everything Cliff could ask for and more. But past arrogance aside, Cliff didn&#x27;t see himself being worth that kind of support now. Certainly not from someone as amazing as Rudeus—Cliff may have had his doubts about Rudeus when they first met, but he was sincere and a hard worker. And it was no exaggeration to say that most of Cliff&#x27;s &quot;once-in-a-lifetime experiences&quot; only happened because of Rudeus. Someone that extraordinary asking <em>Cliff</em> for help was probably a show of friendship more than anything else.</p>
<p>Still, this was everything he could have wanted. Elinalise and Clive would be safe, he&#x27;d have Orsted&#x27;s formidable backing, and the road to the top of the Millis Church hierarchy would be wide open. It was everything Cliff wanted. And yet, something about it felt subtly off. Cliff didn&#x27;t yet understand why that was.</p>
<p>What should he do? What did he want? Every day he agonized over it until it was time to go home to Elinalise and stop thinking.</p>
<p>&quot;I guess I&#x27;ll hang around for a bit longer.&quot;</p>
<p>Cliff had planned to go straight home after submitting the report, but he turned sharply on his heel. If he went home now, then the day would end like all the others did. That wouldn&#x27;t be good.</p>
<p>Saint Millis once said, &quot;If childbirth be the duty of people, then shun it not, but indulge in it not.&quot; Saint Millis also once said, &quot;Let thyself anguish, and do not flee from thy anguish.&quot; That meant that it wasn&#x27;t right to flee from his anguish and indulge in Elinalise. The phrase &quot;Always let thy heart be at ease&quot; was also in the teachings of Millis, so fraying his own nerves over this was no good either.</p>
<p>But he had to make a decision soon. A decision on how he would answer Rudeus&#x27;s request.</p>
<p>Cliff had said that he&#x27;d decide after discussing it with Elinalise, but Elinalise had no comment either way. All she said was to think for himself. She said it not to abandon Cliff, but to give him a gentle push. If that was Elinalise&#x27;s stance, then Cliff felt obligated to work this out on his own. Elinalise would live a very long time—many times longer than Cliff would. In fact, their child likely would as well. Compared to her experience, Cliff was a baby. Yet Elinalise never treated him like a child; she saw him as her beloved husband. Elinalise respected him, so Cliff wanted to return the favor.</p>
<p>&quot;I can do this. I <em>am</em> a genius.&quot;</p>
<p>That phrase slipped out like a habit. It was once something he believed without question; now, it was a mantra to motivate him to action. He knew full well by now that he was no genius, but it cheered him up to repeat those old words and remember believing that they were true.</p>
<p>&quot;I&#x27;m…we…should…!&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Hm?&quot;</p>
<p>Cliff faintly heard the echo of arguing voices from down the hall. Fights weren&#x27;t particularly rare at the University of Magic. At any other time, Cliff would have ignored it. But in this moment, Cliff found himself drawn to it and descended the stairs. Among those voices was one Cliff recognized.</p>
<p>&quot;That&#x27;s what I said! We&#x27;re the ones who should be doing this!&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Exactly! We can&#x27;t expect other people to wipe our ass! We&#x27;ve gotta protect this school ourselves!&quot;</p>
<p>A number of students were shouting while gathered around a petite girl. They weren&#x27;t threatening her, though; it seemed she was some kind of leader, so the others were pleading with her to make a call. And that girl was someone Cliff knew well.</p>
<p>&quot;Please, President!&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;You have to let us go, President Norn!&quot;</p>
<p>It was Norn Greyrat. She stood scowling, surrounded by the other students.</p>
<p>&quot;Norn, what&#x27;s wrong?&quot; Cliff called out. &quot;Is there some sort of problem?&quot;</p>
<p>All of the students, Norn included, turned to face Cliff. Her expression relaxed a bit, but other students stepped forward before she could respond.</p>
<p>&quot;This is student council business!&quot;</p>
<p>Standing in Cliff&#x27;s way was a girl about as tall as him and a beastman who might have been twice his height. Cliff recognized the two of them as well; they were current members of the student council.</p>
<p>&quot;Hey, guys! Could you move, please?&quot;</p>
<p>Norn wedged herself between the two and pried them apart so she could wriggle through. It was the kind of motion that Rudeus, if he were here, would have said a dumb pun to himself for, like &quot;Wow, Norn&#x27;s really <em>come between you</em>!&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I&#x27;m sorry, Cliff,&quot; Norn said. &quot;Everyone here&#x27;s just a bit worked up.&quot; &quot;Cliff Grimor… This kid? The one from the Demonic Circle of Six?&quot; &quot;He&#x27;s not just a &#x27;kid.&#x27; He&#x27;s someone I owe a great deal to!&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Oh… Sorry.&quot;</p>
<p>The beastman muttered an apology, but kept his glare. The old Cliff might have responded to that look with hostility or fear. The current Cliff had seen worse. Things that would strike fear into any reasonable heart merely by existing. Compared to Orsted or Atofe, this beastman was a puppy.</p>
<p>&quot;So, what happened?&quot; Cliff asked. &quot;Could you tell me, if it isn&#x27;t too much trouble?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Well…&quot; Norn began. &quot;The truth is, there are rumors that a ghost is haunting the school.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Hmm.&quot;</p>
<p>Cliff had heard those rumors as well. Every night, people would hear moaning voices or rattling noises, or they&#x27;d see a translucent figure in the hall… so the stories went. In fact, there were even students who&#x27;d collapsed, sapped of all their mana. But it was far from uncommon at the University of Magic to see students passed out from excessive practice, and ghosts were a common rumor. Or so Cliff thought…</p>
<p>&quot;So, next, well… When we went to investigate, we found a door deep within an unused underground storehouse that had a strong seal placed on it. When we opened it, Skeletons came out.&quot;</p>
<p>Norn fumbled over her words as she explained to Cliff. It was like she was hiding something. Cliff was sure she was, but he chose to let it go.</p>
<p>&quot;Yeah, it sounds like you messed up. If something&#x27;s heavily sealed, then whoever sealed it probably had a good reason for doing so.&quot;</p>
<p>A loud &quot;Guh!&quot; was heard from among the student council. It came from a feisty-looking girl with pigtails; she was probably the culprit who lifted the seal.</p>
<p>&quot;For now, we got a teacher&#x27;s help to reapply the seal,&quot; Norn continued, with a tone suggesting that this was where everything went wrong.</p>
<p>The door was sealed with Saint-tier barrier magic. A Wraith had slipped through that Saint-tier barrier and appeared outside. That meant it was probably a high-level Wraith lurking in that underground storehouse.</p>
<p>The university contacted the Magicians&#x27; Guild and requested professionals who could exterminate it. Or, that was the plan, but it&#x27;d hit a snag. Beginner-tier divine magic was more than enough to take down a typical Wraith, but highlevel Wraiths were different beasts. If it was an A-rank Deadly Wraith inside that storehouse, they&#x27;d at least need Advanced-tier divine magic. Unfortunately, there weren&#x27;t any Advanced-tier divine magicians in the Magicians&#x27; Guild.</p>
<p>The university gave up and contacted the Adventurers&#x27; Guild in the hopes of obtaining an Advanced-tier divine magician, but alas, this wasn&#x27;t Millis; Advanced-tier divine magicians weren&#x27;t exactly on every street corner here in the Northern Territories. To make it even worse, the Magicians&#x27; Guild complained about the idea. They&#x27;d have to call in a divine magician from a different town&#x27;s branch. Borrowing a magician from the Adventurers&#x27; Guild would hurt their reputation, they said. But even if the school could get the divine magician from another town to come, they wouldn&#x27;t show up right away.</p>
<p>And so, the days passed…until the first victim appeared.</p>
<p>The cause was uncertain; maybe the seal hadn&#x27;t been reapplied correctly, or maybe it&#x27;d been faulty since its first application. The victim was an unnamed female student who fell into a coma after the Wraith attacked her and drained her of her mana. Her only symptom was simple mana exhaustion, not anything lifethreatening. She was back in class the next day.</p>
<p>But ever since, the victim count steadily climbed.</p>
<p>For the moment, it appeared that the Wraith was still warded within the seal and could only escape outside to attack students at a particular time of day. But Wraiths would steadily power up with each feast of human mana they consumed. If it continued to attack students, it would soon grow strong enough to break through and bring an army of Skeletons with it. The potential fallout</p>
<p>could be catastrophic.</p>
<p>&quot;That&#x27;s why some among the student council have suggested that we should go down there and defeat the Wraith before that happens…&quot; Norn concluded.</p>
<p>&quot;I can at least use Beginner-tier divine magic!&quot; chimed in one student.</p>
<p>&quot;I bought some weapons from the workshop district that are strong against Wraiths!&quot; chimed in another.</p>
<p>&quot;This is what we&#x27;ve been studying magic for!&quot; added one more.</p>
<p>&quot;President, please, give us the word!&quot;</p>
<p>Wraiths were by no means impossible to fell by methods besides divine magic. Normal attacks had some small effect, and magical items or implements would inflict damage. In that sense, a divine magician was not <em>strictly</em> necessary to exterminate a Wraith.</p>
<p>&quot;Hmm, I see,&quot; said Cliff. &quot;Well, what do you think?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I&#x27;m against it,&quot; stated Norn. &quot;If this monster were something we could handle on our own, then the Magicians&#x27; Guild and the teachers surely wouldn&#x27;t be waiting on some divine magician.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;You&#x27;ve got that right,&quot; agreed Cliff. Divine magic might not have been the only method, but it was far and away the most effective. A seasoned adventurer wouldn&#x27;t even try to fight a Wraith without a divine magician or a lot of preparation. They were that dangerous. And this was a high-level Wraith, to boot; underestimating it could easily get them all wiped out.</p>
<p>It was there that Norn deflated.</p>
<p>&quot;But I can&#x27;t just stand by and watch more students be harmed…&quot;</p>
<p>Norn couldn&#x27;t totally oppose taking action while students were getting hurt. And caution aside, many of the students making up the student council <em>were</em> the cream of the crop. They were proficient enough to make even Norn consider that they might have a shot. At the same time, she couldn&#x27;t deny that she had a long way to go compared to people like her brother, which made her waffle on making a decision.</p>
<p>&quot;What should we do?&quot; wondered Norn as she furrowed her brow.</p>
<p>&quot;Come now, you could just… No, wait, you have a point.&quot;</p>
<p>himself. He started to realize what Norn was feeling.</p>
<p>Absolutely, Rudeus could solve this problem in a snap if Norn told him about it. He was no master of divine magic, but his skills at offensive magic were Imperial-tier. If anything, Cliff speculated he was on the cusp of Divinetier. Taking out a Wraith or two would&#x27;ve been nothing for him. But it just wouldn&#x27;t have been right. To Norn, it was out of the question. She couldn&#x27;t explain in words why that was, but given Cliff&#x27;s own dilemma—which he needed to solve himself—he understood.</p>
<p>&quot;All right, let&#x27;s try this,&quot; said Cliff. &quot;If you&#x27;re all right with it…&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;…?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I&#x27;ll give you my help.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Huh?&quot; Norn said in surprise. At Cliff&#x27;s suggestion, she went from inattentive trance to suddenly present. &quot;That&#x27;s right, you can use Advanced-tier divine magic…&quot;</p>
<p>Cliff had reached Advanced-tier divine magic. Divine magic at Intermediate-tier or above couldn&#x27;t be taught without permission from the Millis Church, so it wasn&#x27;t taught at the University of Magic. They didn&#x27;t even have staff who could teach it.</p>
<p>But Cliff was the pope&#x27;s grandson. Millis made an exception for him and gave permission for him to learn divine magic. As such, the University of Magic invited a special instructor to give him Advanced-tier lessons. Cliff was just about to graduate, so that instructor had departed. It was on him.</p>
<p>&quot;President, this is a job for the student council! Sir Cliff might be a part of the Circle of Six, but we still shouldn&#x27;t involve regular students!&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;That&#x27;s right! We&#x27;re the ones who should do this! If not, people are gonna say the student council is too incompetent to do anything themselves! They&#x27;ll say that our president is powerless!&quot;</p>
<p>The two students who had stood in Cliff&#x27;s way earlier objected loudly to the idea. But Norn&#x27;s spine straightened. She glared them down.</p>
<p>&quot;Stopping the attacks matters more than our pride!&quot; Norn sternly rebuked. The two students shrunk back. &quot;And besides, what if something happened to you guys? Any of you could be next.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;President…&quot;</p>
<p>Norn turned back to Cliff and looked him in the eyes. Her eyes were steely —nothing like the eyes she had when she first visited Cliff or when Rudeus left for the Begaritt Continent. Those had been the eyes of a lost lamb, eyes that trembled in fear and uncertainty. Looking at Cliff now were eyes that had gained determination with each passing year.</p>
<p>She&#x27;d visited the church where Cliff worked whenever she needed to talk; all those confessions and complaints must have made a difference.</p>
<p>&quot;Cliff, are you up for this?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Yeah.&quot;</p>
<p>Cliff had heard Rudeus say with glee that &quot;Norn had really grown up&quot; every now and then, but Cliff hadn&#x27;t seen it given that he&#x27;d only ever heard her complain and do confessions. But now, he felt like he was seeing a glimpse of that girl Rudeus was talking about. It also delighted Cliff to hear Norn ask him for help instead of her brother.</p>
<p>&quot;All right, council,&quot; she said, &quot;we&#x27;ll be infiltrating the underground storehouse! But if it ever turns into more you can handle, retreat immediately! Are we clear?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Y-yes!&quot;</p>
<p>And so, Cliff and the student council ventured down into the underground storehouse.</p>
<p>***</p>
<p>The underground storehouse lay before them.</p>
<p>The University of Magic was a distinguished institution with over two centuries of history since its founding. I couldn&#x27;t put number on its age, but I&#x27;m certain that Cliff or anyone in the student council could produce it if you asked them. Anyway, the University of Magic&#x27;s building had gone through plenty of extensions and reconstructions since its founding, building it into the mammoth of a school we now knew. The elegance of the building&#x27;s layout speaks to the characters of the capable administrators and architects who first laid these buildings&#x27; foundations. But no matter how much effort first went into the tidy facades of the buildings, waves of repeated renovations combined with the battering of time left some buildings that a generous eye might glide over while</p>
<p>admiring the campus&#x27;s beauty. One of the buildings was this very storehouse.</p>
<p>There were a number of these storehouses arranged on the periphery of the building, and they were all stuffed with the University of Magic&#x27;s history. Magic wands from two hundred years ago, scrolls from one hundred and fifty years ago, a principal&#x27;s century-old family toupée—anything that might have any use at all was tossed in here when no immediate use was obvious.</p>
<p>In short, it was a dump.</p>
<p>Once Norn took office as student council president, she decided that it was time to take out the trash. If the junk were cleaned out from the storehouses, the school would have more space. So, she suggested a plan to renovate them into student locker rooms. Garbage cleanup in an unused room; it was the kind of practical albeit inessential little project that suited Norn.</p>
<p>But recently, the student population had grown too large. It became a pressing reality that the school was running out of personal lockers to offer students.</p>
<p>There were teachers who weren&#x27;t on board. They said that everything in those storehouses was an artifact of history, some of them valuable. You couldn&#x27;t toss everything indiscriminately. But Norn shut those complaints down by saying, &quot;If they really are valuable, then that&#x27;s all the more reason why they shouldn&#x27;t be abandoned in the corner of a storehouse.&quot;</p>
<p>In the end, the student council appropriated the necessary funds, hired assistants within the school, and began work on clearing out the storehouse. This project was received relatively positively, and many students eagerly joined so they could earn a little cash.</p>
<p>But as the work continued, a few of those student workers met the Wraith.</p>
<p>&quot;That was how this all began, so we feel some responsibility as the student council,&quot; Norn explained to Cliff as she held a lamp in one hand.</p>
<p>&quot;Well, as far as I&#x27;ve heard, the student council shouldn&#x27;t have any need to feel like they&#x27;re at fault.&quot;</p>
<p>In retrospect, victims popped up once in a while before cleaning had ever started. Even with the barrier reapplied, the attacks kept increasing in frequency. This was proof that the Wraith in the storehouse was growing more powerful. Even if the student council hadn&#x27;t taken on this project, the Wraith would have broken free from that barrier sooner or later. If anything, the student council helped everyone discover the Wraith even quicker, so there was a silver lining</p>
<p>here.</p>
<p>&quot;Ooooh…&quot;</p>
<p>Groaning at Cliff&#x27;s words was a girl, the same pigtailed one who&#x27;d clung to Norn earlier. Both her fists gripped her fifty-centimeter wand as she glared at the pitch-black stairwell leading to the underground storehouse. Her teeth were clenched and her body shook. She was the one who&#x27;d found the sealed door in the dark. The one to peel away the seal was, also, her.</p>
<p>The first time she opened that door, a Skeleton had leapt out. Its surprise attack caught and injured one of the other students who&#x27;d followed her down. The cleanup job turned into a clash. They barely managed to destroy the first Skeleton, but immediately it resurrected. The rest of the student council rushed to the sound of the commotion. They&#x27;d managed to hold the door back with Beginner-tier barrier magic long enough for a teacher with Saint-tier barrier magic skills to arrive, but that friend of the girl who&#x27;d broken the seal was still badly hurt. If they had been a little less lucky, the collateral damage could have been far worse.</p>
<p>She might not have known that a Wraith was behind that seal, but she couldn&#x27;t deny that she removed it partly on a whim. That would typically be grounds for expulsion. However, Norn had covered for her. She tied the incident to the recent ghost stories and told the lie that they had bumped into the storehouse&#x27;s door and accidentally disturbed the seal while searching for ghosts.</p>
<p>The fact that the Skeleton continued to resurrect and attack until divine magic pulverized it to dust proved that there was a Wraith controlling it. There really <em>was</em> a Wraith there, and it really <em>was</em> attacking students, so Norn hadn&#x27;t fabricated everything. The girl who&#x27;d opened the door must have been sick with guilt regardless.</p>
<p>&quot;It sure is creepy,&quot; said Cliff as he followed her lead and peered into the darkness. The sealed door was somewhere in there. The Skeleton scare had put a halt to the storehouse cleaning project; the area was declared off-limits by the student council&#x27;s authority.</p>
<p>Cliff recalled the last time he&#x27;d been in this position. It was when he joined Rudeus to search the building that would eventually become his manor. Back then, Cliff was shivering just like the girl who stood beside him now.</p>
<p>&quot;Hey, what was your name, again?&quot; asked Cliff.</p>
<p>&quot;Huh?! M-me?!&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Yes.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;It&#x27;s Sheila, okay?&quot;</p>
<p>Sheila glared at Cliff as though to say, <em>So? What about it?</em> It reminded Cliff so much of his old self that he couldn&#x27;t help but laugh.</p>
<p>&quot;Sheila, have you ever done things like… That is, have you ever adventured into a forest or a dungeon before?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Uh, no, I haven&#x27;t! But I&#x27;m sure a Demonic Circle of Six member like <em>you</em> has <em>soooo</em> much experience! So? Who cares?!&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Oh no, I have almost none,&quot; Cliff said. Sheila eyed him with suspicion. He continued, &quot;It&#x27;s just that there&#x27;s something I once heard from someone who <em>did</em> have that experience. He said that when beginners try to take on too much, they end up unable to handle any of it. Focus on doing one thing, and doing it right.&quot;</p>
<p>Was that from the time when he tagged along with Stepped Leader on an adventure? No, it had to be from when he searched that manor with Rudeus a few days later. Cliff recalled that Rudeus gave him a single order: &quot;If we run into an enemy, use basic-level divine magic to attack them.&quot; Cliff kept that order in his head, and when the doll did attack, he was able to fend it off with divine magic. Right. Beginners can&#x27;t handle too much.</p>
<p>&quot;Is there anyone in here who&#x27;s used to fighting monsters or has worked as an adventurer?&quot; Cliff asked the group.</p>
<p>Of the seven student council members, two hands went up in response. One belonged to the beastman, and the other belonged to a human. Many beastfolk grew up in forests, where they&#x27;d fight their share of monsters. The human probably had a history as an adventurer.</p>
<p>&quot;All right, I&#x27;ll have you two give the orders. For everyone else, let&#x27;s decide on your roles beforehand.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Hey, Cliff,&quot; came a gruff voice.</p>
<p>&quot;What is it?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I wasn&#x27;t gonna push you too hard on it since the President said she owed you a lot, but you ain&#x27;t our boss,&quot; the beastman from before said.</p>
<p>Cliff stopped for a few seconds, but he soon realized that anything he said wasn&#x27;t going to get through to this guy.</p>
<p>&quot;Huhhh? It doesn&#x27;t matter who&#x27;s in charge, does it? It&#x27;s not like I know much about fighting monsters, anyway…&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;But you&#x27;re the president!&quot;</p>
