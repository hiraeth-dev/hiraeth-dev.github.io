<p><strong>&quot;D</strong><em>EAR RUDEUS GREYRAT,</em></p>
<p><em>I hope this letter finds you recuperated, and your mana regenerated.</em></p>
<p><em>I want to discuss our next steps. I will be waiting for you in your cabin on the outskirts of Sharia.</em></p>
<p><em>Due to certain circumstances, it would be preferable for you to come alone.</em></p>
<p><em>—Orsted&quot;</em></p>
<p>After reading this brief letter, I asked Aisha to make me breakfast right away.</p>
<p>I ate a good, solid meal, then returned to my room to get dressed. I tried to pick my very nicest clothes, double-checking with Aisha several times to make sure I looked okay.</p>
<p>And then, with Aqua Heartia in one hand and my diary from the future in the other, I walked out of the house.</p>
<p>Zenith happened to be out in the yard playing with our pet Treant Byt, so I called out, &quot;I&#x27;ll be back soon, Mother,&quot; as I left. She vaguely waved her hand at me in response; it almost looked like a <em>see you later</em> gesture. At her side, Byt wiggled his branches as well.</p>
<p>I hadn&#x27;t said a word to Sylphie or the others. I knew they&#x27;d want to come along. But the letter told me to come alone, so that was exactly what I was going to do. It&#x27;s not like I was going off to battle this time, anyway.</p>
<p>I couldn&#x27;t say that I completely trusted Orsted. Not yet, anyway. But his letter had showed some concern for my well-being, and its tone wasn&#x27;t unfriendly. Also, Nanahoshi seemed to think he was a</p>
<p>decent guy, since she&#x27;d opposed my plan to fight him on an emotional level. At the very least, he seemed more trustworthy than the Man-God. That was definitely what I <em>wanted</em> to believe.</p>
<p>&quot;I&#x27;m still kind of nervous, though,&quot; I murmured to myself as I made my way down a quiet street in Sharia. Every time I passed a puddle of water in the road, I couldn&#x27;t help but stop to examine my reflection and make sure I looked all right. I&#x27;d decided to work for Orsted—in other words, he was my new boss. And when the boss called you in for a meeting, you wanted to look your best.</p>
<p>&quot;Wonder if I should have put on some cologne or something…&quot;</p>
<p>I had taken a bath this morning, but after the night I&#x27;d spent with Eris, it was very possible there were some unfortunate smells lingering on my body. What exactly was the boss going to think if I came into his office stinking of sex? I couldn&#x27;t imagine he would fire me on the spot, but it might leave a bad impression. That was the last thing I wanted right now.</p>
<p>Orsted… was the only person who stood a chance to find and defeat the Man-God. And supposedly, he <em>was</em> going to make it happen someday, with the help of my descendants. Sucks to be the Man-God. But he was the one who&#x27;d betrayed my confidence first, so whatever. I wasn&#x27;t about to sympathize with a guy who&#x27;d kill Roxy and Sylphie to protect himself.</p>
<p>I was Orsted&#x27;s pet dog now. I&#x27;d wag my tail for him and bare my fangs at his enemies. That was the only way I could protect my family.</p>
<p>&quot;All right…&quot;</p>
<p>Having reconfirmed my resolve, I headed for the outskirts of the city with quicker, more confident steps—while carefully avoiding the muddy water splashed by passing carriages.</p>
<p>I arrived at my cabin outside the city walls to find it <em>different</em> somehow. It&#x27;s hard to explain in words, but there was… something strange about the air around it. If this were a manga, the cabin would definitely have a menacing aura scribbled around it. It was obvious from a glance that Orsted was already waiting for me inside.</p>
<p>I took a few deep breaths, then knocked sharply at the door.</p>
<p>&quot;It&#x27;s Rudeus Greyrat, sir! I&#x27;m here as requested!&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Ah. That didn&#x27;t take long.&quot;</p>
<p>Even though I&#x27;d <em>known</em> he was in there, I trembled a little at the sound of his voice. There was definitely a part of me that was still terrified of him. &quot;Do I have your leave to enter?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Why are you asking me for permission? Aren&#x27;t you the owner of this cabin?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Yes, sir! Coming in, sir!&quot;</p>
<p>When I opened the door and stepped into the cabin, I found Orsted seated in one of the chairs within, glaring sharply at me.</p>
<p>Well… maybe he wasn&#x27;t <em>glaring.</em> His face was just scary-looking by default.</p>
<p>I pulled the door shut behind me and walked over as crisply as I could. I stopped directly beside the chair across from Orsted, and stood at attention. He glared up at me with a slightly suspicious expression on his face.</p>
<p>&quot;Hmm. I half expected you to come here with all your friends in tow… but I see there&#x27;s only two of you.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Yes, sir! I came alo— Wait, two of us?&quot;</p>
<p>I was a little thrown off by that one. Unless Orsted&#x27;s eyes were getting so bad that he was seeing double, the comment didn&#x27;t seem to make much sense.</p>
<p>&quot;Eris Greyrat!&quot; Orsted shouted. &quot;You may enter as well!&quot;</p>
<p>An instant later, the door to the cabin swung loudly open. Eris was standing just outside. Her sword was already dangling from her hand, and there was murder in her eyes.</p>
<p>&quot;Orsted!&quot; she cried, swinging her blade up to point at him. &quot;If you lay a finger on Rudeus, I&#x27;ll cut you down on the spot!&quot;</p>
<p>The force and fury of her voice would have made a lesser man pee his pants, but Orsted was totally unfazed. &quot;I have no intention of harming him.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Well, I don&#x27;t trust you!&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I suppose you wouldn&#x27;t.&quot;</p>
<p>Without another word, Eris stalked over to a corner of the cabin and folded her arms menacingly.</p>
<p>Still a little stunned by this dramatic entrance, I looked from Orsted to Eris and back again. Should I be making my excuses right now? Explaining that I hadn&#x27;t brought her with me? Insisting that I didn&#x27;t view him as an enemy? The problem was, I couldn&#x27;t make excuses for the sword she was holding. What was I supposed to <em>do</em> here?</p>
<p>As I hesitated, Orsted spoke up. &quot;What&#x27;s the matter, Rudeus Greyrat? Have a seat. We need to talk.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Er, right. Pardon me.&quot; I sat down, of course. But my mind was still on Eris and her unsheathed weapon. &quot;Uhm, about Eris…&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Your reaction made it clear enough. She followed you here without your knowledge, I imagine.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Well, yeah. I guess she did… Would you, uh, mind if I had a word with her before we got started?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Feel free.&quot;</p>
<p>At least it seemed like he wasn&#x27;t too upset. Turning around in my chair, I quickly beckoned Eris over with my hand.</p>
<p>&quot;Eris…<em>why</em> did you follow me?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I saw you all dressed up. I just wanted to know where you were going.&quot;</p>
<p><em>All dressed up?</em> Well, hmm. I <em>had</em> picked out my best clothes and fidgeted with my hair for a while. Maybe she&#x27;d thought I was going out on a secret date or something…</p>
<p>&quot;You understand that I&#x27;m working for Orsted now, right?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;…Yeah. But it&#x27;s <em>Orsted</em>, Rudeus. He&#x27;s obviously planning something, right? I&#x27;m worried he might be tricking you somehow.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;That&#x27;s possible, but it&#x27;s too early to say for sure. For now, do you think you can keep calm and just let us talk?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;…&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;We can fight him together if I figure out he&#x27;s tricking me. I&#x27;m counting on you, Eris.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Oh. Yeah! Got it!&quot;</p>
<p>Apparently satisfied by this, Eris sheathed her sword and took a seat next to me. It was a good thing her mind worked in such simple ways.</p>
<p>&quot;I&#x27;m sorry about that.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;It&#x27;s all right.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;It seems that Eris has a very hard time trusting you, Sir Orsted… But I suppose that&#x27;s just the curse at work.&quot;</p>
<p>Orsted&#x27;s eyes seemed to flash at that. &quot;Who told you about my curse in the first place?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;The Man-God. He claimed you suffer from a number of them, actually.&quot;</p>
<p>I answered honestly and without hesitation. I&#x27;d prepared myself to tell Orsted everything about my conversations with his enemy.</p>
<p>Orsted raised a hand to his chin and turned his gaze upward for a moment. The ceiling of my cabin wasn&#x27;t much to look at, so it seemed like he was just thinking things over.</p>
<p>&quot;First things first. Allow me to fulfill my promise to you.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Huh?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Why do you look so surprised? I&#x27;m not like your former master. I keep my word.&quot;</p>
<p><em>That&#x27;s nice to hear, but I&#x27;m just wondering what he&#x27;s talking about… did he promise me something at some point?</em></p>
<p>&quot;I&#x27;m speaking of my method to protect your family from the Man-God.&quot;</p>
<p><em>Oh. Ohhh! Of course. How did I forget about that?</em></p>
<p>I guess I hadn&#x27;t thought of that whole agreement as a <em>promise</em>, exactly. It felt more like a contract. You know, the kind you use to sell your soul to the devil. But then again, the provisions of a contract are basically just a bunch of promises, right? Sure.</p>
<p>&quot;Are you sure? I haven&#x27;t actually done anything on your behalf yet.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Yes, but I imagine you wouldn&#x27;t be able to focus on any task if you&#x27;re constantly worrying about your family&#x27;s safety.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Well, yes. That&#x27;s true enough.&quot;</p>
<p>Huh. He was actually being kind of considerate here, wasn&#x27;t he? I hadn&#x27;t expected such friendly treatment right off the bat, to be honest. I was expecting him to order me around sternly. The man had a scary face, but he seemed like a surprisingly good boss. I couldn&#x27;t understand why Eris was still glaring at him so fiercely.</p>
<p>&quot;In any case, what specific method did you have in mind to protect them?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;It&#x27;s nothing too complex. You need only summon a guardian beast with a strong destiny and order to it to keep them safe.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I see. Unfortunately, I don&#x27;t know how to use Summoning magic yet.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Very well. I&#x27;ll draw the magic circle. You can just channel the mana through it.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Oh. That works, then. Sorry for the bother.&quot;</p>
<p>Hmm. A guardian beast with a strong destiny, huh? In other words, we&#x27;d have a guard dog protected by the laws of causality…</p>
<p>&quot;Will that really be enough to keep them safe, though?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;The Man-God has no influence over anything but humans. In addition, he can&#x27;t manipulate many individuals at any given time; as long as we are taking action, he should have his hands full trying to stop us. Given his personality, this should be more than enough to protect your loved ones.&quot;</p>
<p>Oh good, now we were psychoanalyzing the guy.</p>
<p>That bit about him not being able to influence <em>many</em> people at the same time was interesting, though. It implied he could control at least a few simultaneously. Had he been meddling in other people&#x27;s lives while he was messing with me?</p>
<p>&quot;However, you mustn&#x27;t let down your guard. The Man-God is devious and unpredictable. Don&#x27;t leave everything to the guardian beast—make sure you&#x27;re there for them as well.&quot;</p>
<p>To be honest, those words sounded kind of <em>wrong</em> coming out of Orsted&#x27;s mouth. He just didn&#x27;t look like the kind of guy who&#x27;d remind you to spend time with your family. Can&#x27;t judge a book by its cover and all, but seriously…</p>
<p>Anyway, since he was willing to prepare this summoning for me, I&#x27;d gladly take him up on it.</p>
<p>Now it was time to get down to business. There were all sorts of things I wanted to ask Orsted, of course, but I needed to keep up my</p>
<p>end of this bargain as well. It couldn&#x27;t hurt to proactively ask what my orders were.</p>
<p>&quot;All right then. What is it you&#x27;d like me to do for you from now on?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;…Don&#x27;t you have any other questions for me?&quot;</p>
<p>Hm. Hadn&#x27;t expected him to push back like that… &quot;Sure I do. Lots of them.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Why aren&#x27;t you asking them, in that case?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Well, I didn&#x27;t want to pester you too much, I guess.&quot;</p>
<p>Orsted let out a sigh and shook his head. &quot;You are my ally now. In other words—&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I&#x27;m your <em>subordinate</em>, Sir Orsted. I think we should keep the chain of command nice and clear.&quot;</p>
<p>The man had beaten the living crap out of me. And now he&#x27;d thought up ways to protect my family. Even I wasn&#x27;t shameless enough to pretend that we were equals in this relationship.</p>
<p>&quot;Very well, if that&#x27;s what you prefer… But regardless, the two of us will be working <em>together</em> to defeat the Man-God. It&#x27;s important that you learn everything you need to know.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Okay, but what if I turned out to be the Man-God&#x27;s spy or something? For all you know, I could start feeding him information every night.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I trust you, Rudeus Greyrat,&quot; Orsted said, staring me firmly in the eyes. &quot;You risked your life for the sake of your family, and that has earned you my respect.&quot;</p>
<p><em>Wow, you&#x27;re gonna make me blush!</em></p>
<p>I mean… I guess I had been pretty desperate back then, sure. And if that was good enough to make him trust me, I certainly wasn&#x27;t complaining. Might as well take him up on his offer.</p>
<p>What <em>did</em> I want to ask him?</p>
<p>A few things came to mind immediately. Why was he so obsessed with the Man-God? What was that Laplace Aspect thing he&#x27;d mentioned? Did he know anything about the Displacement Incident? And could he explain that whole &quot;destiny&quot; deal a bit more clearly?</p>
<p>Those were the big questions for the moment.</p>
<p>&quot;All right then. I&#x27;ll just go down the list one by one, I guess.&quot;</p>
<p>It seemed best to start off with his feud against the Man-God… or the nature of their relationship, really. Hmm. Then again, I probably needed to hear more about Orsted himself before I could get to that.</p>
<p>&quot;Could you tell me more about yourself, Sir Orsted?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;You want to hear my story?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Yeah. If you don&#x27;t mind.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;What has the Man-God told you about me? It seems he mentioned my curses, at least.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Uhhhm…&quot;</p>
<p>It had been five years since that particular encounter, so it was a challenge to recall exactly what he&#x27;d said. I concentrated intently, trying to pull the words out of my memory.</p>
<p>&quot;He said you have four different curses, specifically.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;…Go on.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;The first curse makes everything living in this world hate or fear you. The second keeps the Man-God from seeing you. The third prevents you from going all-out. And he didn&#x27;t know what the fourth one is.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I see,&quot; Orsted said with a small nod. &quot;Let us begin with the first curse, then. From the day I was born, I have indeed been loathed by everything living in this world.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;…I don&#x27;t particularly hate you, though.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Some such cases do exist. Such as yourself and Nanahoshi.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Okay then.&quot;</p>
<p>So there were exceptions. The fact that Nanahoshi and I had come here from a different world was probably relevant here. Should I take this opportunity to reveal the truth about myself? With Eris next to me, I was a little reluctant to do so. But keeping secrets from Orsted didn&#x27;t strike me as a wise idea at this point.</p>
<p>&quot;I wasn&#x27;t trying to hide this from you or anything, but… I was originally from the same world Nanahoshi came from. Maybe that has something to do with it?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;…Is Rudeus Greyrat not your true name, then?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;It&#x27;s a very long story, but I&#x27;m not exactly like Nanahoshi. I just sort of, uhm… woke up here in the body of a baby named Rudeus Greyrat, I guess… I&#x27;m not sure how to explain it, actually.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Ah. So you were reincarnated.&quot;</p>
<p>I blinked in surprise. I hadn&#x27;t expected that word to come out of Orsted&#x27;s mouth so smoothly. Now that I thought about it, though… I felt like I&#x27;d seen a few notes in the diary about the Dragonfolk possessing some means of reincarnation. Something about how they could come back in a new body a couple decades after they died. Maybe it was an ordinary enough concept for them.</p>
<p>&quot;Most likely, the reason you do not fear me is connected to your status as one reborn.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Are there others who don&#x27;t fear you?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Apart from a handful of exceptions, only those descended from the ancient Dragonfolk.&quot;</p>
<p>So Perugius, for one… although he seemed terrified of Orsted, actually. Maybe that didn&#x27;t have anything to do with the curse. Sometimes you have perfectly good reasons to be scared of someone.</p>
<p>&quot;As for the second curse, which keeps me from the Man-God&#x27;s sight… this is no curse at all, in truth.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;What is it, then?&quot;</p>
<p>Orsted paused for a moment to think, then looked me in the eyes once again. &quot;A secret art of sorts, created by the first Dragon God as a tool to use against the Man-God. It enables me to see the flow of destiny, and ensures that certain… laws of this world do not apply to me.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Hmm…&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;The Man-God possesses great knowledge of the future, and his eyes see far. But they are blind to those outside the world&#x27;s jurisdiction.&quot;</p>
<p>Interesting. I had no idea what it meant to be &quot;outside the world&#x27;s jurisdiction,&quot; but being totally invisible to the Man-God sounded very enticing indeed.</p>
<p>&quot;Could you explain that part about seeing the flow of destiny?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Hm. Let&#x27;s see…&quot;</p>
<p>Falling silent, Orsted assumed his contemplative pose again. I had to hope that didn&#x27;t mean he was thinking up a lie on the spot.</p>
<p>&quot;When I look at someone, I can see the broad outlines of their life&#x27;s story.&quot;</p>
<p><em>Well, that&#x27;s kind of vague…</em> &quot;Does that mean you have the power of foresight as well?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;No… I don&#x27;t see the future. I see <em>history</em> as it is ordained by fate.&quot;</p>
<p>Hmm? Now he sounded like a philosopher or something. I didn&#x27;t quite get the difference between this power and actual foresight. For the moment, it seemed easiest to think of it as lower-tier version of the Man-God&#x27;s ability.</p>
<p>&quot;Could you use this secret art on me as well?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;That would be unwise.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;…Why do you say that?&quot;</p>
<p>I wasn&#x27;t sure about seeing people&#x27;s destinies, but it would be <em>very</em> nice to hide myself from the Man-God. I wanted to know the actual reason behind his refusal.</p>
<p>&quot;The art has the side effect of dramatically slowing your mana regeneration rate.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;How dramatically?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;You regenerated your supply of mana completely in ten days, yes? Under the influence of the art, that would take roughly a thousand times longer.&quot;</p>
<p>A thousand times longer? That would be, what… thirty <em>years</em> or so?</p>
<p>&quot;As a result of this, I cannot wield magic freely. And <em>that</em> is the reason I so rarely fight with all my strength.&quot;</p>
<p>Aha. Basically, his mana regenerated so slowly that he couldn&#x27;t use it very often. I didn&#x27;t know how large his mana supply was, but assuming it took years for it to refill, he&#x27;d have to be <em>very</em> careful about conserving power.</p>
<p>&quot;While I cannot use the secret art itself on you, the bracelet I gave you provides a similar effect.&quot;</p>
<p>I glanced down at the bracelet on my left wrist. Apparently it <em>was</em> a Man-God jamming device of sorts. &quot;So this doesn&#x27;t have any side effects? Maybe if we mass-produced it…&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I would have done so already, if it were possible. And removed my curse as well.&quot;</p>
<p><em>Right. Kind of a dumb question.</em></p>
<p>&quot;I used a considerable amount of mana in my battle against you,&quot; Orsted continued. &quot;I will be unable to fight with all my strength for a while.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Huh? Wait, really? But you took me out instantly.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I was forced to resist direct hits from your magic many times, and ultimately to draw the Godblade,&quot; said Orsted, his tone of voice distinctly bitter. &quot;It cost me a great deal.&quot;</p>
<p>Hm. From my perspective, he&#x27;d beat me into the dirt without breaking a sweat… but apparently I&#x27;d put up a better fight than I realized. <em>A solid effort, if I do say so myself. Hoh hoh hoh.</em></p>
<p>&quot;In any case, my supply of mana is quite low at the moment. Accordingly, I will need you to take action in my stead.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;…Right. I&#x27;ll do what I can.&quot;</p>
<p>So I was working off the cost of the damage I&#x27;d done, basically. That seemed fair enough.</p>
<p>&quot;On another note, Sir Orsted… can I ask <em>why</em> you&#x27;re fighting against the Man-God?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Ah… yes, there is that…&quot;</p>
<p>Orsted glanced sideways, gazing at nothing, his tone somewhat reluctant. I had noticed a lot of these thoughtful pauses in this conversation. Was the man lying to me after all? I didn&#x27;t want to think so, especially after hearing that he trusted me… but then again, it would be odd if he trusted me <em>completely</em> at this point. There was a good chance he was feeding me some simple falsehoods for now, and reserving his final judgment until I&#x27;d proven myself reliable.</p>
<p>&quot;The Man-God… caused my father&#x27;s death.&quot;</p>
<p>Revenge, huh? Definitely a classic motive. It was the same thing that had motivated my future self to try and kill the Man-God. It would be easy for me to scoff at such desires at the moment, since no one I loved had actually been taken from me yet. But that diary made it very clear that I had wound up <em>living</em> for vengeance in that timeline.</p>
<p>&quot;In addition, his destruction was the dearest wish of the ancient Dragonfolk. All of the Dragon Gods have existed solely to pursue that goal.&quot;</p>
<p>Okay, so there was some cultural sense of duty involved as well… Wait, <em>all</em> of the Dragon Gods?</p>
<p>&quot;Uhm, how many Dragon Gods have there been?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;I am the hundredth, it seems. And the ninety-nine who came before me all devoted their lives to bringing down the Man-God.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Wow. Okay.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;However, only a Dragon God of great strength and pure blood truly stands a chance to succeed in this task.&quot; Orsted&#x27;s sharp, shining eyes fixed themselves on mine; after a moment, he continued in a calm and steady voice. &quot;It was for that reason that my father, first of all the Dragon Gods, <em>reincarnated</em> me in the future.&quot;</p>
<p><strong>Chapter 13: Explanations</strong></p>
