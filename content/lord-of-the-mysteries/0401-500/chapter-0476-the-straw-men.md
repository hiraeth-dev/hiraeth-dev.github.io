<p>Light suddenly faded from where Ince Zangwill disappeared, staining the area with the richest and deepest darkness.</p>
<p>In the darkness, the sounds of melodic chanting could be heard. It was quiet and tranquil, hypnotic even. Even the countless pale arms flailing upwards underneath the pitch-black water seemed to slow down, no longer as frenzied as before, as if they had won redemption for their souls.</p>
<p>In such a &quot;dark night,&quot; a figure walked out. It was none other than Ince Zangwill who had just been dragged into the spirit world.</p>
<p>Compared to before, he had lost the bonnet on his head. The clothes on his left shoulder were tattered, and a piece of flesh had been torn off. Faint yellow pus bubbled out one after another.</p>
<p>His eyes were no longer indifferent, but filled with pain, as if he was suffering some unimaginable torment.</p>

<p>In a street in the depths of East Borough.</p>
<p>Old Kohler hurried back to his rented apartment, carrying some ham in a paper bag.</p>
<p>He looked around warily, afraid that those ravenous wolves around him would pounce on him and steal his New Year&#x27;s gift.</p>
<p>He had seen wolves back when he was still in the countryside, but to his surprise, he was able to experience that familiar feeling in Backlund.</p>
<p>It&#x27;s still too expensive. I can only split the cost with someone else and cut it into pieces... This is enough for the new year holiday. I can eat two slices of ham for every meal. Three slices, no—at least ﬁve slices of ham. I can cut some of them off and stew them together with the potatoes. I don&#x27;t even need to add salt... With this in mind, Old Kohler looked at the ham in his arms, seeing the white spots mixed in with the red meat, his throat couldn&#x27;t help but twitch and he swallowed a mouthful of saliva.</p>
<p>As he walked, he felt the fog around him grow thicker. The cathedral&#x27;s clock tower that was still relatively clear in the distance was gradually swallowed by a mixture of iron-black and pale yellow colors. Even the pedestrians around him were reduced to blurry shadows once they were ten steps away. Old Kohler suddenly felt like he has been forgotten by the world as he raised his palm to cover his nose.</p>
<p>&quot;Why does the fog smell so bad today?&quot; he muttered, and he quickened his pace.</p>
<p>One step, two steps, three steps. Old Kohler felt his face heat up and his forehead start to burn.</p>
<p>His chest tightened, his throat felt uncomfortable, and soon he was experiencing difficulty breathing.</p>
<p>Have I fallen ill? Damn it, I still want to have a good new year&#x27;s, and now I can only send my savings to the clinic or hospital... No, perhaps I&#x27;ll be ﬁne with some sleep. I&#x27;ll be ﬁne sleeping with a blanket over me! Old Kohler silently muttered to himself as his head grew hotter and hotter and his senses became more and more muddled.</p>
<p>Gasp. Gasp. Gasp. He heard his own labored breathing, and his hands went limp as the bag of ham fell heavily to the ground.</p>
<p>Old Kohler instinctively bent down to pick it up, but he ended up falling to the ground.</p>
<p>He held the bag of ham and tried to keep it in his arms.</p>
<p>At this moment, he thought there was thick phlegm rushing up his throat, blocking it, so he fought back, making panting sounds.</p>
<p>Thud! Old Kohler began to see, through his blurry vision, that a few steps away, someone else had also fallen and was gasping for breath. He was about the same age as him, in his fifties, with white sideburns.</p>
<p>Suddenly, he understood that he was about to die.</p>
<p>It reminded him of his own wife and children, who, like him, suddenly got infected with the plague and died shortly after.</p>
<p>It reminded him of the time when he had been hospitalized. The patients in the same room were still chatting and laughing that night, but they would be sent to the morgue early the next morning.</p>
<p>It reminded him of the friends he had known as a tramp, many of which had disappeared over the course of the winter until they were found stiff under a bridge or some spot which was sheltered from the wind. A small number died from suddenly having food.</p>
<p>This reminded him of the days when he was still a decent worker, when the neighbors in the neighborhood would suddenly die like this. Some of them died from headaches, some of them died from accidentally falling into molten steel, some of them died from all sorts of painful, bloating ailments. Some of them even collapsed silently in the factories, one batch after another.</p>
<p>This reminded him of what he had heard from a drunk in the bar when he was scrounging for information.</p>
<p>&quot;People like us are like straw on the ground, falling when the wind blows. It&#x27;s even possible to fall on our own even without the wind.&quot; The wind is coming... Old Kohler suddenly had such a thought.</p>
<p>He clutched the bag of ham tightly as he groped the pocket of his old jacket for the crumpled cigarette he had always been unwilling to smoke.</p>
<p>What he couldn&#x27;t understand was why his healthy body would suddenly become ill. It wasn&#x27;t like he had never experienced such a dense fog before.</p>
<p>What he couldn&#x27;t understand was the reason for him suddenly collapsing just as his life was back on track, moving in a direction that was sufficiently nice for him. Furthermore, he had been paid in advance by Detective Moriarty to buy his long-awaited ham for the new year, and he was looking forward to tasting it.</p>
<p>Old Kohler fished out that crumpled cigarette, but he no longer had the strength to lift his arms again as they slammed heavily to the ground.</p>
<p>He used the last of his strength to shout out the words that had been building up inside of him, but he could only let the frail words linger at his mouth without producing them.</p>
<p>He heard his last words.</p>
<p>He heard himself ask, &quot;Why?&quot;</p>

<p>She looked at the sky outside, somewhat unsure of the time due to the thick fog which had appeared without her realizing it.</p>
<p>&quot;Anyway, it&#x27;s still early, and we&#x27;ve already done the laundry...&quot; Liv&#x27;s expression grew heavy.</p>
<p>It wasn&#x27;t a good thing to finish work too early. It didn&#x27;t mean that they could rest for it meant not having enough work and inadequate income.</p>
<p>Liv took a deep breath, turned around, and looked at her eldest daughter, Freja, who was wiping her hands and casting her gaze towards the vocabulary notebook in the opposite room.</p>
<p>&quot;It&#x27;s almost New Year&#x27;s. Most of our clients have left Backlund and have gone on vacation elsewhere. We can&#x27;t go on like this. We have to find new work.&quot;</p>
