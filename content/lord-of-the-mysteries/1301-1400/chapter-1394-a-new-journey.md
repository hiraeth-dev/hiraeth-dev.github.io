<p>In a room of an abandoned castle, sunlight shone through the gaps in the thick curtains, illuminating a pitch-black coffin.</p>
<p>Suddenly, the lid of the coffin creaked and slowly moved to the side.</p>
<p>With a thud, it fell to the ground.</p>
<p>A few seconds later, Azik Eggers sat up, looking rather lost.</p>
<p>At that moment, he was wearing loose pajamas that had been popular in Loen years ago. He resembled a noble who had woken up in his manor.</p>
<p>After a while, Azik narrowed his eyes slightly. He looked around in confusion as though he didn&#x27;t know who he was.</p>
<p>He then saw the brilliant sunlight that penetrated through the cracks and saw the dust dancing in the sunlight. He saw letters scattered on the table, ground, and coffin lid around him.</p>
<p>They were like giant snowflakes that blanketed half the area.</p>
<p>Azik got out of the coffin. With a puzzled expression, he bent down to pick up a letter and began reading.</p>
<p>As he read, the confusion on his face disappeared a little, as if he had remembered many things from the past.</p>
<p>Azik immediately found a chair and sat down, allowing all the letters to fly in front of him to stack up like a mountain.</p>
<p>He opened the letters one by one, reading them one after another. There would be pauses in between as he fell deep in thought as if he was seriously recalling something.</p>
<p>The sunlight that passed through the gap in the curtains gradually dimmed. After a long time, it shone inside again.</p>
<p>At that moment, Azik finally finished reading all the letters and completed the long contemplations that resembled Cogitation.</p>
<p>&quot;He&quot; looked at the letters that had been stacked on the table and slowly let out a long sigh.</p>
<p>Following that, he took out a piece of paper, a fountain pen, and some ink that he could still use. He wrote with a warm expression:</p>
<p>&quot;...I&#x27;ve already woken up and received all your letters. They made me recall who I am and who you are. I also remember many memories of the past.</p>
<p>&quot;Your experiences, no matter how complicated and exciting, have exceeded my imagination. It also makes me understand some of the problems that previously plagued me.</p>
<p>&quot;I can feel your joy, your exhaustion, your faith in life, and the heavy responsibility that you have borne from your letters. &quot;I can roughly guess why you ultimately made that choice. If it were me, I might not even be able to make such a decision.</p>
<p>&quot;From the beginning, you&#x27;ve been a guardian. You mimicked others until you were mimicked by others.</p>
<p>&quot;Next, I will begin a journey to pursue the past and witness the changes in this world.</p>
<p>&quot;You seem to still be asleep, but it doesn&#x27;t matter. I&#x27;ll write to tell you about the interesting things that I&#x27;ve encountered, the interesting traditions, and interesting people.</p>
<p>&quot;I think I should be able to send these letters to you via sacrifice...&quot;</p>
<p>The tip of the golden pen reflected the sunlight as it rustled on the white piece of paper, continuously penning down more content.</p>
<p>...</p>
<p>Backlund, in a solarium of a terrace house.</p>
<p>Melissa walked in with a girl who was clearly less than ten years old.</p>
<p>&quot;Aunt Melissa, why here?&quot; the little girl asked, puzzled. &quot;All the stories I heard had mysterious rituals held in the basement.&quot;</p>
<p>With her hair tied up, the bespectacled Melissa smiled and said, &quot;Those are unconventional mysticism rituals.&quot;</p>
<p>She pointed at the altar that had been set up and the unlit candles and said, &quot;You may begin.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Really?&quot; The little girl tilted her head to look at the bright sunshine outside the window. &quot;Do we need to draw the curtains?&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;There&#x27;s no need. It&#x27;s pretty good this way.&quot; After Melissa answered, she smiled at the little girl while she awkwardly mimicked her usual method of holding rituals in a clumsy and unfamiliar manner.</p>
<p>During this process, she would instruct her from time to time and even personally help her to complete the pre-ritual preparations.</p>
<p>&quot;Alright, repeat after me.&quot; Melissa took a deep breath as her expression gradually turned staid.</p>
<p>&quot;Yes, yes.&quot; The little girl tried her best to appear stern.</p>
<p>Melissa looked at the candle flames on the altar for a few seconds before slowly reciting in ancient Hermes, &quot;The Fool that doesn&#x27;t belong to this era...&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Da Pool that dun pelong to diz ela...&quot; The little girl had never learned ancient Hermes before. Although she tried her best to imitate her aunt, she still didn&#x27;t know what she was saying.</p>
<p>&quot;The mysterious ruler above the gray fog...&quot; Melissa continued reciting.</p>
<p>&quot;Da Mesterwes luler apove the gway pog...&quot; the little girl recited in all seriousness.</p>
<p>&quot;The King of Yellow and Black who wields good luck...&quot; After Melissa finished reciting, the candle at the end didn&#x27;t wait for the little girl to imitate her. It immediately burgeoned to the size of a human head.</p>
<p>In the large flame, a slippery tentacle with a somewhat sinister pattern extended out in an indiscernible manner. It was extremely slow.</p>
<p>The little girl was stunned. She retreated and hid behind her aunt.</p>
<p>Melissa pursed her lips and said with a gentle smile, &quot;Don&#x27;t be afraid, go greet him.&quot;</p>
<p>The little girl timidly poked her head out from behind her aunt and saw the terrifying, slimy tentacle gently swaying in the brilliant sunlight that shone through the windows. It seemed to be attempting to swat away the dust or was waving at her.</p>
<p>&quot;Go, don&#x27;t be afraid,&quot; Melissa repeated.</p>
<p>The little girl finally mustered her courage and stood in front of the altar.</p>
<p>She recited the incantations she had just invented before revealing a sincere smile and raised her palm. The slippery tentacle whose patterns had disappeared paused for a few seconds. It seemed hesitant and somewhat out of practice.</p>
<p>Then, it raised its head and curled up slightly, lowering itself inch by inch.</p>
<p>Amidst the sunlight, it high-fived that tiny palm.</p>

<p>Mmh, LoM 2 story is going to be set in-between &quot;Klein Sleeping or even a little bit before he went to sleep&quot; and ends with &quot;He wakes up&quot;.</p>
<p>Even this time frame setting about LoM 2 was something that was decided before LoM 1 was even written. Why did I consider a second book when doing my settings? For one, I feel my world setting is too huge and detailed, there are way too many things to write about. If I were to dump everything into one book, then inevitably there will be a confusion of Main storyline and Side storyline; Unnecessarily long Side Storyline; Scattered and Boring; Structurally Bloated to an unsightly degree. Secondly, the growth curve of the Tarot Club Members cannot keep up with our MC Klein. If I gave them too many Side Quests and more stories, for the sole purpose of justifying and making their &quot;improvement&quot; logical enough, then it will crash against the Main Story&#x27;s structure. This will also cause the problem of confusion of Main/Side Storyline. If I don&#x27;t give them those extra quests and stories in LoM 1, and still let them grow, making the Tarot Club members exert critical influence and usefulness at crucial moments, then it will only make the readers feel cheap. There will be a great number of readers/friends who will feel that the Tarot Club members just rode on the Klein Train and sucked off of the Main Character&#x27;s plot armor aura. They will feel that the Tarot Club members obtained everything &quot;too easily&quot; or &quot;Because they are main side characters needed to defeat Error, etc., etc.&quot;</p>
<p>So, after balancing all my options, combining all situations from different perspectives, I made the final decision and oath to at least MUST write an LoM 2. In this way, LoM 1 could leisurely walk its Main Storyline, doesn&#x27;t have to go &quot;dungeoning&quot; and &quot;farming exp&quot; all over the world map, keeping the focus in one country, in Backlund. And Tarot Club members are only finishing up their life&#x27;s first half, completing metamorphosis and growth, and leaving their comfort zones. They will go to LoM 2 to demonstrate to us, an even grander, more amazing, and more exciting life and story.</p>
<p>For me, this is also a challenge. How to, after a few years later (remember it won&#x27;t be until AT LEAST 2022 for LoM 2), to bring every reader back into this world. How to allow new readers, who never read LoM 1, to also smoothly read LOMT 2 without any difficulties and limit. Before LoM, I don&#x27;t have experience with writing series/trilogy type books.
